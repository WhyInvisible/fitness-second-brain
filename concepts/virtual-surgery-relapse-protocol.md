# Virtual Surgery: the back-niggle relapse protocol

Per McGill Ch 3 + Ch 13, the patient-side equivalent of "have surgery without having surgery." It works because much of what makes surgery deliver pain relief is the **enforced post-surgical rest**, not the surgery itself. Replicating the rest + adding the spine-sparing rebuild often delivers the same outcome WITHOUT the scarring, nerve damage, and permanent bone removal.

## The protocol (run this if back niggle returns)

### Phase 1 — Pretend you just had surgery (Day 0 to ~Day 14, until acute pain resolves)
- **NO gym.** No lifting, no running, no stretching, no yoga.
- **Tummy lie + fist under chin, 3 minutes, several times per day.** Especially first thing in morning, mid-afternoon, before bed.
- **Walk gently** — slow, frequent, short. If 10 steps causes pain, do 6 every hour. If 50 steps are fine, walk for 5 min every couple of hours.
- **Spine hygiene 24/7** ([spine-sparing-daily-life](spine-sparing-daily-life.md)) — hip-hinge for EVERYTHING, lumbar support when sitting, no slouching, no twisting under load, no lifting in the first hour after rising.
- **Self-diagnose** ([mcgill-pain-trigger-diagnostic](mcgill-pain-trigger-diagnostic.md)) to identify the trigger that caused the relapse.
- **Eliminate the trigger** from daily life.

### Phase 2 — Build back the daily floor (Day 14 to ~Day 28, after acute pain resolves)
- **McGill Big 3 daily** ([mcgill-big-3](mcgill-big-3.md)) at the starting dose (6/4/2 pyramid, 8–10 s holds). NOT progressed yet.
- **Cat-camel before Big 3** (7–8 slow cycles, never end-range).
- **Walking** — build interval walking to 30 min × 3 per day (pre-breakfast, midday, after dinner). Brisk pace, arm swing from shoulder.
- **No yoga, no static stretching of the spine.** Hip-flexor and hamstring work can resume IF the issue is hip not back.

### Phase 3 — Hip restoration (Week 4+ if pain is fully gone)
- Walking lunge, hip airplane, clamshell, glute-only back bridge (hip-airplane.md when created).
- One bodyweight deep squat per day to preserve the pattern (NOT loaded).
- Continue Big 3, walking, spine hygiene.

### Phase 4 — Return to load (Week 6+ if rock-solid pain-free)
- Push: push-ups, cable pull at 20 lb / ~9 kg × 8 reps descending pyramid.
- Pull: suspension straps, fire-hose pulls, then graduate to pull-ups.
- Carries: suitcase, farmer, then loaded.
- ONLY THEN: light loaded hinges (trap-bar at sub-bodyweight load), with [bar-speed-rule](../concepts/bar-speed-rule.md) and [hip-hinge-mechanics](hip-hinge-mechanics.md) governing.
- "Never sacrifice exercise form to lift or move more load." (McGill)
- "Only add more load when your spine is in a neutral posture that is possible to maintain." (McGill)

### Phase 5 — Resume marathon training (Week 8+ depending on phase + severity)
- Easy running first. Strides. Build mileage gradually.
- Strength session limited to bodyweight + Big 3 + carries until 4 weeks symptom-free.
- Re-introduce loaded compounds last.

## When to escalate to a physio

If pain persists after 2 weeks of strict Phase 1, OR if there's any radiating leg pain / numbness / weakness / bowel-bladder change, escalate immediately. Bring the McGill book to the appointment.

## Why this works (and why "just rest" doesn't)

Plain "rest" without spine hygiene means continuing to slouch on the couch, brushing teeth bent over, picking up the phone from the floor with a bent spine — i.e. all the things that caused the relapse, just with less gym work. The Virtual Surgery protocol systematically removes the cumulative load AND replaces flawed movement engrams with pain-free ones. The tissue actually gets to heal.

## Practical application

For an athlete with a back history who is currently pain-free (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles), this is **dormant** — a contingency plan, not an active protocol. But:
- The athlete should know the protocol exists and how to find it (this note).
- The [MOC-injuries-management](../MOCs/MOC-injuries-management.md) decision tree should point here at "Persistent niggle 3+ days" before any other action.
- If a back niggle appears during a high-load marathon week, this protocol AND the [marathon-goal-wins-conflicts](marathon-goal-wins-conflicts.md) tension need explicit reasoning — the back wins over the marathon if the niggle is acute.
