# Compound lifts only, mostly

Squat, deadlift / RDL, press, pull. Single-leg work (split squats, step-ups) is gold for runners.

## Why

Compounds produce the most strength + economy benefit per unit time. Isolation work (bicep curls, lateral raises) has its place but is secondary for runners.

Matthews Ch 5 cites the Ball State 2000 study directly: two groups did 4-compound upper-body training for 10 weeks; group B added bicep curls + tricep extensions. NO additional arm size or strength in group B. The conclusion isn't "never train arms" — it's "the compounds are what move the needle, isolation is a small additive at best."

## Single-leg priority

Running is a series of single-leg stances. Bilateral lifts (back squat, conventional deadlift) build absolute strength; single-leg work transfers that strength into the running stride.

## Practical menu

- Squat: front squat or back squat (heavy)
- Hinge: trap-bar deadlift (back-friendly — the default if [profile.md](../profile.md) records a back history) or RDL
- Single-leg: Bulgarian split squat, step-up, single-leg RDL
- Skip: isolation work beyond what supports compounds

## What "mostly" allows

Some isolation work is justified:
- **Calf raises** — runners' calves need direct training; squats / deadlifts don't load them enough. See [calf-periodised-rep-ranges](calf-periodised-rep-ranges.md).
- **Face pulls / band pull-aparts** — posterior delt + rotator cuff health (shoulder injury prevention)
- **Hip abduction work** — for hip stability in single-leg running stance
- **Direct arm work** — only outside the marathon block, only when there's surplus time, only as the final 2 sets
- **Loaded carries** — Dicharry's runner-relevant single-implement work (overhead carry, suitcase carry, farmer carry) — see [carries-train-anti-lateral-flexion](carries-train-anti-lateral-flexion.md)

## What "mostly" doesn't allow during marathon block

- DB flys, cable crossovers, pec-deck
- Lateral raises (except as minor accessory)
- Bicep curls except as accessory
- Tricep pushdown except as accessory
- Any machine that isolates a single small muscle

## Where Matthews and Dicharry agree

Both authors are emphatic that natural lifters / runners get their strength + body-composition outcomes from a small number of barbell compounds + heavy loading. Both reject the "lots of isolation exercises with light weights" magazine paradigm. See [big-5-compound-priority](big-5-compound-priority.md) for the unified Big 5 list.

## Where Matthews extends this for non-marathon use

Outside a marathon block (e.g. an off-season strength phase), Matthews' 4-day BLS split adds:
- DB variants alongside barbell main lifts (incline DB bench, DB row, DB press)
- Isolation accessory work for visible muscle (lateral raise, face pull, curl, tricep)
- Higher-volume splits (Upper Body day, Arms day with biceps + triceps focus)

This is all legitimate when the goal shifts from "support marathon" to "build aesthetic muscle." Just understand that's a different goal.
