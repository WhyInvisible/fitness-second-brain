# Back-safe exercise contraindications (the "don't do this" list)

Per McGill Ch 2, 5, 9, 11. For anyone with a back-injury history (check [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles), the following exercises range from sub-optimal to actively harmful. They share a common feature: they load the spine while it's moving (the wrong job for torso muscles — see [core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion](core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion.md)).

## The banned list

| Exercise | Why it's bad | Mechanism | Substitute |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sit-ups, crunches, bicycle crunches** | Flex spine under load | Disc compression while flexed → delamination | McGill curl-up (head barely lifts) |
| **Pilates roll-up** | Segmental spinal flexion under load | Same as sit-ups, exaggerated | McGill curl-up + bird-dog |
| **"Imprint" the spine to the floor (Pilates)** | Deliberately flattens lumbar lordosis | Disrupts neutral spine → trains the injury pattern | Lumbar-supported neutral posture |
| **Knees-to-chest stretch** | Flexes spine, fires stretch reflex | 15 min relief, then re-flares disc pain worse | Tummy lie + fist under chin (the opposite) |
| **Scorpion stretch** (knees drop side-to-side) | Spine twist while flexed | Disc tear mechanism | Stop-twist tool (locked torso) |
| **Russian twist** | Spine rotation under load | Disc tear mechanism (per p. 98) | Pallof press, anti-rotation cable hold |
| **Superman / back extension** | Spine hyperextension under load | McGill measured ~6,000 N (~600 kg) compression on hyperextended spine | Bird-dog (extends hip not spine) |
| **Conventional barbell deadlift (post-injury)** | Maximum spine shear + compression + bending opportunity | The classic disc-bulge mechanism | Trap-bar deadlift, hip thrust, RDL with shorter ROM |
| **Back squat to depth (post-injury)** | Anterior pelvic tilt at bottom → spine flexion under load | Disc bulge mechanism + anterior femoral impingement | Goblet squat, front squat, split squat |
| **Kettlebell swing IF technique flawed** | Repeated spine flex-extend under load | Disc tear / endplate damage | Hip thrust (static load, hip drive) |
| **Static stretching of painful tissue** | Triggers stretch reflex (15 min relief) but worsens tissue | Re-sensitises the joint | Pain-free movement repetition |
| **Hamstring stretch as back fix** | Tight hamstrings are usually a SYMPTOM of back issues, not a cause | Stretching them doesn't fix the cause | Address the back; hamstrings often loosen as back heals |
| **Yoga poses that flex or twist to end-range** | Same as stretching | Same | Stop-twist tool, hip mobility instead |
| **Single-arm cable pulls heavy** | Asymmetric twist load on spine | Excessive twisting torque (p. 126) | Symmetric pulls first; only graduate to single-arm with locked torso |

## Permitted exercises (the McGill-approved list)

- **McGill Big 3** ([mcgill-big-3](mcgill-big-3.md)) — daily
- **Cat-camel** (NOT a stretch — 7–8 cycles only, never end-range)
- **Walking** ([walking-as-back-therapy](walking-as-back-therapy.md)) — brisk, arm swing
- **Hip-hinge patterns** ([hip-hinge-mechanics](hip-hinge-mechanics.md)) — shortstop, golfer's lift, trap-bar
- **Carries** — suitcase, farmer, overhead — anti-rotation under load
- **Push-ups** — anterior chain, anti-extension
- **Pull-ups, suspension rows, fire-hose pulls** — posterior chain
- **Cable pulls (symmetric)** — start light, descending pyramid
- **Walking lunge** — Phase 3 only
- **Hip airplane, clamshell, back bridge (glute-only)** — Phase 3 only
- **One bodyweight deep squat per day** to preserve pattern

## Application — audit the current programme against the list

Re-run this audit whenever the plan changes, especially if [profile.md](../profile.md) records a back history:
- The [MOC-core-training](../MOCs/MOC-core-training.md) rotating extras include "hollow body hold" — Dicharry would call this anti-extension and fine; McGill would say it's not primary but acceptable. KEEP, but de-emphasise.
- Hanging leg raise — fine if strict (no swing, no spinal flexion). Acceptable.
- Check that no sit-ups, Russian twists or supermans are programmed.
- If yoga is in the plan, an athlete with a back history skips poses that flex/twist the spine to end-range (no Camel/Wheel/Bow until acute pain history is many years cleared, and even then with caution).

## The "stretching is good" myth

McGill's biggest cultural opponent. Stretching a SHORT muscle (Dicharry's framework — [flexibility-vs-mobility-three-stiffness-causes](flexibility-vs-mobility-three-stiffness-causes.md)) IS justified. Stretching a PAINFUL back is almost never justified — it stimulates a 15-min stretch reflex (false relief) while loading the disc further.

Practical translation for an athlete with a back history: stretch hips, hip flexors, hamstrings, calves, T-spine via Dicharry's protocol when those tissues are genuinely short. Do NOT stretch the lumbar spine. The cat-camel is the ONE motion exercise McGill allows for the lumbar, and even that's a mobility cycle, not a stretch.
