# Perfect practice makes perfect (sloppy reps wire sloppy patterns)

Low's most-repeated maxim, borrowed from gymnastics coaching:

> "Practice does not make perfect; perfect practice makes perfect."

Sloppy reps don't just "not help" — they actively wire incorrect motor patterns into the brain. Once a sloppy pattern is grooved, it's harder to un-wire than to wire correctly in the first place.

## The mechanism

Motor learning is automatic — it happens in the cerebellum, motor cortex, and basal ganglia regardless of intent. If you practice a handstand with bent elbows + flared scaps, your brain memorises THAT as the handstand pattern. Six months in, when you try to do it correctly, you have to override the wired pattern (hard) instead of just doing it (easy).

This is why "more is not better" for skill work, and why fatigue is the enemy:

- A fatigued rep is almost always a sloppier rep.
- Sloppy reps wire incorrect patterns.
- Therefore: fatigue degrades skill acquisition, even if it builds strength.

## The rules that follow

1. **Skill work goes first, when fresh.** Not after strength work. See [skill-work-when-fresh](skill-work-when-fresh.md).
2. **Stop when you're off.** Don't push for "one more rep" if the last 3 were bad. Get out of the position, rest, retry only if quality returns.
3. **If you can't hold proper form at the current progression, drop a progression.** Don't grind through. See [easier-progression-is-the-fix-for-cheating-reps](easier-progression-is-the-fix-for-cheating-reps.md).
4. **Always fight for the position — don't bail.** Especially for handstand. Bailing teaches "when threatened, fall". Fighting (correcting) teaches "when threatened, save it". See [handstand-is-the-calisthenics-squat](handstand-is-the-calisthenics-squat.md).
5. **Visualise before attempting.** Especially for complex skills (muscle-up, kick to handstand). 10 s of mental rehearsal before the attempt primes correct firing.
6. **Calm down before retrying after a bad rep.** Take a few deep breaths. Frustration → tension → bad rep → more frustration. Reset.
7. **Film yourself.** Video doesn't lie. You will see faults a coach would see.

## The 3 stages of motor learning

Dicharry frames this neatly:
1. **Cognitive** — every rep is effortful; you have to think about each cue
2. **Associative** — reps are correct with thought, sloppy without
3. **Autonomous** — reflexive correct form, even under fatigue

Most calisthenics skills take **2–3×/wk for many months** to reach autonomous. Doing it once doesn't count. Doing it sloppily during the cognitive phase actively delays the transition to autonomous.

## The bell-curve corollary

Per [handstand-is-the-calisthenics-squat](handstand-is-the-calisthenics-squat.md): focus on raising the **consistent (median) quality** of your reps, not chasing PR outliers. A consistent 5-s handstand is more valuable than a one-off 10-s hold buried under 9 failures.

## Active when?

ALWAYS. Even maintenance reps during marathon block should be done with attention. Don't bang out sloppy pull-ups while distracted on a podcast.
