# Earn the next progression

Don't move on until the current progression is solid (5+ clean reps minimum, ideally 8+, hit consistently across 2 sessions). Tendons adapt slower than the strength feels — this is the operational rule that bridges the gap between [progressive-overload](progressive-overload.md) and [connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months](connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months.md).

## Why this rule is brutal-but-correct

Calisthenics progressions look like steps. Each step is genuinely harder because of leverage shifts (per [exercise-difficulty-scale-levels-1-16](exercise-difficulty-scale-levels-1-16.md)). Moving up before tendons are ready = slow injuries (elbow, shoulder, wrist tendinopathies) that take months to recover from. Steven Low (a physical therapist, whose own injuries came from over-pushing planche training in college) is emphatic that this is the single most-broken rule in calisthenics.

## The "5+ clean reps" rule (Low's specific formulation)

- **Clean** = full ROM, no kipping, no compensations, correct scapular position (per [protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control](protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control.md))
- **5 reps** = the lower bound. **8 reps** = safer / preferred upper threshold before advancing.
- **2 consecutive sessions** at the standard before advancing — eliminates lucky one-off sessions.

## The isometric / eccentric variants

For isometric holds (planche, front lever, L-sit): work the **60–75 % of max hold time** for N sets to hit 15+ total reps' equivalent (per [concentric-isometric-eccentric-equivalence](concentric-isometric-eccentric-equivalence.md)). Advance when you can hit max hold of next progression's hold target for 2 consecutive sessions.

For eccentric work (one-arm chin-up eccentrics, MU negatives): advance when you can do 3 sets × 3 cluster reps × 7–10 s with no rest between cluster reps — at which point one concentric rep is usually achievable.

## Example: muscle-up gate

Need **5 chest-to-bar pull-ups + 5 ring dips** with full ROM AND correct false grip (per [false-grip-rings-vs-bar](false-grip-rings-vs-bar.md)) before attempting strict ring muscle-up. Note that 10 regular pull-ups is not the same as 5 chest-to-bar. Even when the athlete's current numbers (see [profile.md](../profile.md)) technically open the gate, **don't start muscle-up work during a marathon block** (see [calisthenics-maintenance-in-marathon-block](calisthenics-maintenance-in-marathon-block.md)).

## Example: planche / front lever gate

Don't skip the tuck → advanced tuck → straddle → half-lay sequence. Each step has a connective tissue adaptation period. Attempting full lever / planche before the prerequisite is hit = inevitable shoulder tendon issue.

## When you DO have the strength but should still wait

A frequent trap, especially for athletes coming in with a barbell or general-strength background: **muscle is ready, connective tissue is not.** Symptoms:
- Twinge at joint when warming up
- Dull achiness on tendon (biceps tendon at shoulder, medial elbow, wrist) after sessions
- Soreness that persists >24h on the joint not the muscle

These mean: even if your reps say "advance", stay another mesocycle at the current progression and add more isolation / prehab work. See [easier-progression-is-the-fix-for-cheating-reps](easier-progression-is-the-fix-for-cheating-reps.md) for the inverse rule when form is the limiter.
