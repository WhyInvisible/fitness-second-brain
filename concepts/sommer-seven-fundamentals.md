# Sommer's seven fundamentals (FSAS + FBAS + FLS)

Sommer's GymnasticBodies (GB) curriculum is built around **seven fundamental gymnastic strength training (GST) elements**, organised into three categories. The seven together are the canonical adult-GST curriculum — Sommer's claim is that any serious gymnastic strength program must develop all seven, in this order, with no skipping.

## The seven

**FSAS — Fundamental Straight Arm Strength** (4 elements):
1. **Front Lever (FL)** — supinated grip horizontal hang, body parallel to ground, face up
2. **Straddle Planche (sPL)** — pronated grip prone hold, body parallel to ground, face down, legs straddled
3. **Side Lever (SL)** — single-arm side hold, body parallel to ground sideways
4. **Manna (MN)** — seated, hands behind hips, body lifted with legs pointing past vertical toward face

**FBAS — Fundamental Bent Arm Strength** (2 elements):
5. **Hollow Back Press handstand (HBP)** — press handstand with controlled hollow-back finishing position; in F1 the entry-level versions are pushups/dips
6. **Rope Climb (RC)** — assisted-pulling skill; in F1 the entry-level versions are rows

**FLS — Fundamental Leg Strength** (1 element):
7. **Single Leg Squat (SLS)** — the pistol squat as the primary single-leg pattern

## The progression logic

Every fundamental is approached via **Preparatory Elements (PE)** → **Specific Elements (SE)** broken into the standard 12-week mastery template. For example:

**Front Lever progression (across F1 + F2):**
- PE1–PE6 (F1): Bent Hollow Body Hold → Bent HB Rock → Straddle HB Hold → Straddle HB Rock → HB Hold → HB Rock
- PE7–PE14 (F2): Negative Body Lever Straddle → ... → Body Lever → ... → Kip Extension
- SE1+ (F3): Open Front Lever → ½ Straddle FL → ...

Equivalent ladders exist for all seven fundamentals across F1 + F2 + F3.

## Sommer's claim of completeness

Sommer asserts the seven fundamentals together cover the full surface area of GST:
- **FSAS (front lever, planche, side lever, manna)** trains the four cardinal straight-arm leverage positions (anterior/posterior/lateral horizontal + posterior compression)
- **FBAS (hollow back press handstand + rope climb)** trains the two bent-arm strength patterns (vertical push + vertical pull, with HBP also developing the active flexibility/strength bridge between front-body and back-body)
- **FLS (single leg squat)** trains the single-leg knee-dominant pattern (the only leg pattern Sommer prioritises at this level — hip-dominant work is folded into the FSAS/FBAS work via shoulder bridge variants)

Missing this completeness, you have holes that surface later. Manna missing? Your compression / posterior shoulder / scap retractor will hard-cap your front lever, planche, and press handstand. Side Lever missing? Your obliques will fail in the side planche / one-arm work. Etc.

## Comparison with Low's framework

Low approaches the same skill domain via the 16-level chart and per-category skill progressions (handstand / pull / push / dip / L-sit / lever / muscle-up / squat). The differences:

- **Low lets you pick goal skills** ("I want a muscle-up + handstand → here are the ladders"). Sommer says "all seven, in order, no choice."
- **Low's "L-sit to manna" progression** = Sommer's Manna (MN) progression. Same destination.
- **Low's handstand ladder** = Sommer's H1 + the HBP fundamental. Sommer treats handstand as a parallel curriculum that runs alongside the F-books.
- **Low has no equivalent to Side Lever or Rope Climb as primary elements.** Low covers rows and side-plank-like exercises in passing.
- **Low has no Straddle Planche as a beginner target.** Low puts full planche at Level 10–11 and works through it via planche pushup progressions. Sommer puts straddle planche as one of four FSAS elements every student trains from F1 PE1.

The frameworks are reconcilable but Sommer's is more committed to the *full GST curriculum*. Low's is more flexible / goal-driven.

## Practical application

**During a marathon block**: not active. Maintenance work continues on pull-up / push-up / dip volumes per [calisthenics-maintenance-in-marathon-block](calisthenics-maintenance-in-marathon-block.md).

**Early off-season (the first few months after the A-race)**: still not the curriculum to run. Use Low's framework for a muscle-up + handstand block. But note: **what Low calls "muscle-up Stage 0 = pull-up volume" maps to Sommer's RC/PE1–PE6 (Hinge Row → Bent Arm Chin Hang)** — you're really building the rope-climb fundamental even though you're calling it pull-up volume.

**Later (once muscle-up + handstand are achieved)**: serious consideration. Adopting the Sommer F1 PE1–PE6 across all seven fundamentals = ~12 months of work. After that, F2 PE7–PE14 + first SEs = another 12+ months. F3 = a third year. Combined with H1+H2 running in parallel, that's a 3–5 year arc.

**Equipment requirements for the full curriculum** (check against the equipment in [profile.md](../profile.md)):
- Pull-up bar
- Parallel bars
- Gymnastics rings
- Rope or substitute
- Parallettes
- Mat / soft surface
- Weighted bar for shoulder mobility (dumbbells/barbell work)

Several can be home-built; some require gym access.
