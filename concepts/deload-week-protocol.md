# Deload week protocol — every 8-10 weeks

Per Matthews Ch 16. Heavy weightlifting accumulates CNS fatigue that compounds invisibly across phases. Every 8-10 weeks, deliberately drop intensity for a week (or take 4-5 days completely off) to restore CNS, joints, and motivation.

## The protocol

**Option A — Deload week (recommended first):**
- Same exercises, same sets-and-reps, **40-60% of working weight**
- 4-5 lifting sessions per week, but each is light + short
- Use to practice form, work on technique weaknesses, reset

**Option B — Full rest week (if Option A doesn't restore):**
- 4-5 consecutive days off all weights
- Light movement (walking, easy cardio, yoga) is fine
- Return to training after 4-5 days; resume at the load you ended the previous phase

Matthews' recommendation: start with Option A. If you don't feel reinvigorated and physically/mentally ready to hit heavy weights again at the end of the week, escalate to Option B for the next deload.

## Why every 8-10 weeks (not weekly cutbacks)

- Cutback weeks within a block (every 3-4 weeks, see [cutbacks-every-3-4-weeks](cutbacks-every-3-4-weeks.md)) handle short-term fatigue
- Deload weeks handle ACCUMULATED CNS fatigue from sustained heavy training
- Research-supported: significant strength loss doesn't occur until ~5 weeks of complete detraining (cited in BLS Ch 16). 1 week off is biologically inconsequential for strength.
- Mental component: 8-10 weeks of "beat last week" is psychologically grinding. The deload is a planned break to want to come back.

## How to eat during a deload

- **Bulking:** reduce calories to maintenance for the week
- **Cutting:** keep calories the same (small deficit is fine even with reduced training)
- **Maintenance:** no change

Matthews' point: don't over-compensate either direction. The deload is for recovery; food intake should be steady.

## Application: integrating with marathon training

The deload-every-8-weeks BLS rhythm aligns awkwardly with marathon periodisation. A marathon block has its own [three-week-taper](three-week-taper.md) that already serves as a strength deload (volume drops, intensity holds in running; strength goes to near-zero).

**Within a marathon block:** [cutbacks-every-3-4-weeks](cutbacks-every-3-4-weeks.md) is sufficient for strength maintenance — no formal BLS-style deload needed. The whole strength block IS the "lower-intensity" phase.

**Off-season / strength-primary block (after the A-race):** Matthews' rhythm applies fully. 8 weeks of progressive overload → 1 week deload → 8 weeks → 1 week → ... This is when CNS fatigue from heavy 4-6 rep lifting can accumulate, and the explicit deload becomes valuable.

**Warning signs that you need a deload sooner than week 8:**
- 2 weeks of failed lifts (couldn't beat last week) on multiple lifts
- Sleep deteriorating
- Workouts feel like grinding rather than energising
- Joints ache between sessions
- General malaise / motivation drop
