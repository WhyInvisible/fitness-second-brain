# Balance training and precision jumping as accessible high-leverage skills

Per Le Corre chapters 26 (Balancing) and 28 (Airborne), balance training (especially single-leg + narrow-surface) and precision jumping (mark-to-mark landings, stuck on first contact, no step-out) are two of the highest-leverage skills with the lowest equipment requirements and lowest recovery cost — yet they're almost entirely absent from conventional fitness programming.

## Why balance training matters

1. **Single-leg balance is the best predictor of standing balance generally.** Le Corre: "Single-leg balance is arguably the best indicator of bipedal standing balance (or lack thereof)." Standing walking is just a sequence of single-leg balances + transitions; deficient single-leg balance → walking + running inefficiency directly.
2. **Trains joint stability dynamically.** Static "core stability" drills (planks, etc.) don't train the joints to handle balance perturbations. Beam walking, single-leg stands, tiptoe holds do.
3. **Develops *both* righting and tilting reflexes.** Righting (correcting against gravity while on a stable surface) and tilting (correcting against gravity while the surface itself moves) are *different* reflexes — they don't transfer between each other (SAID principle). Train both: stable narrow surfaces (beam, log) AND unstable surfaces (slackline, foam, balance board) AND uneven outdoor surfaces (rocks, hill faces).
4. **Address arm-counterbalance dependency.** Most untrained people balance via arm-flailing. Le Corre's progression: start with arm-counterbalance allowed, work toward arms-relaxed-at-sides + counterbalance via leg + hip + spine adjustments only. This frees the arms for tasks (carrying, gesturing, throwing) while balancing.
5. **Builds ankle / knee / hip / spine awareness simultaneously.** Balance is a whole-chain neuromuscular activity. Trains proprioception across the chain, not just one joint.
6. **Elderly + middle-aged longevity.** 1 in 3 people over 65 fall annually; 1 in 4 over 50 who break a hip die within a year. Balance training reduces both fall risk and fall consequences. For a younger athlete the payoff is decades away, but the habit is easiest to build now, not later.

## Why precision jumping matters

1. **Landing skill > jumping skill.** Le Corre: "No jump is good if the landing is bad." Most plyometric programs ignore the landing entirely. Precision landing (square stance, stuck on first contact, no step-out, balanced) is the actual skill that transfers to safety + performance.
2. **Trains landing across launch + trajectory + landing as one system.** Jumping is a 3-phase movement (launch / trajectory / landing). Practising the landing in isolation misses the integration. Precision jumps train all three: you launch knowing the landing is small + stuck + balanced; the launch is calibrated to make that landing achievable.
3. **Builds eccentric force production.** Square landing demands deceleration of body mass through hips + knees + ankles in a controlled hinge + squat — eccentric leg work that direct-transfers to descending stairs, downhill running, agility tasks.
4. **Develops *accuracy under power*.** Most plyo work is "jump high / jump far"; rarely is it "jump to *this exact spot* and stick the landing." The accuracy + power combination is the actual real-world demand (clearing a stream, hopping rocks, exiting a vehicle on uneven ground).
5. **Plyometric carryover with low injury risk.** Most plyometric injuries come from landing inefficiency on tall boxes / long jumps / depth jumps at intensity. Precision jumps at *low* intensity but high *accuracy* deliver most of the neural adaptation with much less mechanical risk.

## Le Corre's progression frameworks

### Balance progression (beam walking)

1. **2x4 board on the floor** — flat, stable, ~3.5 in wide. Forward walk, then backward walk.
2. **Single-leg stand on the board** — start with foot fully on board, progress to ball-of-foot only, progress to single-leg tiptoe.
3. **Add tasks** — head turns, body rotations, arm reaches while balancing.
4. **Narrower surface** — board edge, painted line, rope on floor.
5. **Rounded surface** — log, pole, rounded beam.
6. **Slightly unstable surface** — soft surface (foam), then slacker variant.
7. **Add elevation** — only after the surface variant is owned at floor level; danger = manageable consequence.
8. **Add load** — backpack / weighted vest.
9. **Combine** — balance + carry, balance + throw / catch, balance + low-intensity manipulation.

### Precision jump progression

1. **Standing leg-swing jump** — single-leg launch, two-feet landing on a small target (1 ft × 2 ft board, or chalk mark). Distance 2–4 ft. Stick the landing; no step-out.
2. **Two-foot power jump** (broad jump) — both feet launch, both feet land. Distance increasing from 3 ft.
3. **Vertical precision jump** — jump up to a slightly elevated small landing surface (a low box edge ≤ 12 in). Land balls of feet, decelerate through ankles + knees.
4. **Distance + small target** — jump 4–6 ft horizontally to a small landing surface (1 ft × 1 ft board).
5. **Add depth** (downward jump) — only after square landing on flat is owned. Start with low elevation drops to large surfaces; progress slowly.
6. **Combine** — jump + land + immediately balance, jump + land + immediately sprint, jump + climb position.

**Rule:** stick the landing on 3–5 consecutive reps before progressing intensity (Le Corre's 3–5 rule).

## Doses

- **Balance:** 5–10 min daily on a 2x4 board (in the flat). High frequency, low intensity. No equipment cost.
- **Precision jumps:** 5–8 jumps × 2–3 sets, 2×/week. Quality over quantity. Stop the set when accuracy drops, not when fatigue hits.

## Application

- **During a marathon build:**
  - **Daily 5-min balance** on a 2x4 board (or painted line + tiptoe) as part of a morning routine or pre-run warm-up. Trivially low recovery cost; single-leg work helps any left/right asymmetry (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles) and builds ankle stability (running injury prophylaxis). See exercises/balance-beam-walk.
  - **Precision jumps 1× / week** as part of a strength-session warm-up — 5 × leg-swing precision jumps + 5 × two-foot precision jumps, low distance (3–4 ft), small target. ~3 min. Doesn't substitute for power-block plyometrics ([plyometrics-ground-time-not-jump-height](plyometrics-ground-time-not-jump-height.md)) but adds the *accuracy* dimension Dicharry's box hops don't have. See exercises/precision-jump.
- **Travel or no-equipment stretches:** balance on real-world surfaces (curbs, walls, low fences, fallen branches). Precision jumps onto natural targets (between stones, between marks on a path). Daily.
- **Off-season after the A-race:** add unstable-surface balance (foam, balance board, slackline if available) once stable-surface beam work is owned. Precision jumps progress in distance + downward variants for power development.

## Cross-source

- **Dicharry's [plyometrics-ground-time-not-jump-height](plyometrics-ground-time-not-jump-height.md)** — Dicharry rule: ground contact ≤ 250 ms, box hops mid-shin to knee only. Le Corre's precision-jump frame is *complementary* — Dicharry trains rebound speed; Le Corre trains accuracy + landing quality. Different stimuli, both useful.
- **Pavel's [pop-and-float-ballistic-cue](pop-and-float-ballistic-cue.md)** — KB-swing "pop and float" is the same ballistic principle as Le Corre's airborne phase. The float moment teaches body awareness mid-air.
- **Dicharry's [body-drives-form](body-drives-form.md)** — if you can't balance on one foot for 30 s, you can't run efficiently on one foot for 90 min. Body capability gates form.

## What it's NOT

- It's NOT a substitute for the Power-block plyometric work in the Dicharry corpus (see [power-block-6-weeks-pre-race](power-block-6-weeks-pre-race.md)). It's a *complement* — lower intensity, higher accuracy focus.
- It's NOT static "core stability" work. Balance is dynamic + corrective; planks are isometric + endurance.
- It's NOT high-impact / high-intensity plyometrics. Keep distance + elevation low. Risk-to-danger ratio: low danger throughout learning phase.
- It's NOT to be done barefoot from day 1 on outdoor surfaces. Build foot strength gradually (Le Corre's 3-grade footwear descent).
