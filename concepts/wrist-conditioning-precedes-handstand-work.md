# Wrist conditioning precedes handstand work

Sommer's distinctive contribution to handstand pedagogy: **the wrists need their own dedicated 4-series, 8-step progression program**, mastered before serious handstand work begins. The wrist is the single most common reason handstand training fails for adult athletes — desk-job wrist mobility is poor, the loads in inverted positions are large, and the connective tissue takes months to adapt.

## The four wrist progressions (H1)

Sommer provides four parallel wrist progressions, each with 4 preparatory elements + 4 specific elements (8 steps total per series, 32 wrist exercises total):

1. **WP — Wrist Pushup** (wrist flexion-extension in pressing pattern)
   - PE1 Wall Wrist Walk → PE2 Long Hollow Wrist Walk → PE3 Kneeling Wrist Walk → PE4 Wrist Walk
   - SE1 Wall Wrist Pushup → SE2 Long Hollow Wrist Pushup → SE3 Kneeling Wrist Pushup → SE4 Wrist Pushup
   - Each = 5×5r mastery

2. **FKP — First Knuckle Pushup** (knuckle flexion strength + finger conditioning)
   - PE1 Wall First Knuckle Walk → ... → SE4 First Knuckle Pushup
   - Each = 5×5r

3. **WR — Wrist Rock** (ulnar + radial deviation under load)
   - PE1 Wall Wrist Rock Walk → ... → SE4 Wrist Rock
   - Each = 5×5r

4. **FTP — Fingertip Pushup** (finger extensor + small joint stability)
   - PE1 Wall Fingertip Walk → ... → SE4 Fingertip Pushup
   - Each = 5×5r

Plus four **wrist stretches** (WRS/1, WRS/2, WRS/3, WRS/4), 10r each before every workout.

## Why this scale of conditioning

Sommer's reasoning, implicit across the H1 program structure:
- The wrist is loaded in an unfamiliar position (extension under bodyweight) every time you do any handstand work
- Adult wrist mobility is typically poor (desk work, never inverted)
- Wrist tendinopathy is slow to heal (8–16 weeks) and prevents all handstand training during recovery
- Building wrist tolerance ahead of handstand strength means you avoid the failure mode of "I have the shoulder strength but my wrists give out"
- The conditioning also builds the finger / hand control needed for balance correction in freestanding handstand (per Low's "correct from fingertips, not shoulders")

## Why this matters more than most calisthenics writers acknowledge

Most calisthenics frameworks (including Low's, to some extent) treat wrist conditioning as part of the warm-up — a few wrist circles + wrist stretches before each session. Sommer treats it as **its own multi-month curriculum** that begins before serious handstand work.

This is consistent with [connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months](connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months.md): the wrist's connective tissue adapts on the same months-to-years timeline as the shoulder's, but is loaded just as hard. Without dedicated programming, it becomes the rate-limiter.

## The four stretches (do before every workout — WRS/1-4)

Each prescribed as 10r before each workout:
- **WRS/1**: heel of palm on floor, lean back, fingers pointing toward body
- **WRS/2**: back of wrist on floor, lean back, knuckle-side down
- **WRS/3**: heel of palm on floor, fingers pointing directly forward (extension stretch)
- **WRS/4**: heel of palm on floor, little fingers turned inward, lean back (rotational stretch)

These four cover the four primary wrist ROM directions and take ~3 minutes total.

## Practical translation

**During a marathon build (handstand at maintenance only)**: not active. Wrist work isn't needed while handstand work is at maintenance level only.

**Start of a handstand-focused block (e.g. the off-season after the A-race)**: **Adopt the four wrist stretches (WRS/1–4) immediately as part of every handstand session warm-up.** This is the single most valuable Sommer import for this phase. 10r each, 3 min total, before any wall-handstand work.

Optionally adopt WP/PE1 (Wall Wrist Walk) and FTP/PE1 (Wall Fingertip Walk) as warm-up additions. Both are low-cost and start building wrist conditioning before it becomes a problem.

**Diagnostic for whether to expand**: if wrist discomfort emerges during handstand work, immediately stop the handstand work for that session, complete the 4 wrist stretches + light wrist mobility, and start the full WP / FTP / WR / FKP curriculum the next session. This is the [no-pain-no-gain-is-wrong](no-pain-no-gain-is-wrong.md) protocol applied specifically to the wrist.

**Equipment**: none — all 4 wrist series are done on a wall + floor.

## Common risk factors

Check [profile.md](../profile.md) for which of these apply:

- **Desk job** → wrist extension ROM is likely limited
- **No prior handstand work** → wrist load tolerance is unbuilt
- **A long running-focused block before the handstand block** → no handstand loading for months, so the wrists start the new block from cold

Any combination of these means wrist conditioning deserves disproportionate attention in the first 8–12 weeks of a new handstand block. Treat the 4 wrist stretches as non-negotiable before any handstand work.
