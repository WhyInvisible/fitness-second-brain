# The Natural Movement vocabulary — what counts as "all the movement skills"

Per Le Corre Manifesto principle 6 ("Unspecialised"), the curriculum of human movement is ~10–12 fundamental skills — far more than the "squat / hinge / push / pull / twist / bend" of conventional "functional fitness." The full vocabulary is what humans actually USED, before specialisation and sedentarism collapsed the repertoire.

## The taxonomy

Le Corre groups the skills into three families:

### Locomotive skills (moving the body through space)

- **Ground movement** — lying, rolling, crawling (multiple variants: forearm, knee-forearm, knee-hand, foot-hand "bear", inverted foot-hand "crab")
- **Sitting / kneeling / getting up** — the ground transitions (Brazilian sit-and-rise predicts longevity; see exercises/natural-get-up-variants)
- **Balancing** — on stable, narrow, rounded, uneven, slippery, unstable, or elevated surfaces
- **Gait** — walking, running, stepping (over/under/through, up/down, accuracy-stepping)
- **Airborne** — jumping (forward, vertical, depth, broad), landing (square, split, rolling), precision jumping (mark-to-mark)
- **Climbing** — hanging, pulling-up, push-pull climbs, foot-pinch climbs, descents, traverses
- **Swimming** — covered in Le Corre's method but not in the book

### Manipulative skills (interacting with objects + loads)

- **Lifting** — deadlift, lapping (for odd objects too bulky for a single-motion lift), squat clean (for explosive overhead)
- **Carrying** — 15+ named hold variations: hand (farmer), waist, hip, lumbar, chest (with 7+ wrap styles), side shoulder, front shoulder, forehead, head, partner carries
- **Throwing** — overhand rotational, push-press, front-swing (= the KB swing minus release), single-arm + two-arm variants
- **Catching** — partner-based, eye-hand coordination, eccentric deceleration of the load

### Combative skills (defending self / others — covered in MovNat but not in this book)

- **Defending** — blocking, evading, sprawling
- **Grappling** — controlling, escaping, restraining
- **Striking** — hands, feet, elbows, knees (the same throw mechanics as object-throwing)

## Why the vocabulary matters

1. **"You can't train what you don't have a name for."** Le Corre's central operational reason for naming hundreds of positions + techniques. Unnamed movements are unrehearsed; unrehearsed movements stay unavailable in real-world demands.
2. **The skills are interrelated and work symbiotically.** You can't be a great climber without grip developed in carrying. You can't run well over rough terrain without single-leg balance from balancing-walking. Compartmentalising them creates "competence islands surrounded by helplessness."
3. **Conventional "functional" fitness (squat / hinge / push / pull / twist / bend) is *too narrow*.** It preserves the gym setting and discards climbing, balance, jumping, carrying-variation, throwing/catching — most of the actual human movement vocabulary.

## Cross-source synthesis

- **Pavel's "two exercises is enough"** (swing + get-up) is *the opposite* directional argument from Le Corre's "the full vocabulary is the curriculum." The reconciliation: Pavel's two exercises are sufficient for *power + posterior-chain + body-as-one-piece* development; Le Corre's argument is that this is insufficient for the *broader real-world demands* (climbing, balancing, accuracy-jumping, varied carries, throw/catch). **Synthesis:** Pavel's two exercises as the strength core; Le Corre's broader vocabulary as the breadth supplement (especially during travel blocks and the post-race off-season).
- **Low's calisthenics 5 skill ladders** (handstand, muscle-up, L-sit, front lever, one-arm hang) cover *static-hold strength + ring work*; Le Corre's locomotive + manipulative skills cover everything else. Compatible, not competing.
- **McGill + Pavel + Le Corre triple-agreement** on hip-hinge as the apex lifting pattern. Le Corre adds: lifting odd / unfamiliar objects on uneven terrain is the next-level adaptation gap.

## Practical translation

- **During a marathon block:** a runner who also lifts and does calisthenics typically covers ~60% of the locomotive skills (running, gait, some balancing, some climbing via calisthenics) and ~30% of the manipulative skills (lifting, some carries). Commonly underused: **crawling, ground movement, get-up variants, precision jumping, accuracy stepping, throw / catch, varied carry holds.** The deficit isn't a crisis but a real gap. Audit the athlete's own coverage against the taxonomy above.
- **Travel or low-volume blocks:** specifically train the vocabulary gap — see [MOCs/MOC-natural-movement.md](../MOCs/MOC-natural-movement.md).
- **After the A-race (a few months of off-season):** dedicate one weekly session to vocabulary expansion alongside any calisthenics block.
