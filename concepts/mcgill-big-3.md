# McGill Big 3 (curl-up, side plank, bird-dog)

Per McGill Ch 9. Three back-safe core exercises selected from years of UWaterloo Spine Biomechanics Lab measurement. They earned the "Big 3" status because they uniquely build muscular stiffness around the spine WITHOUT spinal flexion under load, prevent the painful joint micro-movements that re-sensitise the back, and challenge the right muscles (rectus abdominis + obliques + transverse abdominis + quadratus lumborum + multifidus + erectors + glutes) in the right way (isometric, endurance-focused, with the spine in neutral).

## The three exercises (McGill's protocol)

### 1. McGill curl-up
Lie on back, ONE knee bent + ONE leg straight. Hands palms-down UNDER the lumbar (preserves the natural arch — do NOT flatten lumbar to floor). Elbows hover off floor. Brace abs. Lift head + shoulders only ~1 inch — head barely lifts. NO sit-up motion. Imagine a scale under your shoulders reading zero. Neck stays in line with spine. Switch bent leg between sets (unless one leg causes pain, then keep that one bent).

### 2. Side plank / side bridge
Beginner version (use first): lie on side, elbow under shoulder, top hand on opposite shoulder (pulls elbow across chest — relieves shoulder pressure). Knees bent ~90°. Hip-hinge forward (squat pattern) to elevate hips into a straight line from knees to shoulders. Standard version: feet stacked or top foot in front, body straight line from feet to shoulders. Advanced: rolling side bridge (side → front plank 3 s → other side, 1 rep).

### 3. Bird-dog
All fours, hands under shoulders, knees under hips. Find spine "sweet spot" (the position that's pain-free — usually neutral or slight pelvic tilt). Brace abs. Extend opposite arm + leg to horizontal — DO NOT lift higher than horizontal. Push the heel AWAY (don't lift the leg higher). "Sweep the floor" between holds — sweep hand + knee along floor without bearing weight. Advanced: "draw squares" with hand + foot. Throughout: NO spine motion, NO hip rotation.

## Daily prescription — the [Russian Descending Pyramid](russian-descending-pyramid.md)

Holds are ALWAYS 8–10 seconds. NEVER extend the hold time when progressing.

- **Curl-up:** Set 1 = 6 reps, rest 20–30 s. Set 2 = 4 reps, rest 20 s. Set 3 = 2 reps. Done.
- **Side plank:** 6/4/2 pyramid per side.
- **Bird-dog:** 6/4/2 pyramid per side.

**To progress:** add ONE rep to each set (e.g. 7/5/3). Do NOT extend the holds. The short hold restores O₂/acid balance and prevents the muscle cramping that breaks form.

Total time for the full Big 3 done as a pyramid: ~10 min. McGill: "perform this routine every day — even twice per day."

## Five key tips (McGill Ch 9, p. 102)

1. **Daily.** This is not optional.
2. **NOT first thing in the morning.** Discs are swollen — risk is high. Mid-morning to dinner is optimal. Also not right before bed.
3. **Dose matters.** Tender backs may need multiple short sessions per day. Split the pyramid across AM + PM if needed.
4. **Brace, don't hollow.** No "sucking the navel to spine" — that's the Pilates myth McGill explicitly refutes. See [abdominal-brace](abdominal-brace.md).
5. **Spine pain-free, motion at shoulders + hips only.** If you feel spine motion or pain, regress the variant.

## Why "back-safe"

None of the three involve spinal flexion under load. Spinal flexion under load (sit-ups, crunches, Pilates roll-up) compresses discs and delaminates collagen layers — the exact mechanism of disc bulge / herniation. The Big 3 train the same muscles in their natural job description: STOP motion to support load, not CREATE motion under load. See [core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion](core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion.md).

## When to add cat-camel (greasing the joints)

Before the Big 3, McGill suggests 7–8 slow cycles of cat-camel — NOT a stretch, never to end range. This reduces internal spine friction before the stability work. 3–4 seconds per cycle.

## What CANNOT replace the Big 3

Per [back-safe-exercise-contraindications](back-safe-exercise-contraindications.md), the common gym "core" substitutes (sit-ups, crunches, Russian twists, supermans, knees-to-chest, Pilates roll-up) are NOT replacements — they're actively harmful for someone with back history. The Big 3 is the standard.

## Synthesis with Dicharry

Dicharry endorses the Big 3 pattern (his exercises overlap heavily — thread-the-needle plank, super-swiss side plank — but layer dynamic anti-rotation on top). In practice:
- **McGill Big 3 daily** = the back-prehab floor. Non-negotiable. Isometric base.
- **Dicharry dynamic anti-rotation extras** (rotating across the week) = the running-integration layer, added once the Big 3 isometric capacity is established.

See [dynamic-anti-rotation-beats-static-planks](dynamic-anti-rotation-beats-static-planks.md).

## Application

For an athlete with a lower-back injury history (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles), the Big 3 are not "an exercise option" — they are the bedrock daily practice for years of back tissue management ([back-tissue-heals-over-years](back-tissue-heals-over-years.md)). The workspace integrates them into [MOC-core-training](../MOCs/MOC-core-training.md), with the Russian Pyramid dosing and the cat-camel opener above.

## Next-level progressions (post-mastery)

The basic Big 3 above is the REHAB / DAILY MAINTENANCE form. Once these are automatic, UBF Ch 11 lays out a 5-10 year progression ladder:
- **Curl-up:** pre-brace + elbows off floor + breathing under brace.
- **Side bridge:** rolling progression + side bridge under heavy breathing.
- **Bird-dog:** sweep the floor + draw squares + explosive bird-dog + stir-the-pot (swiss ball).

See [big-3-advanced-progressions](big-3-advanced-progressions.md) for the full ladder + 12-week curriculum.
