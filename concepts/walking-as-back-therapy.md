# Walking as primary back therapy ("nature's back balm")

Per McGill Ch 10, walking is the single highest-leverage non-exercise intervention for the back. Brisk walking with arm swing turns a daily activity into therapy — but slow "mall strolling" makes back pain worse. The distinction is mechanistically important.

## Why slow walking HURTS the back

- Pelvis doesn't drop and recover with each step → quadratus lumborum statically contracts → muscle cramps and crushing spine compression.
- Arms not swinging → no torso sling activation → no elastic recoil to unload the spine.
- Locked knees on each step → no spring action of the leg muscles → all impact transfers to the lumbar.

## Why brisk walking with arm swing HEALS

- Each step requires the QL + obliques to STOP pelvis drop → trains the spine stabilisers exactly as they're designed.
- Arm swing from the shoulder (not the elbow) loads the torso slings → elastic tissue stores and releases energy → spine unloads.
- Larger, faster steps cue triple extension at hip/knee/ankle → loads the posterior chain → builds the muscle structure that supports the lumbar.
- The cumulative effect: brisk walking RESTORES the lordotic curve and DESENSITISES the discs.

## The technique (McGill's checklist)

1. Stand tall in pain-free posture (the "switch-off" position from the standing posture assessment — see [mcgill-pain-trigger-diagnostic](mcgill-pain-trigger-diagnostic.md)).
2. Lightly stiffen abdominal muscles (~5–10% brace — see [abdominal-brace](abdominal-brace.md)).
3. Try a few marching steps in place — slightly higher knees than normal.
4. Begin walking. Swing the arms about the shoulders (not the elbows).
5. Larger, faster steps. Walk "like you have somewhere to be."

## The interval-build for an acute back

If 10 steps cause pain → 6–8 steps every hour. Build to 30 min × 3 per day (pre-breakfast, midday, after dinner). Park-bench decompression (lean on bench/railing, hands push down, work hips towards the bar) every few minutes if needed during long walks.

## Practical translation

For a runner, walking isn't exotic. But the McGill framing matters:
- **Easy "shake-out" walks** between hard sessions are not just recovery, they're back therapy IF done at brisk pace + arm swing.
- **Recovery jogs** ([workout-recovery-jog](workout-recovery-jog.md)) at conversational pace get the same benefit — running is just brisk walking with a flight phase. McGill explicitly notes (p. 116) that jogging is fine once walking is pain-free, with a 5-day rest every 2–3 weeks for tissue gristling.
- **Backwards uphill walking with arms overhead** is McGill's progression cue — could be a good post-long-run cooldown drill on a treadmill.
- **If a back niggle EVER appears**, walking — not lying down — is the primary therapy ([virtual-surgery-relapse-protocol](virtual-surgery-relapse-protocol.md) Phase 1).

## Cross-source agreement

Dicharry implicitly endorses brisk walking with arm swing via the arm-jab cue ("swing arms backward, jab elbows into the runner behind you"). Same mechanism: load the torso slings, unload the spine. McGill's elaboration of the SLOW walking failure mode is unique.
