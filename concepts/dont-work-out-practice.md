# Don't work out — practice

Per Pavel Pt I ("Don't 'Work Out' — Practice"), the mental frame for strength training should be **rehearsal of a skill**, not the bodybuilder-flavoured concept of "working out" (whose root meaning is "to exhaust by extraction"). Russians instead call a session a "recharge." The shift is operational, not just aesthetic.

## The two frames

| "Work out" frame | "Practice" frame |
|---|---|
| Extract energy / get sweaty / chase pump | Deposit energy / refine skill / leave with more than you took |
| Performance evaluated by volume + sweat + soreness | Performance evaluated by quality + crispness + control |
| Bigger workout = better workout | Sufficient stimulus = perfect workout |
| Variety / novelty drives progress | Repetition + refinement drives progress |
| Plays the role of "exercise" | Plays the role of "training" |
| Quit when you can't continue | Quit when quality drops |
| Music, phone, conversation OK | Phone off, full attention, no distractions |

Pavel: "Just training. Your session is barely half an hour long; stay focused."

## Where it comes from

The mental model is borrowed from skill domains:
- **Music**: a pianist *practises* scales daily, not "works out" the piano.
- **Martial arts**: kata is repeated for years, not "worked out."
- **Figure skating, gymnastics, diving**: evaluated on style + control, not on volume.
- **Surgery / shooting / piloting**: "competence emerges from focused repetition under conditions of consequence."

Strength + power + endurance are *qualities of motor control* — therefore skills. Treat them like skills.

## Operational implications

1. **Focus is non-negotiable.** No phone, no chat, no TV, no water break, no bathroom. 30 minutes of pure attention.
2. **Quality stops the session, not quantity.** When form fails, the session is over. Adding "one more set with bad form" damages the brain-wiring of the pattern.
3. **The session deposits energy** ([anti-glycolytic-training](anti-glycolytic-training.md), [step-loading-progression](step-loading-progression.md)). Pavel/Ivanov: "the purpose of training is to STORE energy in the body, not exhaust it."
4. **Daily or near-daily** beats episodic-massive sessions for skill consolidation. The motor patterns need frequent rehearsal to become automatic.
5. **Boring is a feature, not a bug.** Step loading + no variety = the brain has bandwidth to refine technique. Constant program-hopping = each session is a re-learning event.
6. **Even warm-ups are practice.** Pavel: extend the mindful-practice mentality to the prying goblet squat, the halo, the stretches. Nothing is "junk reps."

## What practice mode looks like in S&S

- Warm up with prying goblet squats: don't count reps, *evaluate* the depth, the elbow position, the heel contact.
- Swings: don't worry about hitting time targets, *observe* the hike pass quality, the pop-and-float, the glute cramp at the top, the breathing rhythm.
- Get-ups: 30–35 seconds per rep (Anna Cannington's prescription), not because time is the target but because the slow tempo exposes shoulder packing flaws + balance wobbles. "Explore the movements" (Dan John).
- Between sets: walk, shake out tension, breathe deeply. Use the rest *to practise the breath*, not to recover passively.

## Application

Achievement-oriented, multi-discipline athletes tend to "work out" by instinct — chase metrics, push the dose, conflate volume with progress. The practice frame is a corrective against that instinct, especially in the strength domain, where pushing volume is the dominant failure mode.

Concrete: when adding S&S to the plan, the success criterion is NOT "did the athlete hit 10×10 swings with the target bell in 5 min?" It IS "did the hike-pass quality improve across the 4-week block?"

## Quote anchor

- Dr. Ed Thomas (quoted by Pavel): the gym was a place to learn, not to work out; any workout was a by-product of the learning (paraphrased, Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*).
- "At StrongFirst, we view strength, endurance, power, and other athletic qualities as skills." Training is therefore approached as practice, not a workout (Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*).
- Pavel's instruction: set aside 20–30 minutes and rehearse the way you'd practise a musical instrument — fully absorbed and committed to getting better, rather than doing time and ticking off reps (paraphrased, Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*).
- "One of the meanings of the verb 'to work out' is 'to exhaust by extraction.'" (Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*.)
