# HR governs easy; pace governs hard

Fitzgerald's most actionable monitoring rule: **use heart rate to hold yourself back on easy days; use pace to push yourself on hard days; use perceived effort to start segments and to override both when something is off.**

## The asymmetry

- **HR ceilings are respected.** Runners don't chase higher HR numbers — there's no glory in a bigger BPM. Given a Z2 cap (e.g. "stay under 150 bpm"), runners comply.
- **Pace ceilings are violated.** Pace is a performance number. Runners chase faster splits even when the plan says easy. Given an easy-day pace cap (e.g. "stay slower than 5:30/km"), most will sneak under it within a few weeks.

So: enforce the *upper* boundary of easy with HR (the metric that's not motivating), and enforce the *lower* boundary of hard with pace (the metric that *is* motivating).

## The metric matrix (Fitzgerald Ch 6, Table 6.3)

| Zone | Primary metric | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Z1 (recovery)** | HR | Pace is irrelevant; HR keeps it genuinely easy |
| **Z2 (easy / long)** | **HR** | Prevents drift into the [black hole](black-hole-of-training-intensity.md) |
| **Z3 (threshold)** | HR + pace | HR keeps it controlled; pace keeps it productive |
| **Z4 (VO2max, 2–8 min)** | **Pace** | Cardiac lag breaks HR for short reps; pace responds immediately |
| **Z5 (speed, 30–90 s)** | **Pace** + RPE | HR can't track that fast; pace + form/effort do |

**Override:** RPE always has the final say. If today is a flat day (poor sleep, residual fatigue), slow down to keep RPE in zone — don't force pace or HR targets.

## Cardiac lag

When pace jumps, HR takes 30+ seconds to catch up. For 30-second to 2-minute intervals, HR never reaches the target zone even if you're running hard enough. Pace + RPE are the only honest signals for short intervals.

## Practical translation

- Easy days: HR cap at the top of Z2, set from a measured LTHR (record it in [profile.md](../profile.md); zones in [MOC-paces-zones](../MOCs/MOC-paces-zones.md))
- Long runs: same Z2 cap, plus terminal long-run drift allowance (HR climbs even at fixed pace — accept up to ~5–8 bpm drift, slow down if more)
- Sub-threshold (LT1) sessions: HR target + RPE check
- Threshold (LT2): pace target + HR cross-check
- VO2max (3–5 min reps): pace target, RPE backup, ignore HR until last 30 s of each rep
- Hill reps / strides: RPE + form only — HR is meaningless on short uphill efforts
