# Sufficient protein: ~1 g per lb body weight (~2 g/kg)

Per Matthews Ch 12, 14. Protein adequacy is the most common dietary mistake in natural lifting. Hit the protein target; everything else has more wiggle room.

## The numbers (Matthews' framework)

| Goal | Protein (g/lb BW) | g/kg | Hypothetical 80 kg athlete |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulking | 1.0 | 2.2 | ~175 g/day |
| Maintenance | 1.0 | 2.2 | ~175 g/day |
| Cutting | 1.2 | 2.6 | ~210 g/day |

Matthews bumps protein during a cut because energy deficit primes the body for muscle loss; extra protein protects lean mass.

## Distribution across the day

- **4-6 meals spread 3-4 h apart** is Matthews' preference
- Each meal: **30-40 g protein**
- Pre- and post-workout meals get 30-40 g protein specifically
- Whey protein powder is the convenience tool (not a "muscle builder" — just a fast protein source); casein for pre-bed

## Why this much

- Builds and repairs muscle damage from training
- Higher thermic effect than carbs or fat (~25% of protein calories burned in digestion)
- Most satiating macro — easier to stay in a calorie deficit
- Protects lean mass when in a deficit (the key cutting advantage)
- Research consensus: 1.6-2.2 g/kg is the upper end of meaningful benefit; more is just expensive urine

## Where this fits with running

Running adds extra protein demand on top of lifting because:
- Marathon training creates muscle damage in legs (eccentric loading downhill, late-stage long runs)
- Aerobic exercise oxidises some amino acids for fuel during long efforts (BCAA loss)
- Recovery from long runs requires protein synthesis

**In a marathon block:** ~1.6 g/kg is a conservative entry point (record the athlete's own target in [profile.md](../profile.md)) — protective but lower than Matthews' lifting-only number. If recovery is poor or weight loss too rapid, bump to 1.8-2.0 g/kg before increasing calories. For a hypothetical 80 kg runner that's ~130 g/day, rising to ~145-160 g/day.

**Off-season lifting block:** ~2.0-2.2 g/kg (~160-175 g/day for the same 80 kg runner) becomes appropriate as lifting load increases.

## Food sources that hit 30-40 g per serving easily

- Chicken breast (130 g cooked = ~40 g)
- Lean beef / ground turkey (130 g cooked = ~35 g)
- Salmon or other fatty fish (150 g cooked = ~35 g)
- Eggs (3 whole + 2 whites = ~30 g)
- Greek yogurt 0% (200 g = ~20 g, pair with extra)
- Cottage cheese (200 g = ~25 g)
- Whey protein powder (30 g scoop = ~24-27 g)
- Casein protein powder (30 g scoop = ~24 g)
- Tofu (150 g = ~20 g)
- Lentils (200 g cooked = ~18 g, pair with extra)

## The 80% rule on the rest

Matthews' flexible-dieting principle: get 80-90% of calories from nutrient-dense whole foods, leave 10-20% for things you actually want (chocolate, pasta, dessert). Once protein is hit, carbs and fat are negotiable to hit calorie target.
