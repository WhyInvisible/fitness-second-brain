# Concentric ≈ isometric ≈ eccentric (the equivalence formula)

Low's practical conversion for programming bodyweight strength:

> **1 concentric rep ≈ 2 s of isometric hold ≈ 3 s of eccentric.**

This lets you swap exercise types and stay in the right volume range for the goal attribute (strength, hypertrophy, endurance).

## Force production hierarchy

For any movement, force capacity ranks:

> **eccentric > isometric > concentric** (roughly 120–150 % > 100–120 % > 100 %)

This is why you can hold a tuck front lever before you can pull into it, and why you can do a slow muscle-up negative before you can do a concentric muscle-up.

## The practical lookup

For a workout volume of 25–50 reps for strength (or 40–75 for hypertrophy), pick from:

| Exercise type | Rep / time | Example for 30-rep equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Concentric | 1 rep | 30 reps |
| Isometric | 2 s | 60 s total hold time |
| Eccentric | 3 s | 90 s total eccentric time (e.g. 3 sets × 3 reps × 10 s) |

So **5 sets of 12-s planche isometric = 60 s = ~30 reps' equivalent**, putting it in the strength range.

## The "you can do a concentric" diagnostic

> **If you can do 3 sets of 3 cluster reps of 7–10 s eccentrics with no rest between cluster reps**, you can usually do at least 1 concentric rep.

This is huge for pull-up acquisition, one-arm chin-up acquisition, muscle-up acquisition, and front lever pull acquisition. It gives you a clear "test for readiness" signal: when you hit the cluster spec cleanly, attempt the concentric.

## Programming guidelines

- **Eccentrics are CNS-expensive** — max 1–2 per routine, each in a different category (push / pull / legs / core). Don't put eccentric pull + eccentric pushup in the same session.
- **Isometrics are CNS-friendlier** — can be used more freely.
- **Beginners (Levels 1–5) should mostly avoid eccentrics**, except for working toward pull-ups or dips.
- **At Levels 6+** eccentrics become useful for breaking plateaus on movements you can't yet do concentrically.

## Isometric "sweet spot" formula

For an isometric hold (planche, front lever, L-sit):
- Find **max hold time** (1 second short of failure, NOT to failure)
- Use **60–75 % of max hold** as your set hold time (50 % if extra cautious / older / injured)
- Run enough sets to hit **15–18 total reps' equivalent**

Example: max planche hold = 10 s → 6-s × 5 sets = 30 s total = ~15 reps equivalent → in the strength sweet spot.

## Eccentric chaining (cluster) progression

Start: 2–3 sets × 2–3 reps × **3 s** eccentric × 3 min rest
Progress: increase eccentric duration first (3 → 4 → 5 → 6 → 7 → 8 s)
Then: progressively reduce rest *between cluster reps* from full rest → 0 rest

When you hit 2–3 sets × 2–3 cluster reps × 7–10 s with no rest between cluster reps → test the concentric.

## Active vs not-active now

This is **future-loaded** for an endurance athlete — most use cases (isometric planning, eccentric chaining for muscle-up acquisition, front lever progression) belong in the off-season after the A-race. During a marathon block, only the concentric formulas matter for the maintenance pull-up / dip work.
