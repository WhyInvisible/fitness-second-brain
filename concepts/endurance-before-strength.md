# Endurance precedes strength (for the back)

Per McGill Ch 2, conventional rehab gets the order wrong. "There exists a widespread misconception amongst clinicians that strength comes before endurance but this is backwards: endurance needs to be acquired first, strength second." (McGill, *Back Mechanic*, Ch 2)

## The mechanism

Injury happens at the moment perfect form fails — when fatigue accumulates enough that the brain stops maintaining the spine-sparing pattern. A strong but un-enduring back is a Ferrari engine in a rusted chassis: the engine generates more force than the chassis can transmit. Result: the spine takes the load that the trained muscles should have. Re-injury.

To protect the back, the system has to maintain perfect form across THOUSANDS of repetitions per day (every step you take, every chair you rise from, every reach into a cupboard). That's an endurance problem, not a strength problem.

## Practical consequence

McGill's [Big 3](mcgill-big-3.md) use 8–10 second isometric holds with the Russian Descending Pyramid (6/4/2 reps) because:
- 8–10 s is long enough to demand sustained contraction (endurance) but short enough to prevent muscle cramping (which trashes form).
- Descending pyramid keeps total volume up while letting the muscle reset O₂/acid balance between sets.
- Progression is "add reps, NOT time" — so endurance keeps building while peak fatigue stays controlled.

## Where strength enters

After endurance capacity is established (months of daily Big 3, daily walking, daily spine hygiene), THEN loaded patterns can be reintroduced — push, pull, lift, carry, with the [bar-speed-rule](bar-speed-rule.md) governing the entry. This is the "build" phase that comes AFTER the heal phase.

For a runner with a back-injury history, this principle gives the right order:
1. Big 3 daily (endurance for stability)
2. Carries (endurance for anti-rotation under load)
3. Bodyweight hinges and squats (pattern + endurance)
4. Light loaded hinges with bar-speed-rule (strength entry)
5. Heavier compound work (strength)
6. Power / RFD (Dicharry's pre-race block) — only when the lower-level capacity is durable.

## Why "strength-first" rehab fails

McGill's case files (the powerlifter with the split vertebra, several others) show the same pattern: rebuilt strength too fast, kept re-injuring, then a half-year of bone-callousing + pristine movement + endurance-only protocol → finally able to add strength back, and even set records. The rebuild order matters more than the magnitude of work done.
