# BLS warm-up protocol — 4 progressive sets before the first compound

Per Matthews Ch 18. The structured warm-up that primes muscles + nervous system for heavy work without inducing fatigue. Used before the FIRST heavy compound of a session; subsequent exercises (same muscle group) don't need a fresh warm-up.

## The 4-set sequence

| Set | Load | Reps | Rest | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50% of working weight | 12 (slow / smooth) | 1 min | Blood flow + groove the pattern |
| 2 | 50% | 10 (faster) | 1 min | Speed-up the pattern |
| 3 | 70% | 4 | 1 min | Acclimate to heavier load |
| 4 | 90% | 1 | 2-3 min | Final neural prime |
| Then | 80-85% (working weight) | 4-6 × 3 sets | 3-4 min | Working sets |

Example, working weight 100 kg:
- Warm-up 1: 50 kg × 12, rest 1 min
- Warm-up 2: 50 kg × 10 (faster), rest 1 min
- Warm-up 3: 70 kg × 4, rest 1 min
- Warm-up 4: 90 kg × 1, rest 2-3 min
- Working set 1: 100 kg × 6
- Working set 2: 100 kg × 5
- Working set 3: 100 kg × 4-5

## Why it works

- **Goal of warm-up = prime, not fatigue.** The 12, 10, 4, 1 rep scheme drops volume dramatically as load rises, so total work done during warm-up is minimal (~26 reps) — not enough to compromise the working sets.
- **Progressive loading rehearses the nervous system** for the final heavy weight. Set 4 at 90% × 1 means the first working set at 80-85% feels relatively light.
- **No static stretching pre-lift** — Matthews cites studies showing static stretching impairs strength + power for ~30 min after, plus injury rate increases due to muscle damage + analgesic effect.

## When you DON'T need a fresh warm-up

- Moving to a second exercise that hits the SAME muscle group (e.g., flat bench → incline bench: chest is already warm)
- Moving from a heavier to a lighter rep range on the same muscle (e.g., 4-6 work then 8-10 accessory)

## When you DO need (at least) a single light warm-up set

- Moving to a meaningfully different muscle group (chest → side raises for shoulders — the medial delts aren't sufficiently primed by chest pressing)
- Arms day: superset a biceps warm-up with a triceps warm-up to save time (10-12 reps each, 60 s rest, then heavy)

## What about general warm-up before the BLS sequence?

Matthews assumes you walk in cold and the first compound's warm-up sets ARE the general warm-up. In practice:
- **The shoulder warm-up** ([shoulder-warmup](shoulder-warmup.md)) is run pre-upper-body for shoulder health, separately from this load-progression protocol
- **Pre-leg warm-up** (glute activation, hip mobility) is run pre-leg-day, separately
- THEN the BLS 4-set protocol on the first compound

So the full sequence for a Leg Day looks like:
1. Pre-leg warm-up (~5 min): glute bridge, banded clamshell, hip airplane, etc.
2. BLS 4-set warm-up on the squat (~5-7 min)
3. Working sets on the squat
4. Next compound (e.g., RDL): single warm-up set at 50% × 10
5. Working sets on RDL
6. ... etc.
