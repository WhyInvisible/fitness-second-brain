# Pop and float — the universal ballistic cue

Per Pavel Pt I ("The Swing"), credited to Brett Jones: at the top of every ballistic lift, the implement should **go momentarily weightless** ("pop") then **hover in the air for an instant** before gravity reclaims it ("float"). The athlete generates a discrete impulse of hip power that sends the implement to its apex, then deliberately *relaxes* during the float. Do NOT push, do NOT carry, do NOT muscle the implement to the top.

## The mechanical signal

If your two-arm KB swing has true pop-and-float, the bell:
- Rises to between stomach and shoulder height **without conscious arm work**
- Is momentarily motionless at the top (a forensic film would show 2–3 frames of zero velocity)
- Is followed by your glutes still cramped + abs braced — the *body* is still locked at the moment the bell starts to fall

If you do NOT have pop-and-float, the bell:
- Travels in a continuous arc with no apex (you're guiding it)
- Or you're tensing your arms to fling it higher (the hips ran out at chest height)
- Or your glutes release before the bell stops rising (premature reset)

## Why it matters

1. **It's the marker of pure hip drive.** A swing without pop-and-float is a swing where the arms / lower back / shoulders are leaking effort the hips should have produced.
2. **It's the only rest you get in a 5-min swing test.** Pavel: "Enjoy the float on the top; it is the only rest you are going to get."
3. **It enforces the hardstyle alternation** ([hardstyle-tension-relaxation](hardstyle-tension-relaxation.md)) — without a float, you have no relaxation phase, just continuous medium-tension grinding.
4. **It generalises to every ballistic lift.** The KB snatch, the clean, the broad jump, the push press, the kettlebell juggle all rely on the same impulse-then-float pattern.

## The teaching drill (hand-to-hand swing)

The H2H swing is not in the S&S programme as a worked exercise. It exists *only* as a teaching drill for pop-and-float:
- At the top of each swing, release the bell mid-air
- Pluck it out of the air with the other hand
- If you've failed to pop-and-float, the bell will surge forward (centrifugal force) too far to catch — and you must abort, let the bell fall, "save your back to swing another day"
- Success teaches "tame the arc" (Rob Lawrence): shrug the working shoulder back (not up — "like starting a lawnmower"), bringing the bell closer to your centreline

## "Tame the arc" — the companion cue

The bell wants to fly away from the body at the apex (centrifugal). Tame it by:
- Engaging the lat to pull the working shoulder *back* into its socket
- Squeezing the working forearm against the inner thigh on the next hike-pass
- NOT pulling with the biceps

Failure to tame the arc = a swing that pulls the lower back, that requires forward reach to catch, that escalates injury risk over time.

## Where it applies

- **Kettlebell swing** (every variant): always.
- **Kettlebell snatch**: float at the top + punch through into lockout.
- **Kettlebell clean**: float at the top + catch in the rack position.
- **Push press / jerk** (barbell or KB): hip-drive impulse + relax to receive the lockout.
- **Broad jump**: hip-drive impulse + float over distance + soft land.
- **Standing vertical jump**: same pattern, vertical axis.
- **Plyometric box jumps**: same — the [plyometrics-ground-time-not-jump-height](plyometrics-ground-time-not-jump-height.md) rule from Dicharry rhymes with this — short, sharp impulse, then float.
- **Sprint stride**: each foot contact is a pop, the flight phase is the float.

## Application

For someone with a back history (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles), pop-and-float is the **safety mechanism** of the kettlebell swing. Without it, the swing becomes a series of small lower-back muscle pulls trying to lift the bell those last few inches. With it, the spine never has to generate force — the hips do all the work, the spine just transmits.

The float is also where the BREATH happens. Sharp nasal inhale during float (the pavel-simple-sinister "fractional breathing" — two sharp inhales as the bell descends, "nostrils stick together like a nose job"). No float = no breath cycle = lungs lock = bracing fails = back at risk.

## Quote anchor

Pavel's instruction, paraphrased: let the bell hang weightless for a moment at the top, and only once it starts to drop, steer it back between the legs with the lats (Pavel, *Kettlebell Simple & Sinister*, Pt I, The Swing).

> "Enjoy the float on the top; it is the only rest you are going to get." — Pavel, *Kettlebell Simple & Sinister*, Pt I
>
> "Pop and float." — Brett Jones
