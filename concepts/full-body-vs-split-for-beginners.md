# Full-body 3×/wk beats push/pull/legs split for beginners

The math is unambiguous: **a full-body 3×/wk template hits each skill ~50 % more frequently than a push/pull split with the same number of weekly sessions.**

## The math

- Push/pull split, 4 sessions/wk: each skill trained **~2× per week** = ~100 sessions per skill per year
- Full body, 3 sessions/wk: each skill trained **3× per week** = ~150 sessions per skill per year
- **Difference: 50 % more reps per skill per year.** Over a year, a full-body trainee is ~6 months ahead of a split trainee at the same skill.

## Why it works

- Strength is partly a skill. Neural adaptations (recruitment, synchronisation, rate coding, contribution) are movement-specific. More repetitions of the specific movement = faster acquisition.
- Beginners haven't yet accumulated the volume that would make 3× full-body recovery-impossible. Once you're advanced enough to need 30+ working sets per session, full-body breaks down.
- Full-body 3×/wk plus 1 rest day between sessions (M/W/F or Tue/Thu/Sat) gives 48 h between same-pattern hits — adequate for beginner-to-intermediate recovery.

## When to switch

- **Levels 1–5 (Beginner):** full-body always.
- **Levels 6–9 (Intermediate):** still mostly full-body. Can add a 4th session per year of consistent training (so 4 sessions/wk after ~1 year intermediate, 5 after ~2 years).
- **Levels 10+ (Advanced):** split routines become useful — especially straight-arm vs bent-arm splits (planche / front lever / back lever / cross on one day; pushups / pull-ups / dips on another). Reason: straight-arm work is connective-tissue expensive and needs separate recovery from bent-arm.

## The full-body beginner template (Low's recommendation)

3×/wk M/W/F or Tue/Thu/Sat. Each session:

1. **Warm-up** (15–20 min) — blood flow + mobility + positional drills
2. **Skill work** (5–10 min) — handstand
3. **Strength work** (30 min)
   - 2 pushing (e.g. dips + pushups, or HSPU progression + dips)
   - 2 pulling (e.g. pull-ups + ring rows, or front lever + rows)
   - 2 legs (e.g. pistol progression + step-ups, or squats + lunges)
   - L-sit at end (60 s total across sets, not to failure)
4. **Prehab + cool-down** (10 min)

Rep scheme typically 3 × 5–12 with 3 min rest between sets.

## Application

For an athlete starting a calisthenics-primary block (e.g. the off-season after the A-race): this is the template. Full-body 3×/wk for at least the first 12 months. Splits later if/when they reach Intermediate.

## Active when?

When calisthenics is a primary goal. During a marathon build, calisthenics frequency drops to 1–2×/wk maintenance — too low to be a "full body" routine in Low's sense; it's just maintenance volume.
