# Anti-glycolytic training (AGT)

Per Pavel Pt II ("A Lesson from Workers and Peasants", "Lift No Faster Than You Can Talk"), AGT is a strength-conditioning model that **deliberately avoids the glycolytic (sugar-burning, lactate-producing) energy pathway** in favour of the creatine-phosphate (CP) system fuelled by aerobic recovery between sets. Developed by Soviet researcher Yuri Verkhoshansky; popularised in the West by Pavel and the StrongFirst coaches.

## The three energy systems (refresher)

| System | Fuel | Duration | Output | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ATP-CP (phosphagen)** | Creatine phosphate | 0–10 sec | Highest power | Fast (clean burn) |
| **Anaerobic glycolysis** | Sugar (glucose, glycogen) | 10 sec–2 min | Medium power | Slow (lactate clearance) |
| **Aerobic** | Glucose + fat | >2 min | Lowest power | Sustainable indefinitely |

AGT's wager: **stay in the CP system for the work intervals + use the aerobic system to refill CP between sets**. Never let glycolysis dominate.

## Why avoid glycolysis

- **Glycolysis is 1.5–2× weaker than the CP system** (Pavel, citing Verkhoshansky). Chasing the burn destroys power output.
- **Lactic acid pollutes the body** — accumulates in tissue, drives free radical production, disrupts hormonal balance (cortisol), takes hours to clear.
- **Pro-inflammatory** — chronic glycolytic emphasis (HIIT-as-religion) drives systemic inflammation that conflicts with marathon adaptation, joint recovery, and immune function.
- **Slows recovery** — glycolytic sessions take 24–48 hours to truly recover from. AGT sessions can be repeated daily.

## The execution rules

1. **Low reps per set** (5–10) — keeps work intervals inside the CP window.
2. **Generous rest between sets** — dictated by the [talk-test-between-sets](talk-test-between-sets.md) rule: rest until you can speak in short sentences. Typically 1.5–3 min, sometimes longer.
3. **Max power on each rep** — anti-glycolytic does not mean anti-intensity. Each rep is 90% effort, fully explosive. The intensity-rest ratio is what matters.
4. **Moderate-to-heavy load** — heavy enough to recruit fast fibres, light enough to maintain quality + speed across all sets.
5. **Daily or near-daily frequency** — because each session deposits energy rather than extracting it.

## The "rule of 100" alignment

Pavel's S&S volume cap (100 reps per exercise per session) is exactly tuned to keep total work inside the CP-aerobic envelope. >100 reps = glycolysis dominates = strength gains arrest (Vorobyev, cited p. 122).

## Where this overlaps with running

The aerobic/lactate-threshold polarisation in [80-20-rule](80-20-rule.md) and [hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard](hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard.md) is the *same principle applied to endurance*. Both Pavel and Fitzgerald cite Stephen Seiler's research showing elite endurance athletes train ~80% below lactate threshold. Pavel's swing AGT is the strength-domain version: most training avoids the lactate flood, with rare (1×/month) "die but do" max-glycolytic peaking sessions.

## What this is NOT

- **NOT "go light"** — power per rep stays high.
- **NOT "rest as long as you want"** — rest is dictated by talk test, not preference.
- **NOT anti-conditioning** — done daily for months it builds remarkable work capacity, just by the aerobic-recovery side route.
- **NOT incompatible with all glycolytic work** — Pavel allows once-a-week timed-test sessions ("Fridays") that breach the lactate threshold deliberately. AGT is the **majority**, not the only mode.

## Application

This is exactly the model that lets a marathon athlete add kettlebell strength without competing with the running. Marathon training is 80% aerobic; AGT swings are CP + aerobic recovery; the two stack cleanly because neither dumps lactate that the other has to clear. The opposite (HIIT-style "metcons" of kettlebells) would shred marathon recovery within a week.

Practical: when programming swings on a hard-running-day, the rest period between swing sets should be **longer than usual** because aerobic-system bandwidth is already taxed. Trust the talk test — let it stretch to 3+ minutes if needed.

## Quote anchor (paraphrased)

- Pavel casts glycolysis as the fuel behind fashionable HIIT and "metcons": an inefficient pathway that, tapped too often, leaves lactic acid, ammonia and free radicals behind and disturbs hormonal balance. (Pavel, *Kettlebell Simple & Sinister*, Pt II, "A Lesson from Workers and Peasants", pp. 114–115)
- Anti-glycolytic training instead powers hard efforts with creatine phosphate, which Pavel calls clean-burning "rocket fuel", and lets the aerobic system refill it between sets. (Pavel, *Kettlebell Simple & Sinister*, Pt II, pp. 114–115)
