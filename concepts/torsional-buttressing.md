# Torsional buttressing (anti-rotation, McGill-style for athletes)

Per McGill UBF Ch 13 (pp. 262-264), the safest way to train torsional (twisting) strength for athletes — with the spine kept LOCKED in neutral and the actual twisting motion occurring at the extremities, NOT in the spine itself.

## The core principle: twist at the extremities, NEVER at the spine

The spine generates ~4× the compressive load per equal torque about the twist axis vs the flex/extend axis. **Generating torque AT the spine is the highest-injury-risk loading mode in the body.** Therefore:

> When you need to generate or resist a twisting force, the SPINE STAYS NEUTRAL + BRACED, and the rotation happens at the hips, shoulders, and the implement.

This is the "torsional buttressing" position: the torso buttresses (resists) the torsional torque without itself twisting. The lats + obliques + abs + QL + erectors all co-contract as the torsional guy-wires. The motion is generated by the arms reaching, the hips rotating, or the implement moving — never by lumbar twist.

## The 3-plane training order (UBF Ch 13)

Spine loading magnitude differs by plane. Train in this order, mastering each before progressing:

1. **Sagittal plane** (flex/extend) — lowest spine load. Push-ups, hip-hinges, deadlifts, bird-dogs, curl-ups.
2. **Frontal plane** (side bend / lateral flexion) — moderate load. Side bridges, suitcase carries.
3. **Torsional / axial twist** — **~4× the load** of sagittal. Only after frontal is mastered. Even then: spine LOCKED.

Most gym programs invert this — Russian twists and seated torsion machines are common entry exercises. UBF: these are LAST, if at all.

## The McGill torsional progression (Ch 13)

**1. One-arm weight raise (UBF Fig 13.48) — the safest entry.**
- Sit/stand with one arm supporting upper body weight (e.g. on a bench, knee, or wall).
- Other hand holds a weight.
- Raise the weight — the asymmetric load creates a torsional torque the spine must buttress isometrically.
- Spine stays neutral, abdominals braced.
- Low spine load, high torsional buttressing demand.

**2. Static cable hold (Pallof press hold).**
- Stand perpendicular to a cable column.
- Hold the cable handle at sternum, arms extended.
- The cable wants to rotate your torso toward it; you resist isometrically.
- Vary the hand-to-torso distance to vary the moment arm (closer = lower load; further = higher load).
- 8-10 s holds, 3-4 reps/side.

**3. Lateral cable walkout.**
- From the static hold, step LATERALLY away from the cable column.
- Each step increases the torsional torque to resist.
- 6-8 steps out, then 6-8 back.

**4. Pallof press (dynamic).**
- From static hold position, press arms straight out away from body, hold 2 s, return.
- Spine stays neutral throughout.
- 8-10 reps/side.

**5. Cable woodchop (high-to-low + low-to-high).**
- Stand perpendicular to cable column.
- High-to-low: cable starts overhead, "chop" down and across.
- Low-to-high: cable starts at hip, lift up and across.
- **MOTION IS AT THE HIPS AND SHOULDERS. Spine stays locked.** The "twist" is the arms swinging the cable through an arc + the hips rotating.
- 6-10 reps/side.

**6. High pulls / low pulls.** Variations of the woodchop with the cable starting at different heights.

## What UBF explicitly warns against

- **Seated torsional machines** — "very high spine loads throughout the range of motion." Reserve only for elite athletes whose sport demands it (and even then, McGill had a world-class discus thrower remove twisting work, and the thrower's back recovered).
- **Russian twist** — explicitly banned in [back-safe-exercise-contraindications](back-safe-exercise-contraindications.md) for back-history athletes.
- **"Washing machine" + ball-twist-in-sac drills** — extremely high torsional + compressive spine loads. "We discourage this type of training except for rare cases."

## When to train torsional buttressing

**Prerequisites (per UBF):**
- Sagittal-plane core mastered ([mcgill-big-3](mcgill-big-3.md) advanced forms automatic).
- Frontal-plane core mastered (side bridge rolling progression + carries).
- No back symptoms for ≥3 months.

**Application (athlete with back history, in season):**
- Start now: the one-arm weight raise + Pallof press hold (steps 1-2) are safe entry points as part of core work. They are essentially anti-rotation drills.
- Not yet: dynamic woodchops + high/low pulls. Reserve them for the off-season, once foundational stability is more developed and the prerequisites above are met (check [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles for current back status).
- Dose: add the Pallof press hold 2-3 sets × 8-10 s/side as a finisher to the existing core circuit. Replaces or supplements the bird-dog explosive variant.
