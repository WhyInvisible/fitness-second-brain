# Big 3 advanced progressions (Stage 2 → performance)

Per McGill UBF Ch 11. The Big 3 ([curl-up, side bridge, bird-dog](mcgill-big-3.md)) as introduced in Back Mechanic is the **rehab** form. UBF Ch 11 lays out the **performance** progressions — the next 5-10 years of evolution beyond the basic form. Each progression should be earned, not rushed.

## Why progress beyond the basic Big 3

The basic Big 3 builds spine stability sufficient for daily life + back-safety. It does NOT, by itself, prepare the back for athletic loading or performance demands. UBF Ch 11:

> "Several pro teams and many individual high-performance athletes begin their team workouts with a stabilization routine."

The advanced forms train the SAME muscles + patterns BUT under:
- Higher motor-control challenge
- Higher cognitive load (breathing while bracing)
- Higher dynamic challenge
- Higher transfer to plyometric / explosive work

Once the athlete has done the basic Big 3 daily for several weeks and the basic form is essentially automatic, it's time to layer in the advanced forms (see the gates below).

## Curl-up — the progression ladder

### Beginner (basic — the Back Mechanic form)
- Hands palms-down under lumbar.
- Elbows on floor.
- Lift head + shoulders ~1 inch. Head barely lifts.
- 8-10 s holds × Russian Pyramid 6/4/2.

### Intermediate
- **Pre-brace** the entire abdominal wall MAXIMALLY before each rep. Wall is rigid before any lift attempt.
- **Lift elbows just off the floor** (removes the prying advantage; more load shifts to rectus + abs).
- Still don't lift head higher.

### Advanced (UBF Ch 11, p. 215)
- Pre-brace + elbows off floor.
- **Curl SLIGHTLY against the heavy pre-brace.** The challenge is in the brace, NOT in lifting higher.
- Hands optionally beside head (NEVER behind — no pulling on head), as long as lumbar posture stays neutral.
- 8-10 s holds × 6/4/2.

### Ultimate (UBF Ch 11, p. 216)
- All of advanced, plus:
- **Deep breathing throughout the brace.** Maintain max bracing while taking 2-3 deep breaths per rep. Trains the breathing-under-bracing skill critical for athletic performance.
- 8-10 s holds × 6/4/2.

### Neck trick (any level — UBF p. 214)
For curl-up neck discomfort: place TONGUE on roof of mouth, push hard upward, teeth lightly together. Activates digastric muscles, reduces SCM dominance, eliminates 80% of curl-up neck pain.

## Side bridge — the progression ladder

### Beginner (basic)
- Knees bent, hip-hinge into bridge.
- 8-10 s holds × 6/4/2 per side.

### Intermediate
- Straight legs, top foot in front of bottom foot.
- Full body in straight line.
- Top hand on opposite shoulder (relieves shoulder pressure).
- 8-10 s holds × 6/4/2 per side.

### Advanced (UBF Ch 11, p. 219)
- **Longitudinal rolling.** With top foot in front of bottom foot, roll about the shoulder — anterior to posterior to anterior. Captures different neuromuscular compartments in the obliques.

### Performance (UBF Ch 11, p. 219-220) — "The Roll"
- Transfer support from one elbow to the other through a front plank.
- Hold each position (side-front-side) ≥10 s with deep breaths.
- **Pelvis must NOT lead the ribcage** — pelvis + ribcage move as ONE unit. (When pelvis leads, the difficulty drops by half and the spine bears un-stabilised load.)
- Higher levels: feet on labile surface (Bosu, foam).

### Ultimate — Side bridge under heavy breathing (UBF Ch 12, p. 228)
**Specifically running-relevant.**
- Ride stationary bike or do burpees 30 s to elevate ventilation rate.
- Drop into highest side-bridge variant.
- Hold while breathing heavily — diaphragm must function independently of abdominal-wall bracing.
- If the wall relaxes during inhale, you fall out of the brace.
- This is the diaphragm-under-stiffness skill directly relevant to maintaining torso stiffness during a fatigued marathon.

## Bird-dog — the progression ladder

### Beginner (basic)
- All fours, hands under shoulders, knees under hips, spine sweet spot.
- Extend opposite arm + leg to horizontal. Hold 8-10 s.
- Sweep hand + knee along floor between holds.

### Intermediate (UBF Ch 11, p. 222)
- **Sweep the floor:** between holds, sweep hand + knee along floor WITHOUT bearing weight.
- **Draw squares:** trace a square with hand + foot (out, down, across, up). Motor control challenge.

### Advanced
- **Wrist + ankle weights** during the draw-squares variant. Adds load while maintaining motor pattern.
- **Upper-back contraction enhancement (UBF Ch 11, p. 223):** clench fist, laterally abduct the arm slightly, focus on extension in this new plane. Engages rhomboids + lat dorsi + lower trapezius more fully.

### Performance — explosive bird-dog (UBF Ch 11, p. 224)
- Same setup. Pre-brace.
- **"Explode" the arm and leg up to horizontal** with maximum velocity intent.
- Impeccable form: NO spine motion. Motion only at the hip and shoulder.
- Teaches hip extensors to explode with the torso stiff — **the precursor to plyometric training** for any sport requiring jumps, leaps, or directional changes.

### Stir-the-pot (UBF Ch 13, p. 246) — swiss-ball bird-dog
- Elbows on swiss ball in plank position.
- "Stir" the ball in slow circles by rotating at the shoulders.
- Spine LOCKED, abdominally pre-braced, motion ONLY at shoulders.
- Trains the entire anterior chain with torsional control under unstable surface.
- McGill: "the ultimate exercise for the ultimate fighter."
- See stir-the-pot.md.

## When to progress (the gating criteria)

UBF doesn't give a strict timeline. The gates:
1. **Current form is automatic** — you can do the basic Big 3 without thinking about form.
2. **No back symptoms for ≥4 weeks** at the current level.
3. **The breathing test:** can you maintain the current level's bracing while breathing normally? If yes, ready to add the next layer.
4. **The advanced curl-up's "ultimate" form** requires owning at least 4-6 weeks of advanced form first.
5. **The explosive bird-dog** requires owning sweep + squares with NO spine motion first.

## Practical translation: a 12-week curriculum

For an athlete who has been doing the basic Big 3 for several weeks and passes the gates above, a suggested progression:

**Weeks 1-2:** Add pre-brace to curl-up (intermediate). Continue rest at basic.
**Weeks 3-4:** Add elbows-off-floor curl-up (advanced). Add rolling side bridge (advanced).
**Weeks 5-6:** Add deep-breathing curl-up (ultimate). Side-bridge rolling continues. Bird-dog: add sweep + draw squares (intermediate).
**Weeks 7-8:** Maintain advanced curl-up. Start side-bridge-under-heavy-breathing once a week (after a hard run, to train the diaphragm-under-stiffness specifically).
**Weeks 9-10:** Add explosive bird-dog 1-2 reps/side at the END of the Big 3 (don't replace the static bird-dog). Add stir-the-pot if a swiss ball is available.
**Weeks 11-12:** Full Big 3 advanced + 1× stir-the-pot/week + side-bridge-under-breathing 1×/week.

Total time still ~10-12 min/day. The advanced forms aren't longer — they're harder.
