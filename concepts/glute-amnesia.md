# Glute amnesia (the crossed-pelvis syndrome)

Per McGill UBF Ch 4 (pp. 105-106) + Ch 10 (pp. 188-192), refining a pattern originally described by Vladimir Janda. The dominant hidden pattern in athletes with chronic back-pain history: **the glutes are functionally silent during hip extension**, and the back-extensors + hamstrings do the job instead. The glutes "have forgotten how to fire."

## The pattern

When asked to extend the hip (squat-up, deadlift, bridge, climb stairs):
- **Healthy:** glute max + glute med fire FIRST. Hamstrings assist. Erector spinae stabilises the spine, doesn't drive the motion.
- **Crossed-pelvis / glute-amnesia:** hamstrings fire first, erector spinae cranks to extend the spine, glutes barely activate.

Result: **erector spinae crushes the lumbar spine** with each hip extension. Hamstrings overwork (chronic tightness). Glutes atrophy functionally even when they look fine. Lower back fatigue, frequent twinges, hamstring asymmetry, slow strength rehab — the classic ex-back-pain athlete profile.

## Why it matters for back-injured athletes

McGill: "A strength and power athlete will continue to be compromised with chronic back troubles until these motor patterns are addressed." Adding more squat or deadlift load to a glute-amnesiac spine just keeps re-injuring it. The pattern must be RE-WIRED at the motor level before any loaded strength work.

## Who to screen first

Suspect glute amnesia when the athlete's injury history in [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles shows a combination like:
- A past back injury from a heavy hinge (e.g. a maximal deadlift) — almost always a "hamstrings + back drive the lift, glutes don't" pattern.
- **One hamstring chronically tighter than the other** — a classic crossed-pelvis symptom (the hamstring is COMPENSATING for an under-firing glute on that side).
- Current strength work that loads hip extension (trap-bar deadlift, single-leg deadlift, hip thrust) — this may be reinforcing the dysfunctional pattern, not fixing it, unless glute activation is verified each session.

## Diagnostic tests

**Test 1: Bridge with palpation.** Lie supine, knees bent, feet on floor. Palpate the hamstrings near the sit-bones with both hands. Bridge slowly. If hamstrings cramp / fire HARD before the glutes do, that's glute amnesia.

**Test 2: Clam-shell endurance.** Side-lying, thumb on ASIS, fingers reach posteriorly to palpate glute med (UBF Fig 10.19). Knees + hips bent 90°, feet stay together as hinge. Open the knees clam-shell. **Healthy glute med:** can do 20+ slow reps without cramping. **Glute amnesia:** cramps in 4-5 reps (the gymnast case study, UBF p. 304).

**Test 3: Single-leg squat watch.** Single-leg squat in front of a mirror. Knee tracks in, hip drops on the unsupported side, torso leans away from supporting leg = glute med is asleep.

## The re-patterning protocol (UBF Ch 10)

Do BEFORE any loaded hip-extension work, until the pattern is automatic. ~5-10 minutes pre-leg-day.

**1. Glute med wake-up (clam-shell with palpation).**
- Side-lying, thumb on ASIS, fingers on glute med.
- Knees 90°, feet together as hinge.
- Spread the knees (clam-shell). **Feel the glute med fire** under your fingers.
- 8-10 slow reps per side. No resistance — this is a wake-up, not strength work.
- Progression: lateral leg raise with same palpation, then ankle weight.

**2. Glute max wake-up (coin squeeze).**
- Lie supine, knees bent, feet flat.
- Palpate glute max.
- "Squeeze a coin in the gluteal fold" — activate glute max WITHOUT creating hip extension. Pelvis stays still.
- 5-8 slow reps. Feel the contraction in the glutes only, NOT hamstrings.

**3. Glute-only bridge.**
- Same supine position.
- Activate glute max FIRST. Then bridge up by squeezing harder.
- **Palpate hamstrings** — they should stay relatively quiet. If they fire first, regress to step 2.
- 8-10 reps. Pelvis rises ONLY via glute squeeze, NOT hamstring drive.

**4. Reciprocal-inhibition cue (for stubborn cases).**
- Coach (or self) places foot against athlete's toes during the bridge.
- Athlete mildly pushes toes into the resistance — this creates knee-extension torque (quad fires), which reciprocally inhibits the hamstrings.
- With hamstrings damped, glute max takes over the bridge.
- Then drop the cue and retest pure glute-only bridge.

**5. One-legged bridge.** Once the pattern is established, progress to one-leg.

**6. Carry the pattern into the squat.** Westside cueing: "spread the floor with the feet" (image hip abduction + external rotation throughout the squat). This integrates the now-awake glutes into the loaded pattern.

## Pre-leg-day routine (application)

For an athlete who screens positive, until the pattern is bulletproof, run this BEFORE any session involving trap-bar deadlift, single-leg deadlift, hip thrust, squat, or RDL:

1. Clam-shell with palpation × 10/side
2. Glute coin-squeeze × 8
3. Glute-only bridge × 10
4. One-leg bridge × 6/side (when ready)
5. Goblet squat or wall squat × 5 with "spread the floor" cue
6. THEN start the working sets.

Total: ~5-7 minutes. Folds neatly into the existing pre-leg-day warmup.

## Why this is NOT just "do glute exercises"

The fix is **neurological**, not strength-based. A glute-amnesiac athlete can leg-press 200 kg and still have dead glutes during a deadlift, because the motor pattern routes around them. You must wake the muscle up with palpation + isolation FIRST, then practice the integrated pattern with cues that prevent the old (hamstring + erector spinae) pattern from re-asserting itself.

This is why adding more "glute work" to a glute-amnesiac program doesn't fix anything. The pattern is the problem.

## Cross-source consistency

- Dicharry: glute max = primary hip external rotator, not glute med; lower-cross syndrome (the kinematic equivalent of crossed-pelvis) afflicts 80% of runners. Dicharry's pigeon hip extension + frog bridge + chair-of-death attack the same pattern from the mobility side.
- Pavel: the hardstyle kettlebell swing measured 100% glute MVC in McGill's lab — Pavel's hike-pass + hardstyle tension is one of the best glute-pattern integrators available. Once the amnesia is broken, the swing reinforces the new pattern.
- **Synthesis:** McGill diagnoses + re-patterns the neurology, Dicharry adds the mobility piece, Pavel provides the loaded reinforcement. All three agree the glutes are the missing link.
