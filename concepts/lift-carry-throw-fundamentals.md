# Lift / carry / throw — the manipulative-skill fundamentals

Per Le Corre chapters 30–31 (Manipulation Movement 1 + 2), the manipulative skills — **lifting, carrying, throwing, catching** — are the second leg of the natural-movement vocabulary (alongside locomotive skills). All four share universal principles that apply across infinite object variations.

## The four shared principles

### 1. Combined center of mass management

Holding any external load shifts your centre of gravity. The combined centre of mass (your COG + the load's COG) must stay vertically aligned with your base of support, or you fall / strain / waste energy.

- **Closer is better:** load against the body (waist, chest, shoulder) is far more stable than load held at arm's length.
- **Symmetric distribution is best:** two-hand carries, two-hand throws, balanced loads — less spine torque than asymmetric.
- **Asymmetric is sometimes mandatory:** single-hand carries, side-shoulder carries — when used, the body must tilt subtly *toward* the load to compensate, NOT against the load.

### 2. Neutral spine non-negotiable

The single most important rule across all manipulative movements — direct cross-source agreement with McGill. Lower-back hyperflexion / hyperextension / lateral hyperflexion / hyper-rotation under load = primary back-injury mechanism. The technique exists to keep the spine neutral under all loading conditions.

- **Lift through the hips, not the back.** Hip-hinge default ([hip-hinge-mechanics](hip-hinge-mechanics.md)).
- **Carry with the load aligned, not pulling the spine sideways.** Active counter-tilt + abdominal brace.
- **Throw with rotational power from hips → torso → shoulder → arm, with the spine neutral throughout.** Never twist under load.

### 3. Bracing before contact

Per Pavel + McGill + Le Corre triple-agreement: tension in the abdominal wall, lats, glutes BEFORE the load is lifted / received / thrown. Reactive bracing is too slow — by the time you sense the load, your spine is already loaded incorrectly.

- **Lift:** breath in through abdomen, brace 70–100%, lift, exhale at top.
- **Catch:** brace before grasping; eccentric deceleration through arms → hips → knees.
- **Throw:** brace through the rotational chain; the throw releases the tension into the load.

### 4. Grip is upstream of strength

You can't lift / carry / throw what you can't grip. Grip strength is correlated with overall health + life expectancy (Le Corre cites the studies). Grip strength is *broad* — different grips for different shapes (hook grip for narrow handles, open-hand grip for thick objects, pinch grip for flat surfaces, finger grip for ledges). Training grip with one apparatus (pull-up bar) develops *that* grip, not transferable grip.

## Lifting (chapter 30 summary)

- **Deadlift:** the apex pattern — ground to hip level via hip hinge. Foundation for all heavier lifting. Cross-source with McGill, Pavel, Dicharry, Matthews.
- **Lapping:** lift to thigh / lap level for odd or bulky objects too volumetric for a one-motion deadlift. Lower stress on the spine because the object rests on legs at the transition.
- **Squat Clean:** explosive lift from ground to shoulder rack — used when the object permits explosive power (good grip surface, balanced load, weight not at maximum). Higher technique demand.

**Setting (Le Corre's universal pre-lift checklist):**
1. Grasp + grip-strength check by feel
2. Body position over the load
3. Upper back + shoulder + chest open
4. Neck + eyes forward or slightly up
5. Preload (tension through posterior chain)
6. Brace (abdominal + breath-hold)
7. Lift

### Real-world dimension Le Corre adds

McGill / Pavel / Matthews mostly cover lifting in *gym* contexts (Olympic bar, kettlebell — standardised handle, balanced weight, flat floor). Le Corre extends to **odd objects on uneven terrain** — rocks, logs, packs, partners, unfamiliar loads with shifting centres of mass. Same principles, harder application. Useful for outdoor / travel / real-life capability.

## Carrying (15+ named hold variations)

The carry catalogue is the most expanded section in Le Corre vs other sources. Conventional fitness has farmer carry, suitcase carry, overhead carry, waiter carry. Le Corre adds:

| Hold | Position | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| **Waist** | Both hands at waist level, object pressed against pelvis | Initial post-deadlift hold; short distances |
| **Hip** | One hand on hip, object resting on hip | Asymmetric carry; child carry |
| **Lumbar** | Object high on lower back, hands clasped behind | Backpack alternative |
| **Chest (7+ wrap variants)** | Deep wrap, wrist grip, palm grip, cross-fingers, S-grip, side-squeeze, over-wrap, under-wrap, rack | Medium-volume objects (boxes, kegs, stones) |
| **Side shoulder** | Object on top of one shoulder, hand stabilising | Long objects (logs, planks); high-distance carries |
| **Front shoulder (rack)** | Both elbows up, object across front of shoulders | KB clean position; pre-throw setup |
| **Forehead** | Strap or band over forehead, object on upper back | Long-distance porter technique (Himalaya, Africa) |
| **Head** | Object balanced on top of head, hands free | Long distances on prepared surfaces |
| **Partner** | Two-person carries (fireman, two-arm, log-style) | Cooperative; rescue scenarios |

**Practical implication:** rotate hold variants in training. Same object weight × different hold = different load distribution + different muscular emphasis + adaptability training.

### Universal carry rule

Once an efficient hold is established, the carry should be *only legs + hips moving* — the upper body / spine stays as still as possible. Any positional disturbance → drop load, reset, restart. Don't grind a bad position over a distance.

## Throwing + catching (chapter 31)

- **Throw mechanics:** rotational power from hips → torso → shoulder → arm → release. Same chain whether throwing a stone or throwing a punch. (Same chain as the kettlebell swing — Le Corre explicitly notes the equivalence.)
- **Catch mechanics:** the reverse — receive the load at extended arms, eccentrically decelerate through arms → hips → knees. Symmetric to throw.
- **Eye-hand coordination:** the rate-limiter for both. Trains brain function in addition to physical capability.
- **Partner practice is the norm.** Le Corre emphasises cooperative throwing + catching as the natural mode (passing tools, rescue equipment, food, weapons historically).

### Front Swing = KB swing minus release

Le Corre explicitly notes the equivalence: the kettlebell swing is a *throwing-and-catching motion minus the release at the top and grasp at the bottom*. Same hip-hinge mechanics, same rotational power production, same eccentric reception. This means the kettlebell swing trains the *throw-and-catch* skill, not just "the swing." In practice: a regular KB swing habit already trains the throw-pattern's lower-body sequence; what's missing is the *release + accuracy* dimension at the top. Add medball throws or stone throws once in a while to complete the pattern.

## Practical translation

- **Lift:** a trap-bar deadlift is a sound foundation (per McGill back-safety), especially if the athlete has a lower-back history (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles). Add occasional *Lapping practice* with an odd object (sandbag, slam ball, log) — same hip-hinge mechanics, applied to an asymmetric / awkward load. Builds real-world transferability.
- **Carry:** the standard runner carries are Dicharry's (farmer, suitcase, waiter, overhead). Add **one new carry variant per month** for variety. Rotation: month 1 = chest hold (bear hug a slam ball or stone); month 2 = front shoulder rack (KB or stone); month 3 = side shoulder (log or long bag); etc. See exercises/various-carry-styles.
- **Throw:** add medball throws to power-block work (Dicharry already has medball-acceleration sprint, medball-granny-toss, medball-push-press, medball-twist, medball-triple-bound). Le Corre's contribution: the *accuracy + release-timing* emphasis vs Dicharry's *power-only* emphasis. Use the same medball, add target-throwing practice.
- **Catch:** partner-based catch practice is rare in solo training. Even brief once-a-week 5-min sessions with a partner (or wall-rebound for solo) develop reactive skills that little else in a runner's training touches.
- **Real-world test (annually, or on a trip with hills):** carry a 20–25 kg pack uphill 1–2 km on uneven ground. Easy test, hard practice. Reveals capability gaps no gym session does.

## Cross-source agreement

The lift / carry / throw chapter is the highest-density cross-source area in the corpus:

- **McGill** rules the spine-safety constraint (neutral spine non-negotiable, hip-hinge default, brace before load).
- **Pavel** rules the kettlebell + ballistic technique (hike pass, pop-and-float, fractional breathing, the swing as the apex hinge expression).
- **Dicharry** rules the carry catalogue + power-block medball work.
- **Matthews** rules the gym-based lifting progression (trap-bar deadlift, bench, overhead press).
- **Le Corre** rules the real-world application (odd objects, uneven terrain, cooperative carries, throw-and-catch as practical skills).

All five agree on the universal principles above. The disagreement is on emphasis + context, not mechanics.
