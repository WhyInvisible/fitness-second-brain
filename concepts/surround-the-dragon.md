# Surround the dragon

Per McGill UBF Ch 13 — a concept that drives the strength chapter's structure:

In paraphrase: a healthy back depends on good mechanics in the joints below it (hips, pelvis, knees) and above it (neck, shoulders), so the strength chapter deliberately includes exercises that integrate those regions — and the "surround the dragon" idea extends to every joint that feeds load into the back. (McGill, *Ultimate Back Fitness and Performance*, Ch 13, p. 231)

The back is the "dragon" — the central structure that must be protected and supported. The supporting joints are the "dragon's guards." A back-focused program that ignores hips, ankles, T-spine, shoulders, grip, and feet WILL fail, because dysfunction in any of the supporting joints loads the back to compensate.

## The list of joints to surround the dragon

In rough head-to-toe order:

| Joint/region | What to train | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Cervical (neck)** | Stability under load; tongue-on-roof activation | Forward head wastes capacity; neck instability = injury under athletic load |
| **Scapulothoracic (shoulder blades)** | Stability (packed, depressed); protract/retract/elevate/depress control | Wild scapulae = lat doesn't engage = spine takes load instead |
| **Glenohumeral (shoulder)** | Mobility; rotator cuff strength; lat engagement | Stiff shoulders force lumbar compensation in overhead work |
| **T-spine** | Extension mobility | Thoracic stiffness pushes barbells too high in squats, causes kyphosis |
| **Hips** | Mobility in all planes; glute activation (no amnesia); external rotation specifically | Stiff hips force spine to bend instead — the dominant back-injury setup |
| **Knees** | Stability (not buckling in); some flexion/extension capacity | Knee tracking issues alter foot-ground force vector → lumbar compensation |
| **Ankles** | Dorsiflexion mobility specifically; foot-grip strength | Limited ankle dorsiflexion = squat depth limited → lumbar flex compensation |
| **Feet** | Strength + intrinsic mobility; toe-grip (1st MTP, big toe) | "Grip the floor" cue requires functional feet; foot dysfunction = full-chain compensation |
| **Grip + forearms** | Strength + dexterity | Hand grip "steers" force through the upper-body linkage — UBF Ch 13 on cable work |

## The implication for programming

A back-safety / back-performance program is NEVER just back exercises. It's:
- T-spine mobility daily
- Hip mobility daily (one of: hip airplane, frog bridge, pigeon, lunge with rotation)
- Glute activation pre-leg-day (per [glute-amnesia](glute-amnesia.md))
- Ankle dorsiflexion mobility weekly (ankle-belt-mobility, wall ankle stretch)
- Foot mobility + grip drills (foot screws, single-leg balance with toe grip)
- Shoulder mobility + scap control daily for upper-body days
- Carries (grip + integration + asymmetric stability)
- THEN the back/core/lift work on top of all that

This is the difference between a back-rehab program (Big 3 alone) and a back-PERFORMANCE program (Big 3 + the dragon-surrounding integration work).

## Practical translation: audit what's in place vs gaps

**Pieces a runner's strength program often already has (tick off what the athlete does):**
- Daily Big 3 (the dragon directly).
- Hip airplane (hip mobility + glute integration).
- T-spine mobility via basketball-mobility, travolta-peanuts in warmups.
- Suitcase + farmer + waiter carries (grip + integration + QL).
- Posture-fix routine (scap + GH shoulder work).

**Common gaps:**
- **Glute activation pre-leg-day** — often missing as a named pre-lift protocol. Add it per [glute-amnesia](glute-amnesia.md).
- **Foot/intrinsic-mobility work** — foot-screws and similar drills tend to sit in the exercise library without being regularly programmed.
- **Ankle dorsiflexion** — ankle-belt-mobility, if used at all, tends to be used irregularly.
- **Pre-leg-day T-spine extension** — basketball-mobility often lives in the shoulder warmup but not the leg-day warmup (where it matters for clean overhead, clean squat racking).

## Recommendations to close the gaps

Pre-leg-day routine (15 min):
1. Cat-camel × 7-8 cycles (lumbar lubrication)
2. T-spine extension (basketball-mobility or travolta-peanuts) × 60 s
3. Hip airplane × 5/side
4. Ankle-belt mobility × 30 s/side
5. Foot screws × 20 s/foot
6. Glute amnesia routine (clam-shell + coin-squeeze + glute-only bridge) per [glute-amnesia](glute-amnesia.md)
7. Goblet squat × 5 with "spread the floor" cue
8. THEN start working sets.

Pre-upper-day routine (10 min):
1. Cat-camel × 7-8 cycles
2. Banded arm circles × 10/side
3. Band pull-aparts × 15
4. Basketball-mobility × 60 s (T-spine extension)
5. Travolta-peanuts × 30 s/side
6. Pec-minor stretch × 30 s/side
7. Suitcase carry × 30 s/side (waking up grip + core)
8. THEN start working sets.

## The opposite of "surround the dragon" — what to avoid

- **Isolation training** — biceps curls at a wall, leg extensions, hamstring curls. These DON'T surround the dragon; they fragment the body. UBF Ch 1: "the pollution of bodybuilding approaches" in back training.
- **Training one joint to the exclusion of adjacent joints.** Strong shoulders + weak scapular control = injury. Strong glutes + weak T-spine = compensation.
- **Sport-specific training without prerequisite mobility/stability surrounding the back.** A runner training only running is missing the dragon-surrounders → injury setup.

## "Train the motion, not the muscle"

The companion principle. UBF Ch 1 + Ch 14: "Train the motion — not the muscle." Surround the dragon by training INTEGRATED motions (push, pull, squat, hinge, lunge, carry, twist with locked spine) — these naturally engage all the surrounding joints. Avoid the bodybuilding error of isolating one muscle at a time and missing the integration.
