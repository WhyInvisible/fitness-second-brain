# Hip-hinge mechanics (shortstop pattern)

Per McGill Ch 7, the hip-hinge is the foundational pain-free movement tool — the substitute for almost every bend the spine would otherwise take. The hips are ball-and-socket joints designed to bend; the spine is a flexible rod that fatigues and cracks under repeated bending. Route motion through hips; lock the spine.

## The shortstop pattern (entry technique)

1. Stand erect, feet shoulder-width.
2. Place hands on thighs. The "crotch" of forefinger + thumb will eventually cap each knee.
3. Keep spine straight (neutral). Push pelvis BACK (NOT down). Slide hands down thighs.
4. Continue until the finger-thumb crotch caps the kneecap. This is the shortstop position — like a baseball shortstop ready for the ball.
5. Knees stay mid-way between heel and toe (shins ~vertical). NO forward knee drift.
6. To ascend: stiffen torso (abdominal brace, tuned). Anti-shrug — push shoulders DOWN away from ears using lats + pecs. Weight transfers down the arms to the knees.
7. Pull hips forward (NOT lift with the back). Spine stays locked. Knees stay over feet.
8. Stand upright.

Mental model on descent: "hips back" (sit back, not down). On ascent: "pull hips through" (not "lift with the back").

## The anti-shrug — the spine-protecting cue

McGill's signature add-on: instead of shrugging shoulders up, deliberately push them DOWN via latissimus dorsi + pectoralis contraction. This stiffens the upper torso, transmits load down the arms (not into the low back), and prevents the unconscious shrug that drives compressive load through the cervical and thoracic spine.

## Where it applies

- Picking up: groceries, kids, laundry basket, gym bag, dog
- Sitting down / standing up from chair, sofa, car seat, toilet
- All loaded lifts: deadlift, RDL, trap-bar deadlift, kettlebell swing, single-leg deadlift, hip thrust setup
- Brushing teeth (foot on bench variant), tying shoes (foot on bench, lunge hips to foot)
- Opening drawers, reaching into the dishwasher, gardening

## Application

If the athlete's back history (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles) includes an injury on a heavy deadlift, the mechanism was almost certainly spine bending under load. The trap-bar substitute (per [MOC-injuries-management](../MOCs/MOC-injuries-management.md)) is half the fix; the other half is the shortstop + anti-shrug pattern as the default for every loaded hinge. Master it unloaded first (chair-of-death-squat, single-leg-deadlift with dowel), then add load only when the pattern is automatic.

## How it compares to Dicharry

Dicharry teaches the same hinge via the dowel test (single-leg deadlift with dowel on spine) — the dowel reveals any spine flexion during the hinge. **Dicharry = self-coaching tool. McGill = step-by-step technique decomposition.** Use both: Dicharry's dowel + McGill's shortstop + anti-shrug + pull-hips-forward.

## Pavel's hike-pass + hinge teaching progression

Pavel (S&S, pp. 47–73) uses the exact same vocabulary as McGill (cites McGill directly, p. 56 — "short-stop drill", "anti-shrug", "lats as the super-muscles"). What Pavel adds is the **hike-pass** as a hinge teaching drill specific to ballistic loads:

1. Feet wider than shoulders, toes slightly out, 2x4s under toes (forces vertical shins by preventing the knees from drifting forward).
2. Hands chop into hip creases. Push tail BACK, NOT down. Feel hamstrings stretch like a bow.
3. **Press straight arms TIGHT against body** — upper arms to ribs, forearms to inner thighs HIGH (the "silverback" stance). This connects the arms to the torso via the lats, so any subsequent ballistic load transmits directly hip-to-implement.
4. From this loaded position, "hike pass" a kettlebell back between the legs (above the knees), then let it pendulum forward. Power-up only when the bell returns to the pendulum's apex.

The hike-pass is the **ballistic-load-specific hinge entry**. For the kettlebell swing, this is the prerequisite drill — Brett Jones: "people usually progress very quickly past this exercise to get to one-arm swings and snatches. It is a mistake."

Pavel's full hip-hinge teaching sequence: hip-hinge drill (unloaded) → short-stop drill → kettlebell deadlift (touch-and-go reps, bell parks between heels) → hike-pass drill (no swings yet) → two-arm swing → one-arm swing. Each stage owned before the next.
