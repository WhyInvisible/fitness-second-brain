# 80/20 -- and structure the 20%

~80% of weekly running **time** (not just session count) should be at low intensity (below the ventilatory threshold ~ Z2 / conversational). The other 20% isn't a single "hard" bucket -- split it: ~10-14% at LT1 (sub-threshold), ~3-5% at LT2 (true threshold), ~1-3% at Zone 4 (hill repeats / short race-pace work).

## Origin: Seiler's research (the science)

The 80/20 pattern was discovered, not designed. American exercise physiologist **Stephen Seiler**, working in Norway in the early 2000s, measured the training of elite XC skiers, rowers, cyclists, swimmers, triathletes, and runners — and found they had independently converged on the same ~80/20 ratio. Validated in controlled trials (Esteve-Lanao & Seiler 2007 + 2013; Stöggl & Sperlich 2014). See [seiler-research-origin-of-80-20](seiler-research-origin-of-80-20.md) for the full study trail.

## Origin: the coaching lineage (the practice)

The pattern existed empirically *before* Seiler measured it. Lydiard's 1960s revolution (replacing Zátopek-era interval training with high-volume slow running) → Bill Squires and the Greater Boston Track Club (Rodgers' 81% easy week en route to Boston) → Kenyan school-to-school slow running base → Mo Farah's contemporary 80% easy on 120 mi/wk. Every winner of a major international competition since Lydiard owes their success to slow running. Per Fitzgerald: "evolution solving problems blindly."

## Why the polarisation matters

The "middle" — Seiler's [black-hole-of-training-intensity](black-hole-of-training-intensity.md), the band between VT and LT — is *almost as stressful* as truly hard running yet *substantially less productive* for aerobic adaptation. Time there is the dominant reason recreational runners plateau. Per Kelemen 2024's review of 13 elite Norwegian distance runners (including the Ingebrigtsens, Marius Bakken, Grete Waitz, Ingrid Kristiansen), every analysed athlete trains at 75-80% easy intensity.

## The execution problem

Most runners *think* they do 80/20 but actually do ~50/50, because of [intensity-blindness](intensity-blindness.md). Self-selected pace converges on RPE ~12.5 ("somewhat hard"), which sits above VT in the black hole. The fix: enforce HR ceilings on easy days (see [hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard](hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard.md)) and run a [week-of-slow-reset](week-of-slow-reset.md) periodically to recalibrate.

## Practical application

- Easy runs at conversational pace, <2 mmol lactate, RPE 3-4, HR ≤ 89% LTHR
- Quality work split by lactate band rather than treating "hard" as one zone
- One Zone 4 stimulus per week (hills or sprints)
- Apply to **total training time**, not session count — and to total training including cross-training (not per-discipline)

## Race-period adjustment

75/25 in base, 80/20 in race weeks (less hard work as race approaches). Fitzgerald's race plans actually run closer to **90/10 in the base phase** (priority: volume ramp) and **~80/20 in the peak phase** (priority: race-specific quality).
