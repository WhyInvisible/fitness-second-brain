# Torso stiffness for running economy

Per McGill UBF Ch 16, the long-distance runner case study (pp. 300-301). The most directly running-relevant statement in McGill's entire body of work:

> "Efficiency in the long distance runner depends on a stiff torso." — McGill, *Ultimate Back Fitness and Performance*, Ch 16, pp. 300–301

McGill's suggested starting point: have the runner imagine there is no joint at all between the shoulders and the hips.

This reframes core work for runners. Core training isn't just back insurance — it's **running economy training**, in the same category as cadence drills and fueling. A stiff torso converts more leg drive into forward motion.

## Why stiffness = economy

Running is force transmission. Each foot strike generates ground reaction force that must propel the body forward via the leg-hip-torso-leg chain. ANY motion in the spine during this transmission is an **energy leak** — leg-generated force gets absorbed by a flexing/twisting lumbar instead of transmitted into forward momentum.

The mechanical analogy (UBF Ch 15): a stiff bicycle frame transmits all pedal force to the wheels; a compliant one wastes it via flex. The torso is the bicycle frame between hips and shoulders. Flex = leak.

Over a marathon, small economy losses compound: a 1% energy leak across a 3:30 race is roughly 2 minutes lost.

## The cue: "no joint between shoulders and hips"

The mental image McGill prescribes. During the run, imagine the entire torso (shoulders, ribcage, lumbar, pelvis) as ONE rigid unit. The arms move at the shoulder joints. The legs move at the hip joints. NOTHING moves in between.

Manifest as:
- Ribcage stacked over pelvis (no anterior pelvic tilt, no slouch).
- Lumbar in slight neutral / extension (NOT flexed).
- Shoulders relaxed but not collapsed forward.
- Arms swing from the shoulder, NOT from the elbow.
- NO twist in the torso with each stride (the arms counter-rotate the legs, the torso doesn't itself rotate).

## What kills running economy via torso instability

UBF Ch 16 + Ch 5 + Ch 12 — common runner issues:

1. **Pelvic drop at foot strike.** Weak QL on the opposite side. Lateral spine torsion follows. See [quadratus-lumborum-training](quadratus-lumborum-training.md).
2. **Asymmetric lumbar torsion at foot strike.** UBF: "I look for movement asymmetry in the torso. For example, upon right foot heel strike there may be torsional motion in the lumbar region." Often caused by leg-length asymmetry, glute weakness, foot mechanics, or alignment.
3. **Anterior pelvic tilt + lumbar lordosis.** Tight hip flexors + weak abs. The spine is in passive end-range = no spring, no efficient force transmission.
4. **Forward head + slouched shoulders.** Chronic erector contraction holding the head up — burns capacity, alters arm swing.
5. **Diaphragm collapse under heavy breathing.** During hard breathing the abs relax (to allow expansion) and the brace is lost. Spine becomes vulnerable + force transmission leaks.

## What to train (running-economy-driven core work)

This is the runner-specific version of the core program — every exercise picked for transfer to gait stability:

**Daily (low cost, high return):**
- [McGill Big 3](mcgill-big-3.md) via [Russian Descending Pyramid](russian-descending-pyramid.md). The foundation. Done.

**2-3×/week (within strength sessions):**
- **Suitcase carry** (per [quadratus-lumborum-training](quadratus-lumborum-training.md)) — 3-4 × 30 s/side, moderate load. The single most running-relevant QL/anti-lateral-flexion exercise.
- **Full side-bridge under heavy breathing** — bike 30 s hard to elevate breathing → drop into highest side-bridge variant → hold while breathing heavily. Trains the diaphragm + abdominal-wall co-existence directly relevant to running fatigue.
- **Pallof press hold** (per [torsional-buttressing](torsional-buttressing.md)) — anti-rotation isometric, trains the resistance to per-stride torsion.

**Pre-run (during warmup):**
- Cat-camel 7-8 cycles → 1 set of each Big 3 → walk briskly with explicit "no joint" cue for first 5 min of run.

## What NOT to do (anti-economy)

UBF's specific runner warnings:
- **Over-stretching hamstrings and spine in flexion.** Common in runner cool-downs. Lengthens what should be tight + weakens the elastic spring. NEVER stretch beyond ROM used in running.
- **Sit-ups, crunches, leg raises.** Train motion at the spine — the opposite of running economy.
- **Body-building isolation core work.** Six-pack drills don't translate to gait stability.

## The asymmetry caveat

If asymmetry is present (e.g. one hamstring chronically tight, glute med possibly weaker on the same side — check [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles), addressing the asymmetry is the highest-return economy intervention. UBF: "I look for movement asymmetry in the torso ... more core stiffness mostly always helps."

If the athlete has a one-sided pattern:
1. Add extra suitcase-carry volume on one side until the asymmetry resolves — the QL opposite the loaded hand works hardest, so load the hand that targets the weaker side.
2. Watch for pelvic drop during single-leg stance in a mirror or video. If the pelvis drops on the swing side, glute med work on the stance side is priority.
3. Run a glute amnesia self-test on both sides per [glute-amnesia](glute-amnesia.md).

## How this differs from generic "core for runners"

Most "core for runners" prescriptions = planks, sit-ups, bicycle crunches. UBF's running-specific position:
- Planks are fine but the side-bridge under heavy breathing is more specific.
- Sit-ups + bicycle crunches are actively counterproductive (train spine motion under load).
- Carries (especially suitcase + bottoms-up KB) are more specific than supine core work.
- The key isn't more reps — it's **anti-motion under fatigue**.

## Race-day application

The cue "no joint between shoulders and hips" can be cycled through during the marathon — re-cueing every 5 km or whenever fatigue spikes. By km 30+ when form starts breaking, this cue (along with cadence + relax-shoulders) is the highest-leverage in-race technique reminder.

Common late-race failure mode (cramping around km 30+): if the legs cramp from an undertrained lower body, the body compensates by hunching forward + twisting → spine takes more load → cascade. A stiffer torso (built in training) gives more time before that cascade hits.
