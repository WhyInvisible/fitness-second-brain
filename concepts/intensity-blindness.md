# Intensity blindness

Runners systematically misperceive their habitual pace as "easy" when it is actually moderate. Left to themselves, recreational runners settle into a self-selected pace that lands at RPE ~12.5 on the Borg scale ("somewhat hard") — *above* the ventilatory threshold, in the [black hole](black-hole-of-training-intensity.md). They are not aware they are doing this.

## The evidence

- **Gilman 1993 (ASU)** — recreationally competitive female runners self-reported 60% easy / 20% moderate / 20% hard training. HR monitors revealed actual distribution: **46% / 46% / 9%**. They believed they were doing 3× more easy than moderate; they were doing equal amounts.
- **Wayne State 2001** — non-athletes on treadmill, bike, stair climber at "self-selected pace": all three machines, RPE converged on 12.5 — same psychological setpoint regardless of physiology.
- **2012 30-female-runner study** — average RPE on self-selected treadmill pace: 12.79, SD 1.15. Tight consensus around "somewhat hard."

## Why it happens

Humans are **task-oriented**. Given a fixed distance ("go run 5 miles"), the instinct is to get it done; the compromise between "finish quickly" and "don't suffer" lands at moderate intensity. This bias is present across all aerobic activities (cycling, swimming, elliptical, stair climbing) — it's a perceptual setpoint, not a running quirk.

## The cost

If you do this every easy day, you spend ~50% of weekly training in the moderate-intensity rut and your adaptation rate stalls (or, like Fitzgerald's client Juan Carlos, your 10K time goes backward and you "can't even run 8:45 pace anymore").

## How to break it

1. **Run a [week of slow](week-of-slow-reset.md)** — one week of all runs at "embarrassingly easy" pace, no watch. Resets perception of what easy actually is.
2. **Use HR, not pace, on easy days.** Pace numbers feed the speed-up instinct; HR ceilings get respected. See [hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard](hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard.md).
3. **Get specific.** "Run easy" is too vague. "Run below [the athlete's Z2 ceiling] bpm" is unambiguous. Take the number from [profile.md](../profile.md) → Current fitness (derived per [MOC-paces-zones](../MOCs/MOC-paces-zones.md)).

## Application

Check the athlete's habitual easy runs against their own numbers: if easy-run HR routinely sits above the Z2 ceiling, or the easy pace they settle into is at or faster than the E-pace range for their current VDOT, intensity blindness is the likely cause. Run the [week-of-slow-reset](week-of-slow-reset.md) before the next build phase to confirm and recalibrate.
