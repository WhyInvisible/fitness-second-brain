# An easier progression is the fix for sloppy reps

The right response to "I can't do this exercise with good form" is **drop a progression**, not "grind through with bad form" or "use a band assist and pretend it's the same".

## Why this is the right answer

- Sloppy reps wire bad patterns ([perfect-practice-beats-practice](perfect-practice-beats-practice.md)).
- Bad patterns at progression N will be worse at progression N+1.
- A clean progression at N-1 builds the strength + neural pattern needed to do progression N cleanly later.
- Cheating reps tend to recruit compensating muscles that won't transfer — and may set up injury patterns.

## Common cases

- **Pull-ups with kipping, half-ROM, or not getting chin over bar** → drop to band-assisted pull-up OR eccentric-only pull-up. NOT "do 12 kipping pull-ups".
- **HSPU with elbows flared and arched back** → drop to pike HSPU on the ground or box HSPU. NOT "do half-range HSPU against a wall".
- **Pistol squat with knee caving / using the other foot for balance** → drop to assisted pistol (hold a door) OR Cossack squat. NOT "do bad pistols".
- **Front lever with rounded chest / bent hips** → drop to advanced tuck FL. NOT "hold a sagging straddle".
- **Muscle-up with massive kip / chicken wing transition** → drop to muscle-up negative + assisted MU. NOT "grind out ugly kipping muscle-ups".

## The diagnostic

After a set, ask:
- Was every rep clean (full ROM, proper scapular position, no cheating)?
- If 1–2 reps slipped at the end: OK, this is the working level.
- If 3+ reps were sloppy / I had to cheat the last reps: drop a progression for the next session.

## Why this is counterintuitive

It feels like progress to do harder progressions sloppily and like regression to drop down. **It's the opposite.** Dropping down preserves quality, builds the right pattern, and gets you to the harder progression cleanly faster than grinding sloppy reps.

> The strongest people I know are also the most willing to scale down when form slips.

## The corollary: connective tissue safety

This rule double-counts for any straight-arm progression. Sloppy reps at a too-hard straight-arm progression don't just teach bad patterns — they overload connective tissue at angles muscle can handle but tendon can't. See [connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months](connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months.md).
