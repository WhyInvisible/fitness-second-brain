# Hardstyle = explosive tension + complete relaxation, alternating

Per Pavel Pt III ("Hard Style"), hardstyle kettlebell training is rooted in the karate principle of *kime* — "an explosive attack to the target using the appropriate technique and maximum power in the shortest time possible" (Masatoshi Nakayama). Translated to strength training: every rep is **full power for an instant, full relaxation for the rest.** Both halves are trained skills.

## What hardstyle IS

- **Maximum intent** on the working phase (the upswing, the punch, the lockout).
- **Complete relaxation** in the transition phases (the float at the top of a swing, the descent, the pause between strikes).
- **Brief contraction window** — masters tense only for "frame 10 of a 10-frame punch." Beginners tense from frame 1 and waste energy bracing the wrong muscles for 90% of the movement.
- **Audible, deliberate breathing** — "KIAI" exhale through the teeth on the upswing, sharp nasal inhale on descent. Power-breathing IS the brace.

## What hardstyle IS NOT

- **NOT maximum strain.** Trying 100% effort triggers antagonist co-contraction that brakes the movement. Pavel cites Volkov: cap explosive effort at ~95%, ~90% for daily practice, ~80% for timed testing.
- **NOT slow grinding.** A grinding rep has neither the explosive tension nor the relaxation phase. It's continuous medium-tension — the worst of both worlds for power, and the most injurious.
- **NOT lazy pendulum** swinging. A passive pendulum swing has the relaxation phase but no tension phase — no power, no adaptation.
- **NOT held-breath bracing for the whole rep.** Breath cycles with the rep.

## Why the alternation matters

1. **Power production** depends on rate of fibre recruitment, not strain. Tense too long → fast fibres fatigue → power drops.
2. **Energy economy** — a boxer who tenses only at impact can throw 100 punches a round. One who tenses for every motion lasts 30 seconds.
3. **Joint protection** — the relaxed phase lets blood circulate, joint capsules unload, soft tissues recover.
4. **Skill transfer** — the same on-off pattern governs every ballistic athletic act: sprint stride, jump, throw, punch, kick.

## How to train both halves

- **Tension drill:** the brace + glute cramp at the top of the swing (or the get-up's full-body plank). [abdominal-brace](abdominal-brace.md) tuning calibrates this.
- **Relaxation drill:** the float at the top of the swing ([pop-and-float-ballistic-cue](pop-and-float-ballistic-cue.md)). The 90/90 stretch + pull-up hang in the cool-down. Shake-the-water-off-the-fingertips between sets.
- **Volume-knob practice** (Brett Jones): call out an effort number 1–10 each rep, hit that level deliberately. Discover what 90% feels like, what 50% feels like.
- **The percentage drill** (taught to spec ops): partner-fed strikes at random effort levels (50/80/90/50/70/...) — experienced fighters' hardest strikes land at 80–90%, not 100%.

## Where it applies beyond kettlebells

- **Calisthenics holds** (L-sit, planche lean, front-lever): full brace for the hold, full relaxation between sets. The core of any post-race skill block.
- **Sprint strides**: explosive ground contact, relaxed shoulders + face during flight. [workout-strides](workout-strides.md) implicitly trains this.
- **Climbing**: dead-hang relaxation between dynamic moves.
- **Loaded carries**: pulsed brace tuned to step rhythm, not continuous strain. [carries-train-anti-lateral-flexion](carries-train-anti-lateral-flexion.md).

## Practical translation

The hardstyle frame is the antidote to the "grinding intermediate" lifting habit (high tension for moderate-length efforts = lots of work, little power, slow recovery). For an athlete with marathon training stealing recovery capacity, hardstyle's energy economy is essential — train **harder but shorter**, with deliberate relaxation, and the strength session doesn't compete with the running.
