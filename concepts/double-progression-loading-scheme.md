# Double-progression loading scheme

Per Matthews Ch 16. The simplest reliable mechanism for [progressive-overload](progressive-overload.md): work in a rep range, beat last week by 1 rep at the same weight, and when you hit the top of the range on ONE set, add weight on the next set.

## The rule

In Matthews' version: as soon as ONE set reaches 6 reps at a weight, the next set goes up 10 lb (5 lb a side on a barbell, 5 lb per dumbbell). If the heavier weight only gives 2-3 reps, the remaining sets drop back to the old weight, and the athlete tries the jump again the following week (Matthews, *Bigger Leaner Stronger*, Ch 16).

For BLS the rep range is 4-6. Other valid ranges:
- **3-5** (more strength-biased, less hypertrophy) — the better fit for lifting inside a marathon block, per [heavy-low-rep-not-high-rep-light](heavy-low-rep-not-high-rep-light.md)
- **6-8** (more hypertrophy-biased, slightly easier on CNS)
- **8-10** (Matthews' "lighter accessory" range, used on Upper Body day)

## Why two layers of progression

- **Layer 1 (reps):** add a rep at the same weight = real strength gain even with no load change
- **Layer 2 (weight):** when you cap the range, the weight goes up = the real overload mechanism

A typical week-over-week pattern at 4-6:
- Wk 1: 100 kg × 4, 4, 4
- Wk 2: 100 × 5, 4, 4
- Wk 3: 100 × 5, 5, 4
- Wk 4: 100 × 6, 5, 5 → top of range hit on set 1 → add 10 lb (4.5 kg) on set 2
- Wk 4: 100 × 6, 104.5 × 4, 100 × 5
- Wk 5: start with 104.5 × 4, build up reps over next several weeks

## Why this works

- Eliminates the question "should I add weight this week?" — the rep count answers it
- Eliminates the question "how much should I add?" — 10 lb (or 1.25-2.5 kg via fractional plates if 10 lb stalls)
- Forces honest tracking: if you don't log reps, you can't apply it
- Aligned with [bar-speed-rule](bar-speed-rule.md) — the set ends if speed crashes, you don't grind out a 6 by force

## When NOT to add weight even if you hit the top

- Bar speed dropped > 50% during the set (Dicharry rule — see [bar-speed-rule](bar-speed-rule.md))
- Form broke down (rounded back, knee collapse, grinding)
- Sleep / recovery is compromised
- During a marathon-build maintenance phase ([cut-strength-in-peak-weeks](cut-strength-in-peak-weeks.md)) — hold loads, don't add

## Practical translation

- **During a marathon block:** use double-progression on the rep range 3-5 (lower than BLS) for the few heavy lifts in [MOC-running-strength](../MOCs/MOC-running-strength.md). Add 2.5 kg per week max. Cap progressions during high-mileage weeks.
- **Off-season / strength block after the A-race:** use Matthews' full 4-6 range with 10 lb increments. This is where the mechanism shines.
- **Microload after plateau:** if you stall for 2 weeks at 6 reps, drop the next jump to +2.5 kg via fractional plates rather than the full +5 kg. Phase 4 loading in [MOC-running-strength](../MOCs/MOC-running-strength.md) already implies this.
