# Movement efficiency beats movement effort

Per Le Corre Manifesto principle 10 ("Efficient") + Part 2 (Movement Efficiency), the goal of skill training is efficiency — *performance × energy conservation × safety* — not maximum exertion or fatigue. The same movement executed efficiently looks easy and feels easy; executed inefficiently it looks ugly even at low intensity and accelerates fatigue + injury risk.

> "Efficient movement and practice do not make the impossible possible, but they do enable an existing potential to become a reality." — Le Corre, *The Practice of Natural Movement*, Pt 2

## The five universal efficiency mechanics

Le Corre identifies five mechanics that apply to *every* movement technique — they're the building blocks of efficient execution:

1. **Breathing** — abdominal/diaphragmatic, nasal at rest, mouth-supplemental at intensity, synchronised with effort. Inhale = tensing effect; exhale = relaxing effect; brace = abdominal tension + brief breath-hold for compression-bearing tasks. **Breathing is the first technique because all other techniques depend on it.**
2. **Position** — alignment of body segments to each other and to the environment. Postural integrity is task-specific: "straight back" is right for hinging, wrong for rolling. No all-purpose position.
3. **Tension-Relaxation** — *selective* tension where needed, relaxation everywhere else, alternating dynamically. Total tension = stiffness, early fatigue, cramping. Total relaxation = collapse, no force production. The skill is *which muscles to tense how much, when*.
4. **Sequence and Timing** — body segments fire in a specific order. In a throw: eyes lead → head → hips → shoulder → arm → hand → release. Bad timing destroys an otherwise correct movement.
5. **Local Positional Control** — micro-adjustments at each joint (fingers, toes, eyes, neck, scapula, hip rotation) as the building block of macro-movement. Many "balance failures" are actually local-positional-control failures.

## Why efficiency beats effort

1. **Effort without efficiency teaches inefficiency.** Every grinding rep with broken form is a rep of *practising* broken form — neural patterning of the wrong thing. Stop the set when efficiency breaks, not when failure hits. (Cross-source agreement with Pavel: "Stop when speed drops, not when something gives out.")
2. **Efficiency is what scales.** The same body weight + the same heart can sustain dramatically more output if movement is efficient. Efficiency improvements have multiplicative effects; effort improvements have additive effects.
3. **Efficiency is what transfers across contexts.** Effort is task-specific (can only push max effort in the trained context). Efficiency is mechanical-pattern-based and transfers to any context that uses the same patterns.
4. **Efficiency reduces injury risk.** Most injuries happen in the moment form breaks under fatigue. Efficiency means more reserve before form breaks; means form breaks more gracefully when it does.
5. **Efficiency is what aging preserves.** Strength + power decline faster than skill. Efficient movement patterns once learned persist for decades; raw strength does not.

## The "competence ladder" — how efficiency is acquired

Le Corre's adaptation of the conscious-competence ladder:

1. **Unconscious incompetence** — moving naturally but poorly without knowing it.
2. **Conscious incompetence** — noticing the inefficiency; aware that there's a problem.
3. **Conscious competence** — having learned the efficient pattern but needing active attention to execute it.
4. **Unconscious competence** — efficient pattern is now automatic; conscious mind is free for context.

**The goal of practice is to move people from #1 → #4.** Most people stay at #1 their whole training lives because no one points out the inefficiency. Step #1 → #2 is the diagnostic step — the most important and most often skipped.

## What efficient practice looks like operationally

- **Slow before fast.** "Slow is truth." Slow execution exposes inefficiencies that speed hides. Master slow + correct before adding speed.
- **One issue at a time.** Trying to fix all inefficiencies simultaneously fixes none. Prioritise the *root cause* (often breathing or positioning) before chasing symptoms.
- **Reset between reps.** "Ten sets of one rep" beats "one set of ten reps" for skill learning. Each rep gets full attention; between-reps lets the CNS process feedback.
- **"One shot" mindset.** Treat each rep as the only attempt — forces full mental + physical commitment, eliminates "I'll get it on the next rep" laziness.
- **Visualisation.** Mentally rehearse the sequence before executing. Especially useful for fast movements (jumps) that are too brief to consciously analyse in real time.
- **Deconstruction.** Break complex sequences into segments; fix each segment in isolation; then reintegrate.
- **Exaggerated practice of inefficiencies.** Counter-intuitive but effective: intentionally amplify the inefficiency you're trying to fix — makes the CNS realise how wrong the pattern feels.
- **Stop when quality drops.** Pushing past quality is anti-practice — it trains inefficiency.

## Application

- **In a marathon block:** the highest-leverage efficiency target is *running form during easy miles* (where most volume lives). Already covered by Fitzgerald's [stride-self-optimises-dont-coach-form](stride-self-optimises-dont-coach-form.md) and Dicharry's [precision-workouts-anchor](precision-workouts-anchor.md). Le Corre adds: **breathing efficiency is the first lever**. Nasal at easy pace + diaphragmatic. If chest-breathing happens at easy pace, that's a flag.
- **In strength sessions:** the BLS warm-up protocol [bls-warmup-protocol](bls-warmup-protocol.md) + bar-speed rule [bar-speed-rule](bar-speed-rule.md) + Russian Pyramid Big-3 [russian-descending-pyramid](russian-descending-pyramid.md) already operationalise efficiency-over-effort in a plan built from this vault. Le Corre adds: between-rep reset + slow-before-fast + "one shot" mindset for new lifts.
- **In calisthenics (off-season, after the A-race):** efficiency-over-effort *is* the calisthenics ethos (Low cites it constantly). Le Corre's contribution is the broader vocabulary application — apply the same mindset to crawls, carries, jumps, hangs, not just to the 5 named skills.
- **A specific morning ritual:** 5 min daily of *one new technique slow* (precision jump, balance walk, get-up variant, carry hold) with deliberate attention to one of the five mechanics. Builds the efficiency habit in <0.1% of weekly training time.
