# Super stiffness (elite force transmission)

Per McGill UBF Ch 15, the entire chapter. The signature of elite athletes is not maximum strength, not maximum speed, but the **rapid alternation between maximum stiffness and maximum compliance** — the boom-boom-boom rhythm of contracted-relaxed-contracted that produces both explosive force AND high movement velocity.

This is the highest-level concept in McGill's UBF framework, and the one most overlooked by both clinicians (who train rehab) and S&C coaches (who train brute strength). It is **trainable**, but only on top of an established foundation of motor patterns + stability + endurance + base strength.

## The boom-boom rhythm

UBF measured this in MMA fighters (Georges St. Pierre, Pavel Tsatsouline kicking), elite golfers, sprinters, Olympic weightlifters. The pattern at the moment of athletic effort:

1. **Pre-impact: relaxed.** Muscle compliance allows free limb motion. (Sprinter in blocks. Boxer floating. Golfer at top of backswing.)
2. **First pulse: rapid contraction initiates motion.** Hip + core fire to set the limb on its trajectory.
3. **Mid-motion: relaxation.** Continued stiffness here would slow the limb. The fist/foot/club accelerates with relaxed-yet-aligned mid-chain muscles.
4. **Second pulse at impact: TOTAL body stiffening.** All muscles co-contract for ~milliseconds. The body becomes "effective mass" — the entire bodyweight transmits through the contact point. Then immediately:
5. **Rapid relaxation.** Return to defensive/reset posture, ready for the next cycle.

**The signature:** the second pulse + the rapid relaxation. Both ends of the ON-OFF cycle are trained skills, not raw strength.

## The 8 principles (UBF Ch 15)

### 1. Rapid contraction AND rapid relaxation
Elite sprinters: muscle activation in the blocks is MINIMAL (relaxed = compliance = potential). Gun → explosive contraction → equally explosive relaxation as the limb swings forward → re-activation to plant. All this in the first step. Training: practice "popping" muscles ON-OFF-ON-OFF in isolation drills (one-leg squat with glute "pop"; dead-bug with hip-shoulder "twitch"; matveyev release of isometric contraction upon a strike).

### 2. Tune the muscles — ~25% MVC pre-tension
For the abdominal wall as an elastic spring (used in throwing, kicking, running, golf swing): pre-contraction below 25% MVC = mush, no elastic energy storage. Above 25% = stuck, no movement. **~25% MVC pre-tension** is the sweet spot for storing + releasing elastic energy. (Brown + McGill 2005.)

### 3. Muscular binding (plywood effect)
The three layers of the abdominal wall (transverse abdominis, internal oblique, external oblique) + rectus abdominis, when activated TOGETHER, bind via their fascial planes. Total stiffness EXCEEDS the sum of individual stiffnesses — like plywood vs four loose planks. This is why **abdominal hollowing (TVA only) defeats super stiffness**. The full brace creates the composite. (Brown + McGill 2009.)

### 4. Direct neuronal overflow
Contraction at remote joints "squeezes" neural drive back to the working joint. **The handshake demo:** shake → grip max. Now: grip the floor with feet, isometric hip abduction + external rotation, clench the other fist, brace abs, depress shoulders with lats. Repeat handshake — measurably stronger (often 30%+). Use this to break through sticking points.

### 5. Eliminate energy leaks
"Stay tight." A stiff bicycle frame transmits all pedal force to the wheels; a compliant one wastes it. Stiffness at WEAKER joints lets STRONGER joints transmit their full force. Punch: wrist + elbow stiffen so shoulder power reaches the fist. Sprint: torso stiffens (no joint between shoulders and hips) so leg drive reaches the ground.

### 6. Get through the sticking points
Whole-body stiffening at the moment of mechanical disadvantage SHIFTS DEMAND from weaker joints to stronger joints. Bench press stuck mid-rep: "spread the bar" + "bend the bar" (lat + grip super-stiffness) shifts demand to chest + lats. Strongman tire flip stuck: stiffen entire body, the hips rise the hands.

### 7. Optimize the passive connective tissue
Elite runners "run on passive tissue" — tendon and fascia as springs. **NEVER stretch a runner beyond joint angles used in running.** Stretching beyond sport ROM weakens the spring + modulates the protective stretch reflex. (See Stretching position.) Asymmetry correction is the ONLY valid reason to static-stretch.

### 8. Create shockwaves
For loads at the edge of capacity: stiffen the body + use a SMALL rapid "dip" at hip/knee → a shockwave travels up the stiffened linkage to the load. One-arm KB military press with too-heavy weight: turn body to stone, knee dip, the wave lifts the bell. Russian benchpress technique: heel "stomp" at the bottom initiates a wave that initiates the drive.

## What you can NOT skip to reach super stiffness

UBF is emphatic: super-stiffness is **Stage 5** training. The prerequisites:
- **Stage 1:** motor patterns, ab brace, glute reactivation (no glute amnesia).
- **Stage 2:** Big 3 stability built to advanced forms.
- **Stage 3:** endurance via Russian Reverse Pyramid.
- **Stage 4:** base strength surrounding the dragon.

Skipping to super-stiffness with a glute-amnesiac, motor-pattern-confused athlete = injury. The chapter explicitly (UBF Ch 15): "The foundation for performance has been laid with the 5 stages of back training described in this book."

## Practical translation: when and how

**Not during a marathon block.** This is post-race / off-season content. Reasons:
- Capacity drain — marathon training already maxes the back's load budget.
- The Big 3 + glute reactivation is the right level of core/back work for a marathon training block.
- Super-stiffness training is mentally and physically demanding (deep focus, max effort) — incompatible with the cognitive fatigue + recovery debt of marathon training.

**After the A-race:**
- When calisthenics becomes the primary focus (per overcoming-gravity skill ladders), super-stiffness DIRECTLY applies — every static hold (front lever, planche, L-sit) is a super-stiffness exercise. The chapter becomes the "how to push the upper performance ceiling" reference.
- Specific exercises to try first: handshake demo (test for pre vs post), one-arm KB military press shockwave, clapping push-up, modified bird-dog explosive contraction (the bird-dog → plyometric gateway).

**Always relevant now:** the principle **#7 — don't stretch beyond running ROM**. This applies immediately. Stop chasing flexibility for its own sake. The hamstring + spine kyphosis emphasis common in runner programs is counterproductive. Asymmetry correction (e.g. one hamstring measurably tighter than the other, per [individual-alignment-assessment](individual-alignment-assessment.md)) is the only valid stretching target.
