# Posterior Pelvic Tilt (PPT) — the master alignment cue for handstands and hollow holds

The pelvis rotated **posteriorly** (top of pelvis tipping back, tailbone tucking forward), achieved via simultaneous **glute squeeze + abdominal brace + rib pull-down**. Sommer makes this the master alignment cue across the handstand series and most FSAS work. Without it, the lower back arches, the lever / handstand / press collapses, and the load shifts from the trained muscles to the lumbar spine.

## Why PPT matters

Almost every handstand fault traces back to lower-back arch (anterior pelvic tilt). The arch:
- Disconnects the line between shoulders and hips (no more "straight line from wrist to toe")
- Loads the lumbar spine instead of the abs
- Makes balance correction impossible (the body becomes a banana, not a stick)
- Wires the wrong pattern for press handstand later (PPT must be maintained throughout the press)

The same logic applies to:
- **Hollow body hold** (FL/PE5 in Sommer) — without PPT it becomes an arch, not a hollow
- **Plank** (sPL/PE3) — without PPT the hips sag, the abs disengage
- **Pseudo planche pushup** (HBP/PE3) — without PPT the lean tips into back-loaded position
- **L-sit and manna prep** — without PPT the body can't compress
- **Press handstand entire ROM** (H2) — Sommer says PPT must be maintained from start to handstand

## How to achieve PPT — the muscular pattern

Three simultaneous contractions:
1. **Glute squeeze** — tighten the glutes hard (think "pinch a coin between cheeks")
2. **Abdominal brace** — drawn-in 360° around the spine (per [abdominal-brace](abdominal-brace.md))
3. **Rib pull-down** — pull the lower ribs toward the pelvis (engages the upper abs / serratus)

The combination tilts the pelvis posteriorly and locks it there. The lower back flattens; in supine, the lumbar spine touches the floor.

## Sommer's PPT training progression (H1 PE9–PE12 iM partners)

Sommer trains PPT as a distinct skill via the H1 handstand-prep iM exercises:

| Code | Exercise | Mastery |
|---|---|---|
| HS/PE9>iM | **Standing PPT** against wall (glutes + upper back on wall) | 5r |
| HS/PE10>iM | **Prone PPT** | 5r |
| HS/PE11>iM | **Bent Supine PPT** (knees bent) | 5r |
| HS/PE12>iM | **Supine PPT** (legs flat) | 5r |

Plus H1 SE iM partners:
- HS/SE1>iM Forearm Plank PPT
- HS/SE2>iM Plank PPT (PPT loaded by core)
- HS/SE3>iM Elevated Supine PPT
- HS/SE4>iM Supine PPT Lower

The progression is: learn PPT against a stable surface → learn PPT under core load → learn PPT in inverted positions. By the time you're holding free handstand with correct alignment, PPT has been drilled in 8 different positions.

## The PPT diagnostic test

> "If you can slide your hand under your lower back, you are arched; stop the rep and try again."
> — Sommer, HS/SE4>iM

In supine PPT, the lumbar spine should be flat on the floor — no gap. If you can slide a hand through, the PPT isn't engaged.

## Common errors

- **Hip thrust UP instead of tilt** — PPT is rotation, not elevation. Hips don't change height, they change angle.
- **Squeezing glutes without engaging abs** → posterior tilt happens but ribs flare, lumbar still vulnerable
- **Bracing abs without squeezing glutes** → spine flattens but pelvis stays neutral
- **Pulling ribs down without bracing abs** → upper torso compresses but pelvis untouched
- **Treating PPT as a position to hit then forget** — PPT must be maintained continuously throughout the exercise, not just at start

## Practical application

**During a running-focused block (e.g. a marathon build)**: PPT is a useful habit-builder during any plank / hollow body / bird-dog work. Cue: "tail tuck + glute squeeze + rib down". Build the pattern early so it's automatic when handstand work starts in earnest.

**When handstand work becomes a priority** (often the off-season after a running A-race): PPT becomes one of the two master alignment cues for handstand work (alongside scap elevation per [protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control](protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control.md)). Adopt Sommer's standing PPT drill as the iM partner between wall handstand sets — 5r between every set.

**Back-safety bonus**: PPT is consistent with McGill's spine-sparing doctrine — it produces a neutral-to-flexion-resistant spine for loaded positions. Worth using during squat / deadlift / OHP setup too (per [hip-hinge-mechanics](hip-hinge-mechanics.md)).
