# Big 5 compound priority — squat / hinge / bench / OHP / row

Per Matthews Ch 17 ("Meet your makers"). The "non-negotiable" lifts that drive natural strength + body composition. Matthews lists four (squat / deadlift / bench / military press); we add the barbell row as the fifth because pulling balance matters and Matthews himself programs it every week in every split.

## The Big 5

| Lift | Pattern | Why it's non-negotiable |
|---|---|---|
| **Squat** (back or front) | Squat | Trains every leg muscle, core, posterior chain; allows heavy loading through a stable spine |
| **Deadlift** (conventional, trap-bar, sumo, or RDL) | Hinge | The "ultimate full-body workout"; recruits posterior chain + grip + traps |
| **Bench press** (incline or flat, barbell preferred) | Horizontal push | The dominant chest + anterior delt + tricep builder |
| **Overhead / Military press** (seated or standing barbell) | Vertical push | The dominant shoulder builder; trains traps, core stability |
| **Barbell row** (or T-bar / cable variants) | Horizontal pull | Posterior balance to bench; lats, rhomboids, mid-traps, rear delts |

## The "non-negotiable" rule

**Rule:** the Big 5 go in every week of every training cycle, indefinitely. The "negotiables" (dumbbell variants, isolation work) rotate every 8–10 weeks; the Big 5 never rotate out. (Paraphrasing Matthews, *Bigger Leaner Stronger*, Ch 17.)

The reason: long-term progress comes from the same small set of lifts, applied with [double-progression-loading-scheme](double-progression-loading-scheme.md) over years. Constantly changing main lifts prevents you from accumulating overload across cycles. This is the direct rebuttal to "muscle confusion" theory.

## Why these five over isolation

- They recruit the most total muscle per unit time
- They allow the heaviest absolute loads, which drives strength progression
- They produce systemic anabolic stress (hormonal response, CNS demand)
- They're the lifts on which technique matters most → highest skill payoff for time invested
- A Ball State 2000 study Matthews cites: adding bicep curls + tricep extensions to a 4-compound upper-body programme produced NO additional arm size or strength vs compounds alone

## How they're programmed

- **Always first in the workout** (warm-up sets + 3 heavy working sets)
- **Lowest rep range** (3-6 when running alongside a marathon build, 4-6 for BLS standard)
- **Longest rest** (3-4 min between heavy sets)
- **Linear progression** via [double-progression-loading-scheme](double-progression-loading-scheme.md)
- **One per major movement pattern per training day**

## Application — two contexts

### During a marathon build (constrained by [MOC-running-strength](../MOCs/MOC-running-strength.md))

Only Squat + Hinge actively programmed in the weekly heavy lower-body session (e.g. back squat + trap-bar deadlift). Bench / OHP / row can stay in an upper-body push/pull split but with reduced rep ranges (5-6 instead of 8-10) and compound priority.

### Off-season / strength-primary block (when BLS-style training becomes the A-goal)

ALL 5 lifts in the rotation, every week, e.g.:
- Day 1 (Chest): Incline BB bench
- Day 2 (Back): Trap-bar deadlift + barbell row
- Day 3 (Shoulders): Seated BB military press
- Day 4 (Upper): Incline bench again (8-10 reps)
- Day 5 (Legs): Back squat or front squat

## The hinge constraint for a back-injury history

If the athlete has a lower-back injury history (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles), McGill's guidance dictates:
- **Trap-bar deadlift** is the default heavy hinge — see exercises/trap-bar-deadlift.md. Less shear on the spine than conventional barbell deadlift.
- **Conventional barbell deadlift** is deferred (only in an off-season block, only if no back signals, only with coaching, very light progression).
- **RDL** stays in for hamstring + glute work, capped at Dicharry's [runner-strength-loading-targets](runner-strength-loading-targets.md).
- See [hip-hinge-mechanics](hip-hinge-mechanics.md) + [power-at-hips-not-spine](power-at-hips-not-spine.md) for the technique constraints.
