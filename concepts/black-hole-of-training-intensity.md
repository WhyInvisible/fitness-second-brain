# The "black hole" of training intensity

Stephen Seiler's term, popularised by Fitzgerald: **the range between the ventilatory threshold (VT, ~78% HRmax) and the lactate threshold (LT, ~94% HRmax) is the worst place to spend training time.** It is *almost as stressful* as truly hard running yet *substantially less productive* for aerobic adaptation. Spend too much time there and you accumulate fatigue without driving meaningful fitness change.

## Why it's a trap

- **Stress cost is high.** Heart rate is well above easy. Glycogen burn rate is closer to threshold than to easy. Recovery cost per minute is closer to the hard end of the spectrum.
- **Adaptation yield is low.** Below the true threshold, so lactate clearance machinery isn't maximally stimulated. Above the easy zone, so capillarisation / mitochondrial biogenesis / fat-oxidation gains are blunted relative to the volume cost.
- **It crowds out both ends.** Time spent here is time you can't spend banking long easy aerobic volume (the 80% bias) or sharpening true high-intensity stimulus (the 20% bias).

## Where it lurks

- Self-selected "easy run" pace that creeps above VT (intensity blindness — most amateurs)
- Threshold runs done a little too easy
- VO2max sessions done a little too easy
- Marathon-pace work for runners whose MP sits right in this band (e.g., 3:30 marathoners — a real tension; see below)

## How to avoid it

- **Use HR to enforce the easy ceiling.** Don't trust pace or feel — both default to the black hole. See [hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard](hr-governs-easy-pace-governs-hard.md).
- **Use the 5-zone HR system's buffer gap.** Fitzgerald's Z2 ends at 89% LTHR; Z3 starts at 96% — the 90–95% window is deliberately empty so easy runs don't drift into threshold.
- **Run truly polarised.** When the plan says hard, run hard. When it says easy, run truly easy. No medium.

## The marathon-pace tension

MP for sub-elite runners (~4:30–5:00/km) sits right in the black hole. Two reconciliations:
1. **MP work is justified as marathon-specific neural/metabolic rehearsal**, not as a general aerobic stimulus — it earns its keep through specificity, not through being a smart "middle zone."
2. **Limit it.** Pfitz's 4 MP sessions across the build, not 4 per month.
