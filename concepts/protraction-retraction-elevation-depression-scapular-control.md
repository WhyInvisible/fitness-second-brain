# Scapular control: protraction, retraction, elevation, depression

The four scapular movements that show up in every cue for every bodyweight skill. Mastering them is the difference between "looks right" and "trains the right muscles".

## The four positions

- **Elevation** — scaps shrugged UP toward ears. Required at top of handstand (scaps as "earmuffs"), top of pull-up.
- **Depression** — scaps pulled DOWN away from ears. Required for L-sit, ring support, "packed shoulder" position in front lever / back lever / planche.
- **Protraction** — scaps spread WIDE (round forward). Required at top of pushup, bottom of straddle L-sit lean, in planche position.
- **Retraction** — scaps PINCHED back together. Required at top of row, top of pull-up finish, in scap pull-ups, in front lever pull.

A skill cue typically combines two: "depressed and neutral" or "elevated and slightly retracted" or "depressed and protracted".

## Why this matters

Many "intermediate" calisthenics athletes do skills that *look* correct but actually bypass the proper strength stimulus by using the wrong scapular position. Examples:

- **Rounded back in back lever** = scaps protracted = chest caves = the lats and posterior shoulder don't get loaded properly. Looks like a back lever, isn't building the strength a back lever should.
- **Protracted shoulders in front lever** = chest rounds forward = the strength comes from biceps and forearms instead of lats and posterior shoulder. Trains the wrong system, sets up a future plateau.
- **Elbows flared in HSPU** = recruits trapezius too much = bypasses the anterior delt + triceps loading the freestanding HSPU needs. Builds strength in wrong pattern that doesn't transfer.

## Scapular position by skill (from Low)

| Skill | Scap position |
|---|---|
| Wall / freestanding handstand | Elevated maximally + slightly retracted |
| Handstand pushup (descending) | Elevated → naturally depresses + protracts at bottom |
| L-sit | Depressed + neutral (no protraction, no retraction) |
| Straddle L-sit | Depressed + slightly protracted (lean forward) |
| Manna progression | Depressed + neutral throughout (avoid protracting) |
| Ring support hold | Depressed |
| Ring-turned-out (RTO) support | Depressed + slightly retracted |
| Back lever (all variants) | Depressed + neutral (NOT protracted = no rounded chest; NOT retracted = no arched back) |
| Front lever (all variants) | Depressed; must pull HARD to keep neutral (will drift to protracted if weak) |
| Front lever row (top) | Depressed + fully retracted |
| Pull-up (top) | Slightly retracted, depressed |
| Pushup (top) | Protracted (push hard through floor) |
| Pushup (bottom) | Naturally retracts |
| Row (top) | Fully retracted + depressed |
| Muscle-up transition | Elevated strongly at bottom of dip, depressed pushing out |

## The "scap pull-up" prerequisite

Before bar pull-ups, learn to **shrug down at the bar** without bending elbows. Hang from a bar with straight arms → depress scaps → body rises a few inches without arm bend → relax. 3 × 5–10. This wires the scapular depression that initiates every pull-up.

## The "active hang" cue

Most beginners hang with relaxed/elevated scaps. **Active hang** = depressed, "packed" shoulders. It's the starting position for all pull-up work, front lever, one-arm hang work, German hang variations.

## Practical drills

- **Scap pull-ups** (above)
- **Scap pushups** — high plank → let chest sag (protraction → retraction) → push back up (re-protract). 3 × 10. Wires the protraction at top of pushup that planche progressions demand.
- **Ring support hold** — 60 s with rings as close to neutral / RTO as you can. Trains depression + slight retraction.
- **German hang / skin the cat** — extreme shoulder extension, scap elevation. 5 reps per warm-up.
- **Band pull-aparts** — 3 × 20. Retraction wiring + posterior shoulder.
