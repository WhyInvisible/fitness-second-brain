# Spine-sparing daily life ("spine hygiene")

Per McGill Ch 8, the cumulative load on the spine across all 24 hours of the day determines whether tissue heals or re-sensitises. A 30-min training session matters less than the other 23 hours. "Spine hygiene" = a permanent practice of routing daily motion through hips and shoulders, keeping the spine in neutral.

## The 24/7 rules

- **Sitting** — lumbar support (rolled towel works). Pelvis rolled forward + chest lifted. Stand every ~20 min. Avoid couch slouch and bean-bag chairs. NEVER sit immediately after rising — discs are swollen, max injury risk.
- **Standing** — torso stacked, no chin poke, no shoulder round. Hands behind back (not crossed across chest — that cramps the low back).
- **Walking** — brisk pace, arms swinging from the shoulder (not the elbow), tall posture, larger steps. Slow walking statically loads the spine and worsens disc pain. Brisk walking with arm swing is therapeutic.
- **Bending** — hip-hinge default. Light objects → golfer's lift (one leg back as counterweight). Heavy → shortstop squat. Tying shoes → foot on bench, lunge hips towards foot.
- **Pushing/pulling** — direct the force line through the navel. Step around doors. Brace abs to the task.
- **Sneezing** — if flexion-intolerant, look up and brace; sneeze "upwards".
- **Sleeping** — firm mattress with cushion topper if curvy build, flat firm if angular. NO pillow under knees (flattens lumbar). Side-sleepers: pillow between knees.
- **Avoid** — lifting or spine-bending within ~1 hr of rising (discs hydrated, peak vulnerability). Long sustained postures (any single position > 20 min). Twisting under load.

## Why it matters

If the athlete has a lower-back injury history (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles) — for example disc/endplate damage from a heavy lift — that kind of injury can take up to 10 years to fully gristle. The disc remains vulnerable to cumulative load even when fully pain-free, years after the event. Daily-life mechanics are the difference between healing and re-aggravation. Office sitting + couch slouching + sloppy lifting of laundry + bent-over teeth-brushing add up to more spine load than any gym session.

## The default substitutions

| Old pattern | Spine-sparing substitute |
|---|---|
| Bend over to tie shoe | Foot on bench, lunge hips to foot |
| Slouch into the couch | Firm chair + lumbar pillow |
| Pick up sock by bending spine | Golfer's lift (one leg back) |
| Open heavy door with twisted torso | Step around, brace, direct force through navel |
| Brush teeth bent over sink | Hip-hinge + one hand on counter |
| Reach into car trunk | Brace + hip-hinge, not spine flexion |
| Vacuum one-handed across body | "Waltz" with the vacuum, force through spine |
| Sleep curled fetal | Side-lie with neutral lumbar + knee pillow |
| Lift large object by middle | Lift one end, then the other |
