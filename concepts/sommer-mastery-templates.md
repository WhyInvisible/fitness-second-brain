# Sommer's mastery templates (12-week cycle, deload every 4 weeks)

Sommer's standard dose-progression structure for every GST exercise. Each exercise comes with a **mastery target** (e.g. 5×60s, 5×5r, 5×5r×10s); the target determines which of seven mastery templates applies; the template then dictates the week-by-week dose across a 12-week cycle.

## The seven base templates (F1)

All templates share the same shape: build to mastery at week 11, deload every 4 weeks. The numbers scale with the target.

**60-second template** (e.g. FL/PE1 Bent Hollow Body Hold, mastery 5×60s):
| Wk | 1 | 2 | 3 | **4 dl** | 5 | 6 | 7 | **8 dl** | 9 | 10 | 11 | **12 dl** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dose | 3×12s | 5×12s | 3×24s | 3×12s | 4×24s | 4×36s | 5×36s | 5×18s | 4×48s | 4×60s | **5×60s** | 5×30s |

**30-second template**:
| Wk | 1 | 2 | 3 | **4 dl** | 5 | 6 | 7 | **8 dl** | 9 | 10 | 11 | **12 dl** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dose | 3×6s | 5×6s | 3×12s | 3×6s | 4×12s | 4×18s | 5×18s | 5×9s | 4×24s | 4×30s | **5×30s** | 5×15s |

**10-second template** (advanced iso — straddle planche, etc.):
| Wk | 1 | 2 | 3 | **4 dl** | 5 | 6 | 7 | **8 dl** | 9 | 10 | 11 | **12 dl** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dose | 3×2s | 5×2s | 3×4s | 3×2s | 4×4s | 4×6s | 5×6s | 5×3s | 4×8s | 4×10s | **5×10s** | 5×5s |

**60-rep, 15-rep, 10-rep, 5-rep templates** follow the same shape with reps instead of seconds.

**H1 adds 90-second and 120-second templates** for longer free handstand holds.

## The underlying shape

Every template:
- Wk 1 = 20 % of mastery target × 3 sets
- Wk 2 = same dose × 5 sets (volume up)
- Wk 3 = 40 % × 3 sets (intensity up)
- **Wk 4 = deload (back to wk 1)**
- Wk 5 = 40 % × 4 sets (volume up)
- Wk 6 = 60 % × 4 sets
- Wk 7 = 60 % × 5 sets (volume peak)
- **Wk 8 = deload (40 % × 5 sets, slight back-off)**
- Wk 9 = 80 % × 4 sets
- Wk 10 = 100 % × 4 sets
- **Wk 11 = 100 % × 5 sets (mastery week)**
- **Wk 12 = 50 % × 5 sets (deload before next exercise)**

This is structured wave-loading with built-in deload every 4 weeks — what periodisation literature would call a "linear with deload" or "step-loading with regular reset" mesocycle.

## F3's variant: 3-block self-select entry

F3 reformulates the 12-week template as **3 selectable blocks of 4 weeks each**, with the trainee picking the entry block based on current ability:

- **Block 1 (wk 1–4)** — "if you are not remotely close to mastery"
- **Block 2 (wk 5–8)** — "if you can do some sets, but not at mastery"
- **Block 3 (wk 9–12)** — "if you need more endurance to achieve mastery"

This is a more honest treatment of where trainees actually start. The week-1 dose of the 60s template (3×12s) is genuinely too easy for someone who can already hold 4×24s — they should start at week 5 or 9.

## How to use templates with multiple exercises

For each fundamental (FL, sPL, SL, MN, SLS, HBP, RC), you're typically running ONE exercise (the current PE you're trying to master). The template tells you what dose that exercise gets that week.

So Wk 6 of a 4-day cycle for someone working PE1 of all 7 fundamentals would look like:
- Mon: FL/PE1 4×36s, sPL/PE1 4×9r, HBP/PE1 4×9r, RC/PE1 4×9r (each with iM)
- Tue: MN/PE1 4×9r, SL/PE1 4×36s, SLS/PE1 4×9r (each with iM)
- Thu/Fri: repeat

Once an exercise is mastered (wk 11 dose hit clean), the next 12-week cycle starts at wk 1 with the next PE. The template doesn't change, just the exercise it's applied to.

## Why this structure works

- **Built-in deload every 4 weeks** prevents the cumulative-fatigue accident that derails most self-programmed calisthenics training.
- **The wk-8 mid-cycle deload** (back to 40 % × 5 sets) is the secret ingredient — it lets the wk 9–11 push happen with fresh CNS + connective tissue.
- **Wk 12 deload at 50 %** primes the next exercise — you don't crash into a new harder progression from peak fatigue.
- **The same shape across all seven templates** makes programming portable. You always know "wk 5 is when volume climbs" regardless of whether the rep scheme is reps or seconds.

## Contrast with other source's mesocycle structures

- **Low (Overcoming Gravity)**: 4–8 week mesocycle + 1 deload week. Lower-rep flexibility, more concurrent training in one mesocycle. Sommer's 12-week structure is more rigid but has more built-in recovery.
- **Matthews (BLS)**: 8–10 weeks of heavy lifting + 1 deload. Hypertrophy-oriented. Different rep ranges entirely (4–6 reps barbell vs Sommer's 5–60 reps/seconds bodyweight).
- **Pavel (S&S)**: Step-loading via "own the weight" gates, not calendar-based weeks. Different model entirely (uses kettlebell weight as the discrete progression unit, not reps/sets).

## Practical translation

**During a marathon block**: don't adopt. The 12-week cycle is rigid and would compete with running periodisation.

**Off-season, after the A-race (once on Sommer's curriculum)**: this becomes the way every PE/SE is dosed. Pick the template, enter at the right block (wk 1 if a full beginner to the exercise, wk 5 if partially capable, wk 9 if close to mastery), follow the template strictly.

**Synchronisation note**: if all seven fundamentals are on 12-week cycles starting from different points (which they will be, because you mastered some PEs faster than others), the deload weeks naturally desynchronise. This is OK — Sommer's framework doesn't require globally-aligned deloads. The per-exercise dose is what matters.
