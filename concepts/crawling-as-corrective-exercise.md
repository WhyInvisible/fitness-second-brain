# Crawling as corrective + restorative exercise

Per Le Corre Ch 24 (Ground Movement 1), quadrupedal crawling — the foot-hand "bear crawl", knee-hand crawl, forearm crawl, inverted "crab" crawl, and their rotational variants — is among the highest-leverage and most under-used corrective movements in the adult repertoire. It's how infants learn movement; it should remain part of the adult vocabulary because the same patterns are still corrective + restorative.

## Why crawling is corrective

1. **Trains cross-body coordination.** The contralateral crawl pattern (right hand + left foot, then left hand + right foot) is the same pattern as running. Crawling re-wires the cross-body firing sequence at low intensity, with no impact, with low load. (Cross-source with Dicharry — the bear-walk + cross-crawl is his go-to anti-rotation core warm-up.)
2. **Brakes the lumbar.** With body weight distributed across four limbs + neutral spine, the lumbar bears far less compressive load than in any standing position. Crawl pattern trains spine-stabiliser endurance with minimal compression — useful for back-history populations (cross-source with McGill's [core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion](core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion.md)).
3. **Trains shoulder + thoracic + hip mobility under load.** The foot-hand crawl loads the shoulder in protraction + scapular stability under weight; the knee-hand crawl loads hip flexion + extension cycling; both load thoracic rotation. Mobility *with* stability — the missing piece in static stretching.
4. **Develops grip + wrist strength.** Open-hand foot-hand crawl loads the wrist in slight extension under body weight — direct prep for handstand prerequisites and a corrective for desk-induced wrist tightness.
5. **Restorative effect on the spine.** The flexion-extension cycling at low load (the "alternating arches" of the spine through each crawl step) is the lumbar-friendly version of cat-camel. Decompresses + lubricates without end-range stretching.
6. **Mind-body engagement.** Done correctly, crawling demands attention to spine alignment, hip rotation, hand placement, breathing, sequence + timing — *all five* of Le Corre's efficiency mechanics simultaneously. High cognitive engagement at low metabolic cost.

## The crawl family

| Variant | Position | When useful |
|---|---|---|
| **Forearm crawl** (army crawl) | Belly on ground, weight on forearms + knees | Confined spaces; rehab from injury; back-friendliest crawl |
| **Knee-hand crawl** | Knees + hands on ground | Beginner; mobility focus; pre-bear-crawl drill |
| **Foot-hand crawl (bear)** | Toes + hands, hips up, knees floating | Anti-rotation core; shoulder + scapular stability; full-body coordination |
| **Inverted foot-hand crawl (crab)** | Belly up, weight on feet + hands behind | Shoulder mobility; reverse cross-pattern; deficit-corrective |
| **Rotational knee-forearm crawl** | Lying-prone with hip rotation drive | Hip mobility under load; abdominal anti-rotation |
| **Lateral crawl** | Bear-crawl sideways | Hip abductor / adductor work |

**Le Corre's 4/3 vs 4/2 points-of-support distinction:** the 4/3 pattern (always 3 of 4 limbs grounded between moves) is stable + accessible. The 4/2 pattern (briefly only 2 contralateral limbs grounded — opposite hand + foot) is harder, more athletic, more representative of running's single-leg-flight pattern. Master 4/3 first; 4/2 is the progression target.

## Doses

- **As warm-up filler:** 30 steps forward + 30 backward of the bear crawl. ~60 sec. Cross-source with Dicharry.
- **As corrective block:** 5 min daily of mixed-variant crawling — 1 min each of forearm / knee-hand / bear / inverted-bear / rotational. ~5 min. Replaces or supplements existing mobility warm-up.
- **As stand-alone practice:** longer durations (10–20 min) with progressions for distance, speed, surface complexity, load (vest), direction changes. Le Corre's full progression menu.

## Application

- **During a marathon build:** add a 5 min daily mixed-variant crawl block as part of a morning routine or post-easy-run cool-down. If the athlete has any of these in [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles, it addresses them: hamstring asymmetry (hip rotation under load), shoulder twinge (scapular control + thoracic mobility), back history (no compression, lumbar braced). Cost: trivial. Benefit: significant. See exercises/quadrupedal-crawl.
- **Travel / no-gym phases:** crawl on varied surfaces (grass, sand, dirt, gravel) as part of the daily natural-movement practice. Surfaces are part of the training — Le Corre's complexity axis.
- **Off-season / calisthenics block:** crawl is the gateway to floor-to-handstand transitions; integrate fully with the calisthenics work.

## Cross-source

- **Dicharry's bear walk** (exercises/bear-walk) is the cross-crawl pattern Le Corre calls Foot-Hand Crawl 4/3. Same exercise, different terminology. Cross-link.
- **McGill's [core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion](core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion.md)** principle is *operationally identical* to what crawling trains — spine neutral, limbs cycle, abdominal brace. Le Corre's crawling chapter is essentially McGill core philosophy in motion.
- **Steven Low (Overcoming Gravity)** uses crawling as a foundation strength + structural-balance drill. Same content, calisthenics framing.

## What it's NOT

- It's not a cardio session. Don't chase HR with crawling — wrong tool.
- It's not a strength session. Don't add load until movement quality is owned at bodyweight.
- It's not a stretch. Don't push end-range positions; crawl is rhythmic + mid-range.
- It's not animal-flow choreography. Le Corre is explicit: this is *practical movement*, not aesthetic performance. Don't make it dance.
