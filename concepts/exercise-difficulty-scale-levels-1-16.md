# Exercise difficulty scale: Levels 1–16

Steven Low's universal 16-level scale gives calisthenics what barbells have: a coherent "weight on the bar" measure. Built on the FIG Code of Points (gymnastics rulebook). Lets you place any pushing, pulling, leg, isometric, or skill movement on the same ladder and pick what to do next.

## The four brackets

| Bracket | Levels | Marker skills |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | 1–5 | Wall handstand, pushups, dips, pull-ups, ring rows, tuck FL, tuck BL, frog stand, **strict ring muscle-up with false grip (L5)** |
| Intermediate | 6–9 | Freestanding handstand (L5/6), freestanding HSPU (L7), straddle press handstand, full BL (L7), full FL (L8), straddle planche (L8), V-sit, one-arm chin-up eccentrics (L8) |
| Advanced | 10–13 | Full planche, **one-arm chin-up (L9)**, iron cross (L10), one-arm handstand (L10), manna (L13), weighted dips ~2× BW |
| Elite | 14–16 | Full planche on rings, victorian, maltese, inverted cross, butterfly mount |

## Why a level system matters

Without one, calisthenics writers have to gesture vaguely ("get strong, then do a planche"). With one, you can look at a skill goal, find your current level, and see exactly which progression is one rung below your target. You also see the prerequisites — connective tissue and balance demands that don't show up if you only think about "what muscles work this".

Crucially: **time spent training matters very little; ability level achieved matters a lot.** Two athletes with the same goal but different starting levels need very different programmes.

## Worked example: placing an endurance athlete on the scale (hypothetical)

A runner who has lifted casually but never trained gymnastic skills usually lands mostly in Beginner — Levels 1–4, with zeros on the skills:
- **Pull-up:** roughly L3 if they can do ~8–12 strict reps
- **Dip:** roughly L3 at ~12–15 reps
- **Pushup:** L1–2
- **Pistol, handstand, front lever, muscle-up:** L0 (not yet trained)
- **L-sit:** L0–1 (tuck possible)

A realistic 12-week off-season block from that start (record the athlete's own targets in [goals.md](../goals.md)):
- Handstand: toward L5 (freestanding 10–30 s hold)
- Muscle-up: toward L5 (strict ring MU with false grip — first clean rep)
- L-sit: L3 (full L-sit, 20 s)
- Pull-up: hold L3+ while building chest-to-bar volume
- Pistol: L4 (single clean rep each side)

## How to use the level system

1. **Pick 1–2 goal skills per category** (push, pull, legs, skill). Don't try to chase planche AND iron cross AND one-arm chin-up simultaneously — too much CNS load, too many connective tissue demands, too little practice per skill.
2. **Find the lowest progression in your goal that you cannot yet do for 5+ clean reps.** That's your current working level.
3. **Train at that level for 4–8 weeks** (a mesocycle), with progressive linear addition of reps/sets.
4. **Move up when you hit 8+ clean reps for 2 consecutive sessions.** This is the [earn-the-next-progression](earn-the-next-progression.md) rule.
