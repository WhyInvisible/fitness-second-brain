# The handstand is the calisthenics squat

The one skill that should be practiced almost **every day** for anyone who wants real proficiency in bodyweight strength. Like the squat for general human movement, it's the universal proxy: if your handstand is bad, every other upper-body bodyweight skill suffers downstream.

## Why daily practice (the exception to skill-work-when-fresh)

For most skills, [skill-work-when-fresh](skill-work-when-fresh.md) means 2×/wk max. The handstand is different because:

1. Once basic strength is acquired (Level 3–4: can hold 30+ s wall handstand), it's largely a **balance + skill** movement, not a strength movement.
2. Balance and proprioception adapt only with high frequency. Strength can recover in 48 h; balance memory needs daily reinforcement.
3. Wall handstand can be practiced with low fatigue cost (5–10 min, mostly endurance-level intensity once the hold is comfortable).

**Caveats:**
- "Daily" applies to **wall handstand** + light skill work, not to freestanding kick-ups to failure.
- Days of high overall fatigue (post-long-run, poor sleep) → skip or do only 2-min mobility.
- Wrist soreness = back off; wrists need adaptation time too.

## The bell-curve rule (Low's quality-over-PR rule)

Performance on skill movements falls into a bell curve. If you do 10 handstand attempts and the times are 0/2/4/4/4/4/4/4/8/10 seconds, **your handstand is a 4-second hold, not a 10-second hold**.

Practical consequence: focus on raising your **consistent** hold (the median), not chasing PR outliers. Ten clean 5-second holds > one lucky 12-second hold + nine failures.

## The wall-handstand technique checklist

Reference cues for wall handstand (stomach-to-wall, the recommended beginner variant). These cues apply to every later handstand variant too.

- Hands shoulder-width, 2–6 inches from wall
- Stomach (NOT back) to wall — teaches correct alignment from day 1
- Arms locked straight, pushed tall (shoulders as "earmuffs", scaps maximally elevated then very slightly retracted)
- T-spine fully extended (chest "proud")
- Pelvis slightly **posteriorly rotated** (glute squeeze + ab brace = kills the lower-back arch)
- Legs neutral, knees locked, toes pointed, legs squeezed together
- Cambered hand grip (palm + fingertips + heel of palm = three contact points on floor)
- Fingers spread wide

## The correction rule

Small forward-balance corrections come from the **fingertips digging in**. Small backward corrections come from **distributing weight through the palm**. Do NOT correct with the shoulders or hips — that creates wobbling and wires bad habits. (After Low, *Overcoming Gravity*, Ch 24.)

## How to bail out

Two methods:
1. **Roll out** (preferred for stomach-to-wall): bend arms, tuck chin to chest, roll forward like a gymnast forward roll.
2. **Pirouette** (twist out sideways): only after roll-out is mastered. Don't start with this; it teaches "when scared, bail" rather than "fight for the position".

**Rule: always fight for every inch before bailing.** This builds the strength-stability of the position and reinforces the right habit: handstand is something you correct *into*, not something you bail *from*.

## Progression (Low's levels)

| Level | Skill | Target before advancing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wall handstand (stomach-to-wall), feet barely touching | 30 s hold |
| 2 | Wall handstand, split feet (one foot off wall) | 30 s |
| 3 | Wall handstand, both feet off | 15–20 s |
| 4 | Freestanding kick-up + brief hold | 5 s consistent |
| 5 | **Freestanding handstand** (the bodyweight Level 5 goal) | 30 s |
| 6–9 | 4/3/2/1-finger assist freestanding | 30 s each stage |
| 10 | One-arm handstand | 5+ s |

## Active when?

**Maintenance during a marathon block** if the athlete has any handstand base — wall handstand stages 0–1 (toes against wall, brief unsupported holds) once or twice a week, 5 min total. Cost is trivial, decay if abandoned is rapid.

**Primary focus after the A-race.** Once marathon volume drops, freestanding handstand (Level 5) is a natural headline goal for a post-race calisthenics block (muscle-up is the usual partner goal). Check the athlete's priorities in [goals.md](../goals.md).
