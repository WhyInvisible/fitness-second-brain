# Step loading — own the weight before you jump to the next

Per Pavel Pt II ("Step Loading for Maximal Reliability"), step loading is a progression model in which the load stays **constant** for a defined block (e.g. 4 weeks), then **jumps in a meaningful discrete step** (Pavel: +8 kg for men, +4 kg for women in S&S), then stays constant again. **No micro-loading, no weekly waving, no deload-by-default.** Originated with Soviet sports scientists Matveev, Ozolin, Platonov; sister-concept to Christopher Sommer's "steady state" gymnastics progression model.

## The structure

```
Load
 |
 |              ___________
 |             |
 |  ___________|
 | |
 |_|___________________________ Time
   step 1         step 2
   (4 weeks)     (4 weeks)
```

vs. **wave loading** (intensities/volumes vary every week within a block):

```
Load
 |    /\    /\    /\
 |   /  \  /  \  /  \
 |  /    \/    \/    \
 |_/__________________________ Time
   light heavy light heavy
```

Wave loading needs intentional light days because intentional heavy days deplete you. Step loading does NOT need light days because there are no intentional heavy days within a block — every session is the same "comfortably hard" effort.

## The rule for advancing

Pavel's rule, paraphrased: pick a comfortably hard training load (weight, sets, reps, rest periods) and keep it until it feels almost easy — until you "own" it. Only then move up. (Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*, Pt II, pp. 132–135)

"Owning a weight" = perfect form, any day, without psychological dread, across the full prescribed sets/reps.

In Pavel's S&S, this is operationalised as:
- 4-week step duration
- 1-bell jump (8 kg for men / 4 kg for women)
- Phase the upgrade in: start the heavier bell at set 3, work to set 10, then upgrade sets 1–2 last. ~16–20 weeks to fully upgrade one bell.
- Half-step option for the get-up (4 kg jumps + 2-week steps) if the full jump psyches the athlete out.

## Why big discrete jumps > continuous micro-loading

Pavel's five reasons (citing Vorobyev, Dan John, Mark Toomey, Russian powerlifting coaches):

1. **Stronger strength signal.** "Sharp changes in load are superior to baby steps when delivering the message: get strong." (Vorobyev) — discrete changes match how the nervous system actually adapts.
2. **No tyranny of choice.** Russian KB sets = 16/24/32/40/48 kg. No 17 kg, no 18, no 19. The brain doesn't burn calories choosing; it spends them on the work.
3. **Robs hypertrophy-only progress.** A 1 kg/week increase lets you "sneak up" on a heavier bell through muscle mass alone — robbing you of technical mastery a-ha moments.
4. **Forces ownership.** You can't get to the next bell except by dominating the current one. No fudging.
5. **Bigger lift = bigger person.** Wins against heavy weights produce psychological growth that incremental gains never do.

## Why no deload by default

> "If you do not overload, you do not have to deload."

Wave-loading systems (Westside, 5/3/1, conjugate) require frequent deloads because the wave's heavy days deplete CNS. Step loading's "comfortably hard" effort never does that. Deload only when:
- **Progress stalls** at the current step (drop down a bell or two for a few weeks, then build back).
- **You've taken time off** (illness, travel, life).
- **Physical, mental, or emotional stress** is unusually high in non-training life.

## How it differs from "progressive overload"

[progressive-overload](progressive-overload.md) is the generic principle that load must increase over time. Step loading is a SPECIFIC progression METHOD that implements progressive overload without:
- continuous micro-additions (e.g. 0.5 kg/week)
- intentional within-block intensity waves
- programmed deload weeks
- "AMRAP" / max-rep testing each week

It's the *patient, boring, reliable* form of progressive overload.

## How it differs from "earn the next progression"

[earn-the-next-progression](earn-the-next-progression.md) — a calisthenics principle — IS structurally a step-loading model: hold the current progression until you own a target rep-quality threshold, then move to the next progression. Pavel's KB step loading is the kettlebell-specific instance of the same general principle.

## Practical translation

For a multi-discipline athlete with a marathon as the A-race, step loading is the most sustainable strength progression model. Why:
- Wave loading needs CNS bandwidth to recover from heavy days — bandwidth a marathoner spends on long runs and threshold work.
- Step loading sessions are consistent low-grind moderate-power efforts that don't compete with running.
- The patient timeline (e.g. 16 kg → 24 kg over 4–6 months) matches the slow timeline of back-tissue rehabilitation ([back-tissue-heals-over-years](back-tissue-heals-over-years.md)), which matters if the athlete has a back history (see [profile.md](../profile.md)).
- No deload weeks to coordinate with the running plan's cutback weeks.

Hypothetical example: an athlete who has owned 16 kg swings and 12 kg get-ups gets a 24 kg bell. Don't immediately program it. Instead:
- Weeks 1–4: 16 kg, 10×10 one-arm swings + 12 kg, 5/5 get-ups (own it)
- Weeks 5–8: 16 kg sets 1–2 + 24 kg sets 3–10 swings; 12 kg sets 1–2 + 16 kg sets 3–5 get-ups
- Weeks 9–12: 24 kg, all 10 swings sets; 16 kg, all 5 get-up sets (the new "owned" base)

## Quote anchor

- Pavel quotes old-school powerlifter John McKean to the effect that simply staying with constant weights for long stretches is the easiest way to enjoy training, stay injury-free and keep your sanity, without complicated percentage formulas. (Paraphrase; Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*, Pt II)

> "Step loading is an adult training progression. It weeds out impatient juveniles unable to stay on task." (Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*, Pt II)

> "You are still making progress when you are repeating the same workload over and over... You are 'solidifying your gains.'" (Pavel, *Simple & Sinister*, Pt II)
