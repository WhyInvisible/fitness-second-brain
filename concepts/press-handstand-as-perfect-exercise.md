# Press handstand — "the most perfect of all GST exercises"

Sommer's claim at the opening of Handstand Two: the press to handstand (lifting from a seated / piked position into a freestanding handstand without kipping or jumping) is the single most complete GST exercise — strength, mobility, balance, and agility integrated in one movement over a large ROM. For Sommer, mastering free handstand + press handstand is the gateway to advanced ring strength (Rings Two and beyond).

## The claim

Sommer calls press handstands "the most perfect of all GST exercises" because they combine strength, mobility, balance and agility in one movement. For beginner-to-advanced-beginner athletes he makes them the core of the conditioning programme, to be prioritised above every other exercise at that stage, ring strength included (Sommer, *Mastering Gymnastic Strength Training: Handstand Two*, "The Perfect Exercise").

And: "for the bodyweight enthusiast press handstands are the upper body snatch of bodyweight movements."

## What makes press HS distinctive

A free handstand is essentially a balance skill once basic strength is built. A muscle-up is a strength + transition skill. A press handstand fuses:
- **Vertical pressing strength** (full overhead extension under bodyweight)
- **Compression strength** (active pulling of legs to torso — see [l-sit-to-manna-progression-as-structural-antidote](l-sit-to-manna-progression-as-structural-antidote.md))
- **Scapular protraction + elevation throughout the ROM** (the "stacked" position cue)
- **Spinal articulation** (curling up "one vertebrae at a time" like a reverse Jefferson curl)
- **Upper-back stability** (massive trap engagement to maintain alignment as legs extend forward)
- **Active flexibility** (hamstring / hip / shoulder mobility under load)
- **Static balance** in the final handstand position

No other single bodyweight exercise demands all of these simultaneously over the same ROM. Hence "the most perfect."

## The five technique pillars (H2)

Sommer's H2 opens with five short essays defining correct execution:

1. **Stacked** — at every point, the body parts are in a single vertical line: feet over knees over hips over shoulders over elbows over wrists. The fewer angles, the less stabiliser muscle needed, the cleaner the press.

2. **Elevate** — scapula are both **protracted AND elevated** throughout. To learn the feel: stand up, shrug shoulders to ears, place arms overhead while maintaining the shrug. That's the cue.

3. **Compress** — actively pull legs into torso. Compression = active flexibility, not passive flexibility. Built via MN/SE1>iM and MN/SE2>iM (L-sit and straddle-L compression drills, ideally with hips/back pressed against wall to increase difficulty).

4. **One vertebrae at a time** — the back curls up like "a string of pearls unrolling." Never flat, never arched — a continuous spinal unroll. Think reverse Jefferson curl.

5. **Upper back** — expect massive trap cramps. The upper back is the alignment-maintenance system as the legs extend forward and gravity tries to pull the press apart. Sommer is explicit: the upper-back development built by press handstand is what allows advanced ring strength later.

## Sommer's prerequisite gate

> "Do not however begin training the press handstand specific elements (pHS/SE) exercises until you have mastered the 5×30s free HS from H1 (HS/SE7)."
> — Sommer, H2

You can begin the press handstand **preparatory elements (pHS/PE)** — plank press, tip toe press, etc. — alongside H1 work. But press handstand SE work waits for 30 s free handstand mastery. This is the gate.

Beyond that: continue mastering free HS to 60 s before considering one-arm handstand work.

## Why press handstand is the gateway to advanced rings

Sommer's claim — implicit but built across H2 and the GB curriculum — is that:
- Advanced ring strength (iron cross, butterfly mount, Maltese, Victorian) requires upper-back / trap strength developed in press handstand work
- Without press handstand, your ring strength journey "begins and ends at Rings One"
- The press handstand is more accessible than the ring skills (no rings required) but builds the same scapular-protracted, elevated, fully-active-flexibility pattern

So press HS → Rings Two + beyond. Press HS comes first.

## Practical application

**During a marathon block, or before free handstand is solid**: not active. Way too early in the progression.

**After 30 s free handstand is mastered**: pHS/PE work becomes the natural next handstand goal. The progression begins with Plank Press (pHS/PE1, 5×5r) and Tip Toe Press (pHS/PE2, 5×5r) — both can be done at home with parallettes or just a floor.

**Long arc**: press handstand is a Year 3–4 milestone in the GST timeline (per [time-is-the-most-potent-training-supplement](time-is-the-most-potent-training-supplement.md)). Not something to chase in the first off-season after starting handstand work.

**One useful immediate import**: the "stacked / elevate / compress / one vertebrae at a time / upper back" framework is the right way to think about *any* press-to-handstand attempt, even from a wall-supported partial press. Adopt these cues whenever experimenting with hip-lift-to-handstand patterns.
