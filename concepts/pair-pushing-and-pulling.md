# Pair pushing and pulling 1:1 (shoulder is the lynchpin)

Roughly equal volume each week prevents shoulder imbalances. Steven Low's first calisthenics axiom: **"Keeping the shoulders and shoulder blades operating optimally is the key to bodyweight strength success."** Second axiom: **"The shoulder is the lynchpin of the upper body, just as the hip is for the lower body."**

## Why this matters for calisthenics specifically

Bodyweight programming defaults to push-dominant (dips + push-ups + handstand pushups + planche progressions are all anterior-shoulder heavy). Without conscious pull pairing, athletes develop:
- Forward shoulder posture
- Anterior impingement
- Labrum wear (long-term)
- Force-output inhibition (body limits force when joint instability sensed)

Low's own anterior-shoulder injury in college was the trigger that pushed Low into PT school and made structural balance the philosophical foundation of *Overcoming Gravity*.

## Why this matters for runners specifically

Running posture trends forward (shoulders rolled, T-spine flexed). Adding push-dominant strength (bench press, dips, push-ups) without matching pull volume worsens it. Result: shoulder dysfunction, such as a recurring shoulder twinge at the start of upper-body sessions (log any in [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles; escalation ladder in [MOC-injuries-management](../MOCs/MOC-injuries-management.md)).

## The 1:1 pairing rule

- **Vertical push : vertical pull** = 1:1 (OHP / handstand / HSPU : pull-up / front lever)
- **Horizontal push : horizontal pull** = 1:1 (bench / push-up / planche : row / back lever)
- If time forces a choice this week, **choose pull**.

## Low's recommended pairings (the structural-balance shortcuts)

Three pairings that cover the bases efficiently:

1. **Handstand + L-sit → V-sit → manna progression** ([l-sit-to-manna-progression-as-structural-antidote](l-sit-to-manna-progression-as-structural-antidote.md)) — the gold-standard pairing. Handstand covers vertical push + overhead flexion + anterior shoulder; manna progression covers scap retractors + posterior delts + external rotators + shoulder extension end-range + compression. Two tools, all bases covered.
2. **Dips + pull-ups** — for beginners, the workhorse pairing. Both compound, both build foundational strength.
3. **Planche progression + front lever progression** — for intermediates, the strength-skill push/pull mirror.

## What "1:1" means in practice

Per category. So weekly pull-up volume should equal weekly dip volume; weekly row volume should equal weekly push-up volume. NOT total push reps = total pull reps (vertical and horizontal aren't interchangeable).

## The scap-control overlap

Pairing push and pull is necessary but not sufficient. You also need the right scapular positions trained: protraction (pushing) AND retraction (pulling) AND depression (isometric holds). See [protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control](protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control.md).

## Active when?

ALWAYS. Even maintenance sessions during a marathon block should track push:pull 1:1.
