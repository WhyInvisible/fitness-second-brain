# Spine stiffness (not flexibility); hip + shoulder mobility

Per McGill (Ch 2), mobility is needed at the shoulders and hips, while "Most back pain is best addressed by stiffening, not mobilizing the spine when performing tasks that impose spine load."

## The principle

The body has joints designed for MOBILITY (shoulders, hips, ankles, T-spine, wrists) and joints designed for STIFFNESS / stability (lumbar spine, knees, elbows). Use them according to design.

Common rehab failure: treating the lumbar spine like a mobility joint and chasing flexibility / range-of-motion through stretching. This worsens disc problems by repeatedly bending an already-sensitised structure.

McGill's reversal: the lumbar spine should be STIFF (via [abdominal-brace](abdominal-brace.md), via the Big 3, via spine hygiene). Mobility belongs at the HIPS (where the bending should happen) and at the THORACIC spine (where rotation should happen) and at the SHOULDERS (where overhead motion should happen).

## Joint-by-joint summary

| Joint | Designed for | Training priority |
|---|---|---|
| Lumbar spine | Stiffness / stability | Maintain neutral, avoid bending under load. Big 3 + brace. |
| Hips | Mobility + power | Active mobility work (hip airplane, walking lunge, clamshell). Loaded hinges (trap-bar, hip thrust). |
| Thoracic spine | Rotation + mild flexion/extension | Dicharry's basketball mobility + Travolta peanuts. Cat-camel. |
| Shoulders | Mobility | Posture Fix routine. Overhead carry. Wall-walking. |
| Cervical spine | Slight mobility, mostly neutral | Avoid chin poke, posture work. |
| Knees | Stability + hinge | Single-leg work, no twisting under load. |
| Ankles | Mobility (dorsiflexion) | Ankle belt mobility. Dicharry's hardware-block protocol. |

## How this reconciles with Dicharry

[Dicharry's "flexibility vs mobility — three causes of stiffness"](flexibility-vs-mobility-three-stiffness-causes.md) is about HOW to diagnose stiffness (blocked joint vs short tissue vs fascial adhesion vs cold tissue). It applies primarily to mobility joints (hips, ankles, T-spine, shoulders).

McGill's "spine = stiffness, hips/shoulders = mobility" is about WHERE to target which property. It's an upstream principle.

They're complementary. Use Dicharry's framework when working on hip / ankle / T-spine / shoulder mobility (those joints SHOULD be mobile). Apply McGill's principle to the lumbar spine (where flexibility is NOT the goal — stability is).

## What this rules in / out

- **In:** abdominal bracing, McGill Big 3, hip airplane, walking lunge, Posture Fix routine, ankle belt mobility, T-spine mobility on a basketball, suitcase carry.
- **Out:** lumbar stretching (knees-to-chest, child's pose with extreme flexion), Pilates "imprinting", yoga poses that twist the lumbar to end-range.

## Worked example: a one-sided tight hamstring

A hamstring asymmetry (one side tighter than the other — check [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles) is the classic test case. Standard advice: "stretch the tight hamstring more." Per McGill (Ch 1 myth-bust): tight hamstrings are usually a symptom of back issues, not a cause. The fix is NOT more hamstring stretching — it's:
1. Restore lumbar STABILITY (Big 3 + brace).
2. Restore hip mobility (especially the stiffer hip — McGill notes "stiff hips, particularly on one side" is the BEST single predictor of future back disorders).
3. Asymmetric strength work (single-leg deadlift, single-leg squat).
4. If hamstring length remains an issue AFTER 1–3 are addressed, THEN apply Dicharry's 3-min stretch protocol.
