# Quadratus lumborum (QL) training — the under-trained spine carrier

Per McGill UBF Ch 3, Ch 5, Ch 13. The QL runs from the iliac crest to the 12th rib + L1-L4 transverse processes. It's the **frontal-plane spine stabiliser** — and the muscle most strength programs ignore because it doesn't show in conventional bilateral lifts.

## Why QL matters

- **"It is interesting that people with a paralyzed Quadratus cannot walk."** (UBF Ch 3, p. 76.) QL hikes the pelvis on the swing-leg side to let the foot clear during gait. Without it, gait collapses.
- For runners: QL endurance = **lateral stability of the running stride**. Weak QL = pelvic drop on each foot strike = inefficient gait + lateral hip + IT-band stress + asymmetric lumbar torsion (see UBF marathon-runner case study, p. 300).
- The **strongman super-yoke study** (McGill et al. 2009): a world-ranked strongman tested 500 Nm hip abduction strength in isolation, but generated **750 Nm under the super yoke** load. The "missing" 250 Nm came from QL + obliques lifting the hip to allow leg swing. **Functional strength under load > isolated strength.**

## Why QL is missed by conventional programs

- **Bilateral barbell lifts (squat, deadlift, bench)** load the QL symmetrically — both sides contract equally → no challenge to its anti-lateral-flexion role.
- **Side-bridge** trains it, but most programs use bridges briefly as warm-up not as a strength target.
- **Asymmetric loading** is the QL's natural challenge — and most gym programs don't include asymmetric load.

## The training principle

**QL fires hardest under asymmetric load**, specifically when the body wants to bend toward the load and the QL on the opposite side fights to keep the spine vertical. So:

> QL training = put a load in ONE hand, walk, stay vertical.

This is the **suitcase carry** + variations.

## The QL training hierarchy

| Exercise | QL load | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Side bridge** | Low-moderate | Anti-lateral-flexion isometric; foundational |
| **Suitcase carry** | Moderate | Dynamic anti-lateral-flexion under gait; transfers to running |
| **One-arm overhead carry** | Moderate-high | QL stabilises while shoulder is loaded overhead — high spine compression, deserves robust trunk |
| **Bottoms-up kettlebell carry** | Moderate-high | Lower load but the BU position destabilises — QL + obliques + grip + shoulder all fire |
| **Single-arm waiter carry** | Moderate | One hand at shoulder height — different vector, different challenge |
| **Super yoke (strongman)** | Maximum | Walking under crushing bilateral load — Strongman event, elite only |

## Programming

QL training fits the carries most strength programs already include (suitcase, farmer, overhead, waiter). UBF's contribution is to **name the QL as the primary target** + frame the protocol:

**Weekly minimum (during a marathon build):**
- Suitcase carry **2-3×/week**, integrated into strength or warm-up / prep sessions.
- 3-4 × 30 s per side, moderate load (24-32 kg KB or DB).
- "Hold yourself completely vertical — counter the body's tendency to lean toward the load."

**Progressions:**
- Heavier load (strength budget allowing).
- Longer carries (45-60 s).
- Walking on a slight incline.
- Add bottoms-up KB carry (2 × 20 s/side) for the destabilised variant.

**Form cues:**
- Shoulders packed down (anti-shrug — uses lats to stabilise the load, takes shoulder out of the equation).
- Ribcage stacked over pelvis (no lateral lean).
- Full natural strides (NOT short and choppy — full hip extension on the loaded side keeps QL working through its range).
- Breathe normally throughout (don't hold breath — this is endurance work, not max strength).

## Running-specific link

UBF's marathon-runner case study (Ch 16, p. 300): asymmetric lumbar torsion at foot strike is a common cause of back pain in long-distance runners. **Often the cause is QL weakness on the opposite side.** Strong QL = pelvis stays level during stance phase = less lumbar twist = less back stress = better running economy.

If the athlete has a one-sided hamstring asymmetry (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles): suitcase carry with the load in the hand opposite the affected side may need extra volume (asymmetric attack on the asymmetric pattern). Or just balance both sides until the asymmetry resolves and then re-test.

## Self-test

Stand on one leg. Hold for 30 s. Does the opposite hip DROP (pelvic dip) or stay LEVEL? Dropped = QL on the supporting side is weak. Level = adequate.

In practice: self-test standing on the right leg vs the left leg in front of a mirror, watching the L+R hip levels.
