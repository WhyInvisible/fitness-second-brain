# Cross-training as a volume amplifier (not just an injury fallback)

Fitzgerald's reframe: don't treat cross-training as the thing you do *when injured*. Treat it as the thing that lets you train *more total volume* than your skeleton can absorb as running. Same fitness, less impact.

## The case

- **Flynn 1998 (Purdue)** — 20 runners, +3 sessions/wk for 6 weeks. Group A added runs; Group B added stationary-bike sessions. Both groups improved 5K time by ~2.5%. **Cross-training transferred to running performance about as well as additional running did.**
- **The injury data** — triathletes in a 1998 Staffordshire study had >60% of injuries come from the running discipline despite training three sports. Running is the high-impact bottleneck on volume.
- **Meb Keflezighi** — late-career adopted aggressive cross-training (morning run + afternoon cross). At 38, won Boston in 2:08:37 on ~7 runs + 7 cross-training sessions/wk, defeating the previous year's top-2 marathoners.

## Two approaches

| | Minimalist | Aggressive |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-training sessions/wk | 1 | 3–10 |
| Running sessions/wk | unchanged (5–12) | reduced to 3–7 |
| Increase in total volume | +20% | +50–100% |
| Suits | durable runners, time-constrained, "dip a toe" | injury-prone, >35 yrs old, returning from injury, performance-maximisers |

## The specificity ranking (Fitzgerald Ch 12, Table 8.2)

Best running-carryover → worst:
1. **Antigravity treadmill** (Alter-G) — A grade for specificity, D for accessibility ($25k/machine)
2. **Outdoor elliptical bike** (ElliptiGO) — B+ specificity, A enjoyableness
3. **Uphill treadmill walking** — B+ specificity, easy to do
4. **Indoor elliptical** — B specificity, but boring
5. **Slideboarding** — C+ specificity, hits often-weak hip stabilisers
6. **Cycling** — B− specificity, B+ enjoyableness
7. **Pool running** — B− specificity, D enjoyableness (but lifesaver for stress fractures)

Swimming is *not* on the list — upper-body dominant, poor carryover. French 2002 study: bike volume correlated with run performance; swim volume did not.

## Critical rule: apply 80/20 to TOTAL training, not per discipline

If you run 4 h/wk and cycle 2 h/wk (6 h total), the 20% M/H budget = 72 min — **and concentrate all 72 min in the runs**. Cross-training should be 100% low-intensity. Why? Because:
1. Skill specificity — your hard work should be in the sport you're racing.
2. Recovery — hard cross-training competes with hard running for recovery capacity.
3. Adaptation specificity — the molecular adaptations that matter for running come from running, harder.

**Exception:** if you're prone to Achilles / hamstring / calf strains (faster-running injuries), invert this — do the hard work on the bike or elliptical instead.

## Application

If the athlete does no formal cross-training, adding **1 ElliptiGO or stationary-bike session/wk** (minimalist approach) from the progressive-loading phase onwards is a low-impact volume booster — especially once running volume is high for them or a known niggle (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles) starts whispering. It also works as a mid-week "easy day" alternative when legs are flat.
