# Train the motion, not the muscle

Per McGill UBF Ch 14, p. 276 (paraphrased): don't default to traditional, generic "strength" programmes — make training specific to the sport or activity; treat everything as guidelines rather than rules; and spend limited training time on exercises that build several qualities at once (balance, stability, strength and power). McGill's summary line:

> "**Train the motion — not the muscle.**" — McGill, *Ultimate Back Fitness and Performance*, Ch 14, p. 276

The single sentence that summarises UBF's rejection of bodybuilding-derived training for athletes. The training unit is the **motion / movement pattern**, NOT the muscle.

## What this rejects

Bodybuilding-derived logic:
- "Today is back day" → isolate lats, traps, rhomboids in different exercises.
- "Bicep curls at the wall to lock the elbow" — isolate biceps, eliminate brachioradialis.
- "Hamstring curls" — isolate hamstrings without integration.
- "Tricep kickbacks" — isolate triceps.
- High-volume, high-frequency, high-isolation, aiming at hypertrophy.

This logic is FINE if your goal is aesthetic hypertrophy. It's WRONG if your goal is athletic performance.

## What this prescribes

Train the **fundamental movement patterns** that athletic tasks decompose into:
1. **Push** (horizontal: push-up, bench, standing cable press; vertical: overhead press)
2. **Pull** (horizontal: row; vertical: pull-up, lat pulldown)
3. **Squat** (bilateral, single-leg variations)
4. **Hinge** (deadlift, RDL, trap-bar, single-leg deadlift, kettlebell swing)
5. **Lunge** (forward, reverse, lateral, walking)
6. **Carry** (suitcase, farmer, waiter, bottoms-up, overhead)
7. **Twist with locked spine** (cable woodchop, Pallof press — motion at extremities, spine neutral)
8. **Locomote** (run, sprint, crawl, jump, land, change direction)

Every athletic task can be decomposed into combinations of these. Training the patterns builds athletic capacity. Training the muscles (isolation) does not transfer.

## Why integration > isolation for athletic transfer

- **Athletic tasks require force transmission through linkages.** Isolation training doesn't train the linkage; it trains a single link.
- **Muscle "strength" measured in isolation does not predict performance.** The bench press doesn't predict standing pushing power. The bicep curl doesn't predict pulling power.
- **Stability + balance + co-contraction patterns matter as much as strength.** Isolation work doesn't develop these; integrated motion does.
- **The brain learns motions, not muscles.** Motor patterns are skill. Isolation work doesn't groove sport-specific patterns.

## UBF's case example (Ch 14)

The football lineman who debates "perfect bicep curl" at the wall — UBF Ch 14 opens with this. The lineman benefits from size + strength, sure, but only if directed through the linkage to the ground and across the body to the opponent. The motion actually performed is: lunge + simultaneous elbow + shoulder flexion + rip across the chest with a hammer-grip forearm. **Training that motion** (with a dumbbell, hammer grip, in a lunge) — directly transfers. **Training the bicep curl at the wall** — doesn't.

## Application: what this changes

A runner's strength programme built from this vault is mostly motion-based already (squat, hinge, carry, lunge are the primary lifts). UBF reinforces:

1. **Don't add isolation work unless there's a specific reason** (e.g. addressing a structural-balance gap per structural-balance-shoulder-is-lynchpin, or correcting a one-sided asymmetry such as a tight, weaker hamstring).
2. **Watch for the integration cues** — every loaded lift should engage the full chain. The "spread the floor + bend the bar" cues during squats are exactly this.
3. **Carries are non-negotiable** — they uniquely train integration under load.
4. **Glute amnesia screen** — the most common integration failure (the body uses hamstring + erector spinae for hip extension instead of glutes, because that motor pattern was grooved by isolated work + back-pain compensation). Per [glute-amnesia](glute-amnesia.md), this is a NEURAL fix, not a strength fix.
5. **Plyometric + power work uses bird-dog → modified explosive bird-dog → hops** progressions (UBF Ch 14) — integrated motion patterns, not "explosive bicep" drills.

## Specific exercise audit

What stays (motion-based):
- Trap-bar deadlift (hinge)
- Single-leg deadlift (hinge + balance)
- Goblet squat, front squat (squat)
- Walking lunge, reverse lunge (lunge)
- Push-up, bench, overhead press (push)
- Pull-up, row (pull)
- All carries (carry + integration)
- Kettlebell swing (hinge + ballistic)
- Hip thrust (hinge + glute)

What to question:
- Standalone bicep curl (isolation — only justified if upper-arm asymmetry needs correction)
- Standalone tricep extension (isolation — same)
- Calf raises (isolation — but legitimate if calf-specific strength is the goal, which it is for most marathoners; see [calf-periodised-rep-ranges](calf-periodised-rep-ranges.md))
- Lateral raises (isolation — only justified for posture-fix routine or shoulder pre-hab)

The bodybuilding-style isolation work has a place if it serves a specific structural-balance or aesthetic goal. It shouldn't BE the program.
