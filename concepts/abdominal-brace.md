# Abdominal brace (tune like a dimmer switch)

Per McGill Ch 7, the brace is the muscular tool that creates the stiffness around the spine that prevents the painful micro-movements that sensitise tissue. Most people are taught it wrong.

## What bracing IS

Mild, even contraction of the entire abdominal wall — obliques, transverse abdominis, rectus abdominis — **as though preparing to be punched in the stomach** but mildly. Both the front (six-pack) AND the sides (obliques) activate together, forming the "hoops" that act as guy-wires around the spine.

Self-test: with the abdominals relaxed, push fingers ~5–12 cm lateral to the navel into the oblique muscles. Now activate the brace. The fingers should be pushed gently OUT — NOT sucked in.

## What bracing IS NOT

- **NOT "suck in the navel"** (the Pilates / "draw the navel to spine" cue). This deactivates the transverse abdominis as a load-bearer and creates a vacuum that LESS-stabilises the spine.
- **NOT max contraction.** Over-bracing crushes the spine with its own muscle force. (One of McGill's case files — Octavio the opera singer — over-braced to the point of esophageal sphincter dysfunction and nighttime acid reflux.)
- **NOT held breath.** Breathe normally throughout. Exhale on the hardest part of any exertion.

## Tune like a dimmer switch

Match the brace level to the task — NOT an on/off switch.

| Task | Brace level |
|---|---|
| Walking, standing tall | 5–10% (just enough to keep neutral) |
| Rising from chair, picking up groceries | 20–30% |
| Lifting a child, carrying laundry up stairs | 40–60% |
| Heavy hinge, loaded squat, sneezing while flexion-intolerant | 70–90% |
| Max-effort lift | 100% (rare — most training is sub-max) |

The athlete's job: develop the awareness to know what level a task needs, and produce exactly that, NO more.

## UBF's ~25% MVC quantification for elastic-spring use

UBF Ch 15 adds a specific number: for athletic tasks that use the abdominal wall as an **elastic spring** (running, throwing, kicking, golf swing), the optimal PRE-CONTRACTION level is ~25% MVC. Below 25% = mush, no elastic storage. Above 25% = stuck, no movement, no spring. Right at 25% = the abdominal wall stores energy on the eccentric/loading phase and returns it on the concentric/throwing phase.

For most daily-living tasks (UBF Ch 5): 5-10% MVC is sufficient. Standing with 30 kg in each hand = ~5% MVC. So the dimmer-switch tuning is sub-25% for most life + walking; 25% specifically for elastic-spring athletic motions; higher for max-effort lifts.

## The plywood / binding effect (UBF Ch 5, Ch 15)

UBF's mechanism for why the full brace is so much stronger than partial activation: the three layers of the abdominal wall (transverse abdominis, internal oblique, external oblique) + rectus abdominis, when activated TOGETHER, **bind via their fascial planes**. Total stiffness EXCEEDS the sum of individual stiffnesses — like plywood vs four loose planks (Brown + McGill 2009 measured this in rat preps). This is why **abdominal hollowing (TVA-only) DEFEATS** the bracing effect. The full brace creates the composite; hollowing prevents it.

UBF (Ch 5): "**Athletes focusing on single muscles are creating dysfunction in spines!**" UBF position is more emphatic than BM's: hollowing isn't just suboptimal — it's actively harmful.

## The handshake demo (UBF Ch 10, p. 175)

Quick proof that the brace + total-body stiffness redirects strength: shake a partner's hand, squeeze max. Then: wide foot stance, "grip the ground" with toes + heels, isometrically abduct + externally rotate hips ("spread the floor"), squeeze glutes ("squeeze a coin"), abdominal brace, lift ribcage, depress shoulders with lats, make a fist with the OTHER hand and squeeze. Now repeat the handshake. **Often 20-30% stronger.** The brace + stiffness redirects neuronal overflow from remote joints to the working hand.

## Pec/lat brace as alternative

For tasks where pure abdominal bracing increases pain (some load-intolerant patients), McGill's alternative: brace the latissimus + pectoralis to depress the shoulders down (anti-shrug). Useful in the loaded lifts paired with hip-hinge (the "twist the bar" + anti-shrug cue when picking up a bar — see [hip-hinge-mechanics](hip-hinge-mechanics.md)).

## When to use which

- **Sagittal-plane lifts (deadlift, RDL, push-up, hinge):** abdominal brace.
- **Frontal-plane carries (suitcase, farmer):** abdominal brace + anti-lateral-flexion firing of the side opposite the load.
- **Pull/push from the cable column:** abdominal brace + pec/lat depression (anti-shrug).
- **Walking under load (heavy backpack, carries):** mild abdominal brace, sustained.

## Cross-source check

Dicharry's "360° brace, exhale on the drive, no held breath" cue is identical at the technique level. McGill adds the **calibration vocabulary** (dimmer switch, tune-to-task) and the explicit warning against the suck-in-the-navel orthodoxy.

## Pavel's "breathing behind the shield" overlay

Pavel (S&S, p. 96) adds a third complementary mode for **sustained-tension contexts** (the get-up, loaded carries, holds): "breathing behind the shield." Imagine someone is standing on your stomach. You brace 70–80% to keep from being crushed, then breathe SHALLOWLY behind that braced shield. Deep diaphragmatic breaths would collapse the brace; held breath would deny oxygen. The shield model is the dimmer-switch model at a sustained mid-high setting + a specific breath strategy that preserves the brace under sustained load. Use it for:
- The Turkish get-up (entire 30+ seconds)
- Loaded carries
- Any isometric hold

Pavel's complementary mode for **explosive contexts** is "power breathing" — forceful exhale through the teeth on the working phase (KIAI), sharp nasal inhale on the return. Used for swings + ballistic lifts. The brace cycles with the breath, but stays high throughout. See [hardstyle-tension-relaxation](hardstyle-tension-relaxation.md).
