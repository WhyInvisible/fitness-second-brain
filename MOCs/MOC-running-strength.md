# Running strength training

> The single highest-leverage fix in a marathon plan. Lower-body / posterior chain / core strength is what stops late-race marathon cramping, improves running economy, and keeps joints durable at peak mileage.
>
> Phased over the season — see the [season plan](../plans/season-plan.md) for week-by-week placement. This file is the *menu* and the *progression*; week-specific loads live in [current-week.md](../plans/current-week.md).

## Why this exists

A common marathon failure pattern: on goal pace through 30+ km, then cramping and a big positive split, even with fuel and electrolytes dialled in. A plausible cause is an undertrained lower body, made worse by carrying extra body mass. Strong, conditioned legs:

- Don't cramp under marathon-duration eccentric load
- Stay efficient longer (same pace, lower HR)
- Resist late-race form breakdown
- Reduce injury risk during peak mileage weeks
- Make calisthenics work like pistol squats actually possible

For a runner, **strength means force production with minimal added mass.** Heavy + low-rep beats high-rep + light. Compound movements beat isolations. Bodybuilding-style hypertrophy work actively works *against* a runner.

**Note: Pfitzinger's framework differs from this menu.** Pfitzinger prescribes 2× weekly light-weight, 15-rep circuits (8-exercise full-body) — also targeting "force without mass" but via lighter loads + higher reps. This menu uses **heavier compounds**, which suit a runner who is undertrained in the lower body and needs absolute strength gains (the late-race-cramp fix). Use Pfitz's circuit during bodyweight-only blocks (travel, no gym) and as the maintenance option in the marathon-specific phase.

## Dicharry's load targets (long-term north stars)

Per Dicharry (Ch 9), runner-strength benchmarks worth working toward across the season — these ground the menu and tell you when "strong enough" is reached:

| Lift | Rep target | BW multiple | Example: hypothetical 70 kg runner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Romanian deadlift | 8 reps | 1.5–1.8× BW | 105–125 kg |
| Back / front squat | 6 reps | 1.3–1.5× BW | 90–105 kg |
| Hip thrust | 6 reps | 2× BW | 140 kg |

These are *long-term* targets, not entry points. Hitting them is a multi-season project. Use them to calibrate "are we strong enough yet" — a runner with only 1× BW RDL has obvious headroom; one at 1.5× BW is bumping against diminishing returns and should shift energy back into running.

## Bar speed rule (Dicharry)

> "If your form doesn't break down and your lift speed doesn't decrease more than 50 percent within the set, add weight." — Dicharry, *Running Rewired*

- Lifting/lowering tempo: smooth, ~2.5 s each direction.
- **The set is over when bar speed drops > 50%.** Never grind. Never to failure.
- Holds: brace 360° around the spine, exhale on the drive. **No held breath.**
- This applies to RDL, squat, hip thrust, every loaded lift.

## Hard rules — all phases

- **No conventional barbell deadlift until the progressive-loading phase at the earliest**, and only if the back is quiet. Trap-bar deadlift is the substitute (and the season-long default for anyone with a lower-back history — see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles).
- **Leg session never the day before a long run.**
- **Lower back complains → stop the session**, do yoga / mobility instead. Never push through.
- **Reps in reserve, not to failure.** Always leave 2+ reps in the tank during marathon training.
- Pre-warm with [pre-leg-day warm-up](MOC-warmups-cooldowns.md#pre-leg-day-warm-up-3-4-min). Glute activation matters.
- Form first, always. Video hinges from the side periodically. Neutral spine non-negotiable.

## Year-round anchor — Dicharry precision workouts (2× per week)

Per Dicharry, the missing link between "just running" and "barbell strength" is **precision work — 15–20 min sessions, mostly bodyweight, done 2× per week year-round.** Dynamic warm-ups in disguise — wire motor patterns, cue glute / foot / T-spine engagement, add up to real adaptation over months. Different from the strength sessions below; they don't replace them.

Rotate one of these per session:

1. **Core Circuit** — donkey toes, pigeon hip extension, frog bridge, tippy twist, bear walk, lateral hurdle hop, burpee. No equipment.
2. **Hip Circuit** — twisted warrior, butt scoots, glute rainbow, standing hip circles, pigeon hip extension, tippy twist, frog bridge. No equipment.
3. **Band Circuit** — long-arm band squat, banded hip twist, bear walk, band drive-thru, thread-the-needle plank, foot screws, banded arm circles, band pull-aparts. Needs bands.

Best time: before an easy run (works as dynamic warm-up). Skip on hard run days. Drop to 1×/wk in recovery weeks; bump to 3× if running is light.

## Phase 1 — Rehab / re-entry (~4 weeks)

**Goal:** restore motor pattern, rebuild tendon capacity, no soreness that compromises the rest of the week. DB-only. No barbell hinge.

**Frequency:** 1× per week.

**Mandatory prerequisite** (do before every Phase 1 leg session, ~5 min — Dicharry form-master drills):
- Chair-of-death squat: 2 × 10 unloaded (dowel against head/back/tailbone, chair behind to limit forward knee drift)
- Single-leg deadlift with dowel: 2 × 10 per side
- Don't load the squat or RDL until both are clean.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Load progression, weeks 1 → 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Goblet squat (DB) | 3 × 10 | Start light enough for clean reps; one DB step up per week for 3 weeks; drop back a step in week 4 (cutback) |
| Romanian deadlift (DBs) | 3 × 10 | Same pattern: ~2 kg per hand per week, drop back in week 4 |
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 6 per leg | BW → BW → light DBs → BW |
| Single-leg calf raise | 3 × 12 per leg | BW (slow eccentric) |
| Glute bridge | 3 × 12 | BW → light plate on hips → heavier plate → light plate (cutback) |
| Hanging knee raise | 3 × 8 | BW |

**Cooldown:** 5 min hamstring + hip flexor stretch (extra time on the tighter side, if one is noticeably tighter).

## Phase 2 — Light barbell (~3 weeks)

**Goal:** introduce barbell movements including light barbell hinge. Mini-benchmark at the end of the phase.

**Frequency:** 1× per week.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Loading |
|---|---|---|
| Front squat (or heavier goblet) | 4 × 6 | Start at a load that moves fast on every rep; +2.5 kg / wk if clean |
| Barbell RDL | 3 × 8 | Start conservatively; add 5–10 kg per week while bar speed holds |
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 8 per leg | Light DBs, +2.5 kg per hand per week |
| Trap-bar deadlift (if available) | 3 × 6 | Start light, build cautiously |
| Standing calf raise | 3 × 12 | Machine or light DBs |
| Side plank with reach | 2 × 8 per side | BW |

## Phase 3 — Bodyweight-only maintenance (optional: travel / no-gym block)

**Goal:** maintain. Bodyweight only. No PRs. Don't lose ground.

**Frequency:** 2× per week, ~20 min each.

**Session A — bodyweight legs:**
- Bodyweight squat: 3 × 20
- Bulgarian split squat: 3 × 8 per leg
- Single-leg glute bridge: 3 × 12 per leg
- Single-leg calf raise: 3 × 15 per leg
- Walking lunge: 3 × 12 per leg

**Session B — single-leg focus:**
- Step-up (chair / bench): 3 × 10 per leg
- Pistol squat progressions (box assist → counterweight → full): 3 × 5 per leg
- Wall sit: 3 × 30–60 s
- Glute bridge march: 3 × 10 per side
- Sumo squat hold: 3 × 30 s

**Optional: Pfitzinger's runner circuit** (alternative or supplement for a third weekly session) — 8-exercise full-body circuit, 2 rounds: push-up × 10, step-up × 15/leg, DB lateral row × 15/side (sub bodyweight row), calf raise × 10, bench dip × 15, lunge × 15/leg, triceps press × 15, bodyweight squat × 15. ~30 min total. Works with bodyweight only, so it fits a no-gym constraint.

## Phase 4 — Progressive loading (~6 weeks)

**Goal:** build past previous levels. The real strength gains happen here.

**Frequency:** 1× heavy + 1× supplementary per week.

**Heavy session:**

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Loading |
|---|---|---|
| Back squat (or front squat) | 4 × 5 | Progressive. Cap well below old PRs. |
| Trap-bar deadlift | 4 × 5 | Resume where Phase 2 ended, add 2.5 kg / wk |
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 6 per leg | Moderate–heavy DBs |
| Standing calf raise | 4 × 10 | Heavy |
| Pallof press | 3 × 10 per side | Cable / band |

**Supplementary session (other day, lighter):**
- Single-leg deadlift (DBs): 3 × 8 per leg
- Step-up with DBs: 3 × 8 per leg
- Hip thrust: 3 × 10
- Hamstring curl (machine or Nordic): 3 × 8
- Hanging leg raise: 3 × 8

## Phase 5 — Marathon-specific maintenance (~8 weeks)

**Goal:** maintain. Hold strength. Never to failure. Every drop of CNS goes to running.

**Frequency:** 1× per week, ~30 min.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Loading |
|---|---|---|
| Back squat or front squat | 3 × 5 | ~80% of recent best, leave 3 reps in tank |
| Trap-bar deadlift | 3 × 5 | Same approach |
| Single-leg work (split squat / lunge / step-up — rotate) | 2 × 8 per leg | Moderate |
| Calf raise | 3 × 10 | Moderate |
| Core | as per [MOC-core-training](MOC-core-training.md) | — |

**No PRs in this phase. No burnout sets.** Strength is in the bank.

## Phase 6 — Taper & race (final ~4 weeks)

- **4 and 3 weeks out:** 1× very light session (BW squat 2×10, single-leg glute bridge 2×8 per leg, calf raise 2×10, hip mobility).
- **2 weeks out:** optional very light. Probably skip.
- **Race week:** none.

## Pfitzinger running-form drill circuit (separate from strength)

Distinct from the strength sessions above — these are **neuromuscular drills**, not strength work. Done **after warm-up but before** strides or a hard session, 1–2×/week.

**The 4-drill circuit** (each rep is 15–20 m forward, walk back, then next drill; complete the sequence twice):

1. Skip march walk — exaggerated slow march, high knee + full toe-off, tall posture.
2. Skip march run — same form, exaggerated skipping motion.
3. Kickout — knee up → snap to nearly full extension. Walking then jogging version.
4. Fast feet — short rapid steps on the balls of the feet.

**Why these matter:** per Pfitzinger, drills "exaggerate aspects of the running stride and pay dividends late in your marathon" by preventing the late-race shuffle — shortened stride from fatigue and tightness.

**Pair with short hill sprints** for the highest-return form + economy session: 15 min warm-up → 4-drill circuit ×2 → 6–8 × short hill sprint (10–12 s) → 8 × 100 m strides → cool-down.

## Power block — 6 weeks pre-race (Dicharry RFD)

Dicharry: "In the 6 weeks prior to key races, incorporate a weekly power workout." Rate-of-force-development training has the rare property of building speed *without* requiring much recovery — fits cleanly into taper.

- **Block 1:** 6 weeks before a tune-up race (e.g. a half marathon mid-build) — 1× weekly power workout, alongside reduced strength volume.
- **Block 2:** 6 weeks before the A-race — same, dropping volume into race week.

Use medball acceleration sprints, medball push-press, medball triple bound, box squat jumps, ninja squat jumps, split box jumps, dumbbell push-press, flip-flop sprints, incline sprints, stair bounds. A medball-only Powerball workout (~45 min, in the 6 weeks pre-race) is the safest entry — low equipment, lower joint stress.

**Plyo rules (Dicharry):** mid-shin to knee-height boxes only. Land full foot. No double-bouncing — if you double-bounce, rest. **Tall box jumps don't help runners.**

## Lower-back history — when it's the limiting factor

If the athlete has a lower-back injury history (see [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles — e.g. an old heavy-deadlift injury), it shapes the whole approach:

- **Trap-bar > conventional barbell deadlift** for the whole season. Less shear on the lower back. Reintroduce the conventional bar after the A-race *if* everything's good, and only if wanted.
- **Linear progression, not jumps.** Add 2.5 kg per week max. Plateau? Stay another week before adding.
- **Warning signs:** dull morning low-back ache, asymmetric tightness during a hinge, pain (not just stretch) during forward fold. Any of these = drop weight 20% and reassess.
- **Hamstring asymmetry** — if one hamstring is much tighter than the other (often linked to an old injury), favour single-leg work to address the imbalance.

## Decision rules

- Loads stop progressing for **2 weeks in a row** in a given phase → deload one week, then move to the next phase (don't grind).
- Two failed sessions in a row (high RPE, form breaking) → cut volume 30%, reassess sleep / fuel / running fatigue.
- New niggle anywhere → drop the relevant exercise *that day*, substitute, reassess next session.
- RPE target by the end of any session: ~7/10. Not 10/10. Marathon training already takes the 10/10 budget.
- **The [Matthews double-progression-loading-scheme](../concepts/double-progression-loading-scheme.md) applies to any heavy compound here.** Beat last week by 1 rep; top of range hit → +2.5 kg next session. Stalled 2 weeks → micro-load +1.25 kg or hold.

## Calf training programme (Matthews periodised)

Per [calf-periodised-rep-ranges](../concepts/calf-periodised-rep-ranges.md). The most direct fix for late-race calf cramping (under-trained calves under marathon-duration eccentric load).

- **Phase 1–2:** 1–2×/wk single-leg calf raise (BW, 3 × 15 per leg, slow eccentric)
- **Phase 4 progressive:** add 1× loaded calf session/week (Workout A heavy: standing + seated 3 × 4–6 each)
- **Phase 5 maintenance:** 1× per week (Workout B moderate, 3 × 8–10)
- **Phase 6 taper:** none

Post-A-race / off-season block: full 3× per week A/B/C rotation per Matthews — see [MOC-lower-body.md](MOC-lower-body.md) "Calf training" section.

## Pavel S&S as alternative / supplement

For weeks where back is sensitive, life is too busy for the full session, or you want a minimalist back-friendly hinge + full-body option:

- 25-min session: 10×10 one-arm KB swings + 5/5 Turkish get-ups + warm-up + cool-down
- 2–3×/wk (Pavel's explicit allowance for athletes with heavy other training)
- Replaces (not supplements) one Phase 1 strength session
- Step-loading progression: 16 kg → 24 kg → 32 kg over ~5–6 months
- Back-friendly: 100% glute MVC + 150% lat MVC measured in McGill's Waterloo lab. Hip-drive-only, locked spine, anti-shrug throughout.
- See kettlebell-swing, turkish-get-up, references/pavel-simple-sinister.md, and [anti-glycolytic-training](../concepts/anti-glycolytic-training.md) for why this doesn't compete with marathon recovery.
