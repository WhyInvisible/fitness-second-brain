# MOC — Injuries + niggles management

> A map of the injury-management and prehab concepts in this vault, grouped by the body issue they address. This page is generic: **the athlete's own injuries live in the table in [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles, which the weekly review checks every week.** For each row in that table, find the matching section below and apply its approach.

## How to use this map

1. Read the athlete's rows in [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles (status, history, what aggravates it, what helps).
2. Find the matching issue below. Apply its approach, scaled to the row's status (active / improving / watch / resolved).
3. If a niggle isn't covered here, use the hard rules and the decision tree at the bottom, and flag that a new concept note may be needed.

## Lower back (pain now, or a past back injury)

**Key idea:** a past back injury is managed for years, not weeks. Per McGill ([back-tissue-heals-over-years](../concepts/back-tissue-heals-over-years.md)), disc and endplate tissue can take up to ~10 years to fully consolidate, so for an athlete with back history this is a permanent practice, not a rehab phase — even when they're currently pain-free.

**Approach (permanent, not just rehab):**
- **Pre-leg-day glute reactivation** ([glute-amnesia](../concepts/glute-amnesia.md)) — clam-shell + glute-coin-squeeze + glute-only-bridge with palpation. 5 min, before any session involving deadlift, hip thrust, squat, or RDL. Chronic one-sided hamstring tightness in a back-injured athlete often fits the crossed-pelvis pattern; the fix is glute reactivation, not more hamstring stretching.
- **Daily McGill Big 3** ([mcgill-big-3](../concepts/mcgill-big-3.md)) via [russian-descending-pyramid](../concepts/russian-descending-pyramid.md) — 6/4/2 × 8 s holds. Opened with cat-camel 7–8 cycles.
- **24/7 spine hygiene** ([spine-sparing-daily-life](../concepts/spine-sparing-daily-life.md)) — sitting, sleeping, bending, sneezing, picking up groceries.
- **Hip-hinge mechanics** ([hip-hinge-mechanics](../concepts/hip-hinge-mechanics.md)) as the default for every bend.
- **Power at hips, never at spine** ([power-at-hips-not-spine](../concepts/power-at-hips-not-spine.md)) — with back history, trap-bar over conventional barbell deadlift, long-term.
- **Endurance before strength** ([endurance-before-strength](../concepts/endurance-before-strength.md)) — Big 3 isometric capacity first, loaded patterns after.
- **Contraindicated exercises** ([back-safe-exercise-contraindications](../concepts/back-safe-exercise-contraindications.md)) — no sit-ups, Russian twists, supermans, knees-to-chest, Pilates roll-up, deep barbell squats, KB swings until form is bulletproof.
- **Stiffness, not flexibility, for the spine** ([spine-stiffness-not-flexibility](../concepts/spine-stiffness-not-flexibility.md)) — spine = stiffness target; hips/shoulders = mobility target.
- **Anti-rotation done safely** ([torsional-buttressing](../concepts/torsional-buttressing.md)) — twist at the hips and shoulders, never the spine.
- **If a niggle returns:** run the [virtual-surgery-relapse-protocol](../concepts/virtual-surgery-relapse-protocol.md) immediately. Self-diagnose via [mcgill-pain-trigger-diagnostic](../concepts/mcgill-pain-trigger-diagnostic.md).
- **Walking as therapy** ([walking-as-back-therapy](../concepts/walking-as-back-therapy.md)) — brisk pace + arm swing converts daily walks into back rehab.
- **Returning to heavier lifting** — [return-to-sport-progression-after-back-injury](../concepts/return-to-sport-progression-after-back-injury.md).

**Anchored by:**
- [mcgill-big-3](../concepts/mcgill-big-3.md), [russian-descending-pyramid](../concepts/russian-descending-pyramid.md), [abdominal-brace](../concepts/abdominal-brace.md), [core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion](../concepts/core-stops-motion-limbs-create-motion.md)
- [bar-speed-rule](../concepts/bar-speed-rule.md) — never grind, never to failure
- [body-drives-form](../concepts/body-drives-form.md) — assess before loading

## Hamstring tightness or a left/right asymmetry

**Key idea:** a one-sided tight hamstring is often a symptom (a compensating hamstring, an under-firing glute, or a hip/shin alignment issue) rather than the problem itself. Screen before stretching.

**Approach:**
- Screen for glute amnesia first ([glute-amnesia](../concepts/glute-amnesia.md)), especially with back history.
- Asymmetric mobility focus — extra hamstring/hip work on the tighter side.
- Single-leg strength priority over bilateral (addresses the imbalance).
- Watch it during running and after long runs.

**Anchored by:**
- [individual-alignment-assessment](../concepts/individual-alignment-assessment.md) — Craig's test recommended
- [flexibility-vs-mobility-three-stiffness-causes](../concepts/flexibility-vs-mobility-three-stiffness-causes.md) — 3-min stretches 4–5×/wk if tissue length is the issue
- hamstring-stretch, hip-flexor-stretch
- single-leg-deadlift, single-leg-squat, step-up

## Shoulder twinges (e.g. at the start of upper-body sessions)

**Approach (escalation ladder):**
- Mandatory 5-min shoulder warm-up before every upper-body session.
- 2-week trial of the standard warm-up before escalating.
- If it persists: escalate to Dicharry's Posture Fix routine for 2 weeks.
- If it still persists: see a physio.
- Long term: keep push and pull volume roughly 1:1.

**Anchored by:**
- [shoulder-warmup](../concepts/shoulder-warmup.md) — the 5-min routine
- [posture-fix-routine](../concepts/posture-fix-routine.md) — 10-min escalation if needed
- [pair-pushing-and-pulling](../concepts/pair-pushing-and-pulling.md) — long-term shoulder health
- [protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control](../concepts/protraction-retraction-elevation-depression-scapular-control.md) — scapular positions for each skill
- band-pull-aparts, banded-arm-circles, basketball-mobility, travolta-peanuts, pec-minor-stretch, overhead-carry

## Calf cramping or late-race leg failure

**Key idea:** cramping late in a marathon (often in the last third) is mostly neuromuscular fatigue from an under-prepared lower body, not just a fuelling or electrolyte problem. If a past race went this way, record it in profile.md (Training history and Injuries and niggles) so the plan can target it.

**Approach:**
- Build leg strength through the whole season (Phase 1 → 6 of running-strength).
- Calf-specific work in the pre-leg-day warm-up.
- Watch the affected calf during long runs as mileage builds.
- Rehearse race pace late in long runs ([mp-runs-inside-long-run](../concepts/mp-runs-inside-long-run.md)) and build fatigue tolerance ([brain-trains-fatigue-tolerance](../concepts/brain-trains-fatigue-tolerance.md)).

**Anchored by:**
- [cramping-is-neuromuscular-fatigue](../concepts/cramping-is-neuromuscular-fatigue.md)
- [MOC-running-strength](MOC-running-strength.md) — the whole structure exists to prevent this
- [runner-strength-loading-targets](../concepts/runner-strength-loading-targets.md) — RDL 1.5–1.8× BW target
- [carries-train-anti-lateral-flexion](../concepts/carries-train-anti-lateral-flexion.md) — late-race trunk wobble
- calf-smash, burrito-calf-stretch, foot-screws

## Elbows and grip (when adding pull or grip volume)

- Medial-elbow (golfer's elbow) irritation is the usual failure when grip work is added on top of pull volume. Add grip work gradually and do the elbow cooldown every pull day — see [MOC-climbing-grip](MOC-climbing-grip.md) and [MOC-warmups-cooldowns](MOC-warmups-cooldowns.md).
- Tendons adapt over months: [connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months](../concepts/connective-tissue-adaptation-3-12-months.md).

## Hard rules across all issues

- **Stop if pain (not stretch) appears.** Substitute mobility/yoga.
- **Form first.** Video periodically.
- **Linear progression.** No jumps in load or volume ([progressive-overload](../concepts/progressive-overload.md)).
- **Reps in reserve.** Never to failure during a marathon build.
- **Listen to morning signals** — see [listen-to-morning-signals](../concepts/listen-to-morning-signals.md).

## Decision tree for niggles

1. Sharp pain during warmup → stop, substitute yoga/mobility for the day
2. Dull ache morning of training → train at 70%, abandon if it gets worse
3. Persistent niggle 3+ days → drop the offending exercise for a week, sub equivalent
4. Pain that limits movement → physio before next session

When a niggle appears, starts, or resolves, update its row in [profile.md](../profile.md) → Injuries and niggles so the weekly review sees it.
